WHY
  P-110 (LEDGER): "Share W_q/W_k across 2-4 layers or tie K=V, and reinvest in depth"; prior art MASA (100-700M),
  CLA (1B/3B), and QKV-variants arXiv 2606.04032 (K=V costs 3.1% perplexity); win: "reinvested depth beats the
  baseline at equal total". At 5M every parameter is scarce (the embedding is 31% of the total), and the harness
  already has the switch (config.py qk_share; blocks.py reuses the owner's modules, so parameters() counts them
  once). This is the cheapest capacity-allocation bet with no code to land, and bits per byte is its natural
  metric. K = V is not tested: its one measured cost (3.1%) points the other way.

QUESTION (decided, C7)
  At equal total parameters, does sharing W_q/W_k between consecutive layer pairs, with the saving spent on a ninth
  layer, change held-out chat bits per byte at 5M, 250M slots?

THE CHANGE (one idea, three keys, set by the budget solver)
  model.qk_share: 2, model.n_layers: 9, model.mlp_hidden: 464 (d 192, 3 heads and head_dim 64 unchanged).
  budget.py --target 5e6 --qk-share 2 --fixed-d 192 (2026-09-26) returned two shapes: 8 layers with SwiGLU 552
  (the saving spent on MLP width, its first row) and 9 layers with SwiGLU 464 (its second row). P-110 says depth, so
  the 9-layer row is the arm. Total 5,020,440 vs BASE 5,010,133 (+0.21%; count_analytic), inside the 2% rule.
  W_q/W_k owners by unique layer: 0 0 2 2 4 4 6 6 8 (config.qk_owner), so the ninth layer has its own. The MLP
  width move (488 to 464) is the solver's equal-total fit, part of the one idea. The config generator's C2 check
  allows exactly these three keys for this arm. Depth also changes what depends on it: o_proj/down_proj init std
  (1/sqrt(2 x depth), model.py) and the 1/sqrt(depth index) norm scaling of layer 9; both are part of "one more
  layer".
