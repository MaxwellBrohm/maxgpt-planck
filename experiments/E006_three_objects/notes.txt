G  GENERAL-CHAT REPLAY. E005's recipe with one change: one replay micro-batch is ADDED to every optimizer step
   (code/e006_train.py g_train). Every step is [u, u, u, u, r]: C's four update micro-batches of 4 kept examples,
   in C's order and each scaled by 1/4 exactly as in C, then one micro-batch of 4 replay threads, also scaled 1/4;
   then clip 1.0 and one AdamW step. So G trains on exactly C's 6,400 update examples (after C's 4-example check
   batch), byte for byte, plus 1,600 replay threads; replay is 1/5 of the micro-batches and of the pre-clip loss
   weight. Block shares inside the update part are C's own; as shares of all trained examples each E005 block is
   scaled by 0.8 (E004 block .56, ALIAS .16, IND .08, replay .20).
   Why additive (critique M2): a replacement design trains on 25% fewer update examples, so a lower template share,
   fewer loops or a smaller knowledge loss could come from the smaller update dose alone. Additive G changes one
   thing: replay is present. What cannot be separated from it, and is stated: clipping is active on every step
   (E005's logged norms, every 10th step of 5 seeds: 1.75-12.96, never under the clip), so replay also takes a
   share of the clipped norm; the loop logs the update part's gradient norm before the replay backward and the
   clipped total, per step.
   Why 1 in 5, in whole micro-batches: the loss is a token mean per micro-batch; an update micro-batch has about 30
   labelled tokens (E005's self-check batches: 27-32) and a replay micro-batch about 1,260 (pool median 305 per
   thread). A replay thread inside an update micro-batch would take over 90% of that micro-batch's gradient and the
   update share would depend on thread lengths. A whole micro-batch gives replay a fixed 1/5 of the loss weight.

PRE-REGISTERED RULES AND READINGS (code/rules_e006.py; analyzer analyze_e006*.py, passrule_e006.py)
Common. LIK right = the gold's summed log-prob strictly above every other in-context candidate (metrics_ft.right on
the raw scores); GEN right = strict (gen_grade.py, clauses 1-7), plain render unless named. Paired bootstrap for an
arm difference X - Y on one family: 10,000 resamples with numpy.random.default_rng(6006); each resample draws k seed
numbers with replacement from the k seeds both have and n items with replacement from the family's n, and averages
right_X(s, i) - right_Y(s, i) over the drawn (s, i) pairs; X and Y are paired by seed number and item. 95% CI = the
2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. References (e004w, e005w, E005's Mac records) pair the same way by seed number. A
missing or incomplete seed of an arm makes every pooled reading that uses that arm NOT READ (no imputation).

families x LIK/GEN x plain/chat), AL LIK (4 cells x 2 renders), the continuity sets new/extra/old/uprobe/cross and
knowledge kbig and khard (plain LIK; K_closedbook60 sits inside "old"), and the chat-probe TF, TF2, LOOP and CHECKS;

KNOWN RISKS
  1 Device. MPS -> CUDA changes numerics (kernels, reduction order, attention). C measures it; Q-H5 and Q-chat
    compare arms on one device. E004/E005 levels on CUDA come from rescoring their Mac-trained weights.
  2 CUDA may not be bitwise reproducible even with these settings (warn_only); the dry runs report it and the
    identity check then falls back to "no more than two runs of one entry point differ".
  3 P changes more than GL2 and GLX2 (side effects under ARMS P); GL2 lands .12 below E004's.
  4 One candidate cause is tested. NOT THE CAUSE leaves the 70% E004 block, the IND block and the chat render open.
  5 G's replay takes a share of a gradient norm that is clipped on every step, so "replay present" includes a
    smaller effective step for the update part; the logged update-part norm shows how much.
  6 G's replay holds values, digits, sport words, small talk, creative requests and question frames, and facts
    kbig asks about (hence the unexposed subset; exposure by string matching undercounts paraphrases, so the loose
    list is reported too). Threads are cut at exchange boundaries to fit 768 tokens.
  7 The chat probe is small (53 conversations, 149 turns, written in E001) and greedy.
  8 BIG and H5L use E004's generator and vocabulary: more samples of the same held-out distribution, not a new one.
  9 The GPU is shared: jobs can wait for gpu.lock; at 2026-09-26 00:20 another process held 9.7 of 12.2 GB, which
    the guard's 6,000 MiB start rule would not have allowed; at 04:57 about ten other jobs were queued on gpu.lock
    (flock does not serve waiters in order), so a queue job can time out on the lock and retry. The WSL VM has 15
    GB of RAM. A reboot or a Windows
    update mid-queue kills the running job (GAP line).
  10 Tokenizer. transformers 5.15.1 loads SmolLM2's tokenizer with a ByteLevel pre-tokenizer only; the snapshot's
    tokenizer.json has Digits (individual) + ByteLevel. E004's and E005's saved tokenizer.json files have the same
    ByteLevel-only form, so E006 tokenizes as E004 and E005 did, and all arms share it; whether it differs from
    SmolLM2's pretraining tokenization is a question for every SmolLM2 experiment, not for this comparison. The Mac
    checks used a shim (tokenizers 0.22.2 + jinja2 on the snapshot's tokenizer.json, so with Digits). On the E004
    and E005 templates it equals HF: C's and P's first 6,404 kept (ids, labels) hash the same under both on all 5
    seeds (LOG AT WRITING 14). On OASST text it does not: the whole-template ids differ on 846 of 3,327
    threads (whitespace next to digits, e.g. a blank line before "1."). So every replay count in these notes is the
    HF one, and the queue's step 0 re-runs every check with HF.
  11 Q-H5's operating characteristics: a true 3-6 point effect most likely reads INCONCLUSIVE.
