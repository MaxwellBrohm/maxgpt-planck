WHY
  The baseline optimizer is NorMuon with cautious weight decay on matrices and AdamW on embeddings, norms and gains
  (PLAN 5). Its evidence (LEDGER row 19): Max's 124M A/B, 4.5% (0.134 nats) of paired training loss for block plus
  optimizer, "with the AdamW arm at an under-tuned lr 6e-4"; Wen et al. about 1.4x at 0.1B against tuned AdamW; IMU-1
  used NorMuon at 70M. Row 19: "The real gain at high tok/param is unknown." E2 tunes NorMuon's LRs at 5M with an
  11-arm search (stages A-C). This screen gives AdamW the same search and then compares the two at fresh paired
  seeds, which removes the confound row 19 names (an under-tuned AdamW arm). One asymmetry is left, and it favours
  NorMuon: NorMuon's LR5 can be replaced by E3's 7-seed re-check (E3 "E2 re-check", seeds 2..8; SCREENS C1), while
  AdamW's pick rests on E2's seed-1 search alone. The report states this beside every S003 verdict.

QUESTION (decided, C7)
  At 5M and 250M slots, with each optimizer at its own tuned LRs, does AdamW on the matrix parameters change held-out
  chat bits per byte relative to NorMuon?

THE CHANGE (one key, plus the arm's own LRs)
  optim.kind: adamw. harness/optim.py make_optimizer then runs every group as AdamW (the documented comparison
  arm): only the matrix group's update rule changes, since the embed and scalar groups are AdamW in both arms.
  Unchanged in both: weight decay 0.1 applied as lr * wd, cautious WD, betas (0.9, 0.95), eps 1e-8, clip 1.0, no
  decay on scalars, momentum setting (unused by AdamW). The AdamW arm's LRs come from its own search (below).

WHAT STAYS IDENTICAL
  Everything in C1: model, init, data, stream per seed, batch, schedule, engine flags, eval, scorer.

BUDGET
  B from E2 (C1): 250.0M slots, 7,630 steps, WSD with decay from step 6,104.

LEARNING RATE: E2's search, repeated for AdamW (replaces C3's g-check for this arm)
  At seed 1, E2's SCHEDULE (trunk 6,105 steps, decay branches at 62.5M / 125M / 250M), E2's grids and rules with
  optim.kind adamw in every config: stage A eta in {0.75, 1.5, 3, 6, 12} x 1e-3 at r = 1; stage B r in
  {0.5, 2, 4, 8} at eta_A; stage C g in {0.5, 2} at (eta_A, r_B); E2's extension rule (at most 2 per stage, and
  stage C while its 125M argmin sits at an edge); E2's stage pick (argmin F(CHAT) at the 250M branch, PROSE guard,
  ties to the lower LR); divergence scores +inf. Result: LR5_adamw = (eta_adamw, r_adamw). NorMuon keeps LR5 (E2's
  pick, E3-checked). Both arms were thus chosen by the same procedure at the same seed; seed 1 is never in the paired
  analysis. The branch F(CHAT) curves for AdamW (Q3 of E2: LR against run length) are reported beside E2's.

SEEDS AND PAIRING
  Seeds 101..100+k (C4). AdamW at LR5_adamw and the shared BASE run at each seed. Class: SAME INIT, SAME STREAM
  (E3 rule 1 lists the optimizer in that row): SD_ref = SD_d, df_ref its df; C4's noise check applies.
  k = max(2, k_needed(1%)), at most 5.

METRIC AND DECISION RULE
  C5 and C7: F(CHAT) decides, F(PROSE) guards, in E3 MDE units. The arm is AdamW (cautious WD), so, with what each
  verdict changes in row 19's own check (30M, 1B tokens, 3 LRs per arm, paired loss plus battery):
    NOMINATE      AdamW lowers chat bpb at 5M: a candidate to change the optimizer; row 19's 30M check runs its
                  AdamW arm and decides (the asymmetry above favours NorMuon, so it cannot have produced this).
    REJECT        NorMuon re-validated on loss at 5M, 50 tokens per parameter. What changes: row 19's 30M check
                  drops its AdamW arm (at least 3 runs at 30M, 1B tokens: one per LR); the block part of that check
                  still runs. The asymmetry above could have produced a small REJECT, so the report states it. A later
                  optimizer flip at larger size is then not looked for (KNOWN RISKS).
    TIE           no measurable difference at 5M: the baseline keeps NorMuon; row 19's 30M check keeps its AdamW
                  arm (at 5M the two could not be told apart, so only the larger size can say whether NorMuon's
                  extra step cost buys anything).
    GUARD-FAIL, INCONCLUSIVE  as C7; row 19's 30M check runs as the ledger states it.
  So the screen's only action is REJECT's saving; that is why it is second in SCREENS' cut order and runs after
  S001 and S002 in stage 1.
  Also reported, no rule: the gap in GPU hours per run (NorMuon's Newton-Schulz steps cost time; the batched
  optimizer is the default on cuda, harness/optim.py resolve_batched), and F(CHAT) per GPU-hour.

DIAGNOSTICS (C6; reported)
  Final IND, HD, the clip rate (C5), and the mean training loss over the last 2% of steps (paired training loss on an
  identical stream, LEDGER row 39) per seed. IND onset is printed and labelled "LR-confounded, not read" (C6): the
  two optimizers run at different tuned LRs by design, so no matched-LR reading exists.

AFTER THE LOCK (C8)
  Tier 0 margins with C7; E004 item pick shares reported. P-119's own measure (bits per parameter, tail items) is
  not in this screen.

KNOWN RISKS
  - Weight decay is applied as lr * wd, so the LR search also moves the effective decay (E2 SCOPE); WD itself is
    tuned for neither optimizer.
  - One search seed per optimizer (as E2). A pick that is off by one grid step biases the comparison toward
    whichever optimizer is flatter around its optimum; the stage-C curves are printed so a reader can see this.
  - 50 tokens per parameter is far from the curve's thousands (CORPUS addendum); an optimizer ranking can change
    with run length and size. After a REJECT, row 19's 30M check no longer carries AdamW, so a flip at 30M would
    go unseen; that is the price of the saving, recorded in the ledger row with the verdict.
  - Starter mix and tok_v0_8k only (C1 scope).

HARNESS CODE NEEDED

  test_optim_schedule.py checks that adamw has no Muon group; test_optim_batched.py covers adamw under the batched
  step. The config generator must write E2-style trunk and branch configs with optim.kind adamw (a check that the
  only key differing from E2's stage configs is optim.kind, plus the LRs).
