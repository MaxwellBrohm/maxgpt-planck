WHY
  P-021 asks whether Max's per-head attention gate reduces primacy (LEDGER prior art: gated attention cut
  first-token attention from 46.7% to 4.8% at 1.7B; at OPT-125M-1.3B gating removed no-op attention to first
  tokens; SinkProbe at ~1M, 3 seeds: the gate effect sat inside seed noise and the gate stayed open after 1,500
  steps; Max's 124M A/B measured loss only). PLAN 3d tests it at 30M with correction dialogues, "logging gate
  openness and sink mass". Nothing at 5M says whether the gate earns its place in the baseline at all (row 19),
  whether it engages in a 7,630-step run, or whether it moves first-token attention. This screen answers those three
  cheaply; the primacy question itself needs correction items and waits for the lock and 30M.

QUESTIONS
  Q1 (decided, C7) Does removing the gate change held-out chat bits per byte at 5M, 250M slots?
  Q2 (reported) Does the gate engage in BASE (GATE), and does first-token attention (SINK) differ between the arms?
  Q3 (reported) Final IND and HD in both arms. IND onset is read only if the arm's g pick is 1 (C6 MATCHED LR);
     otherwise it is printed and labelled "LR-confounded, not read" (S001 gets no extra g = 1 runs).

THE CHANGE (one key)
  model.attn_gate: false. The field exists (harness/config.py); with it off, blocks.py builds no attn_gate_proj and
  skips the 2*sigmoid(W x) multiply. Parameters: 5,005,525 vs BASE 5,010,133 (-0.09%; budget.py count_analytic,
  2026-09-26), inside the 2% budget rule, so the shape is not re-solved.

SEEDS AND PAIRING
  Seeds 101..100+k (C4), paired with the shared BASE run at each seed. Class: NEW INIT. The gate projection is an
  nn.Linear that reset_parameters draws from the torch RNG before zeroing it (model.py), so removing it shifts every
  later draw. SD_ref = sqrt(2) * sigma_init from E3 Part 3 (sqrt(2) * sigma_seed if Part 3 did not run), df_ref its
  df; k = max(2, k_needed(1%)) for that SD, at most 5 (E3 rule 2).

METRIC AND DECISION RULE
  C5 and C7: F(CHAT) decides, F(PROSE) guards, thresholds in units of E3's MDE_p(k) (parameters from E3
  results.json). The arm is "gate off", so:
    NOMINATE      removing the gate lowers chat bpb: a candidate to drop it, confirmed at 20M (with its own g-check)
                  before any recipe change. P-021's 30M test still runs (must-run: its target is primacy, not loss).
    REJECT        the gate earns its place on loss at 5M: row 19 re-validated for the gate at this size.
    TIE           no measurable loss effect at 5M/250M (95% interval inside +-1%): the baseline keeps the gate (no
                  change is ever adopted on a tie), and the ledger records "loss-neutral at 5M".
    GUARD-FAIL, INCONCLUSIVE  as C7; nothing changes.

DIAGNOSTICS (C6; reported, no threshold: no repo file gives one for gate openness or sink mass at this size)
  GATE  in BASE, per layer and head: mean, 10th and 90th percentile of 2*sigmoid(W x) over the 200 CHAT windows,
        on the final checkpoint. "Measurably closed" is a question about time, and a full-schedule run keeps no
        early checkpoints (keep_last 2), so the in-training hook (SCREENS CODE, eval.induction) also logs the per-head
        mean every 153 steps on a fixed 32-window subset: the GATE curve. The ledger's P-021 win needs the gate
        "measurably closed"; this is the first look at whether and when a 5M, 250M-slot run gets there (SinkProbe's
        gate did not, at ~1M and 1,500 steps).
        The gate is also a per-head output scale in (0, 2) (blocks.py: gate = 2.0 * sigmoid(attn_gate_proj(x)),
        multiplied into the head output), so a bpb difference between the arms can come from that scale alone: Q1's
        verdict is not evidence about sinks. SINK is the sink reading.
  SINK  both arms, per layer: first-token attention mass; the per-seed paired difference (gate on minus off) and
        the number of seeds in which it is negative (the ledger's "first-token attention falls with the gate on").
  IND, HD  both arms, as C6.

KNOWN RISKS
  - The gate may stay open for the whole run (SinkProbe). Then Q1 compares an unused gate with none and a TIE is
    the expected reading; GATE shows whether that happened.
  - Loss is not behaviour for attention changes (research/lanes/arch.md 1.4): a TIE or REJECT here says nothing about
    primacy, which is why the 30M test runs regardless.
  - New-init noise is larger than the paired LR noise E3's SD_d measures; k may reach 5 and the label
    "underpowered" (C4).
  - Starter mix: chat-kind tokens are 2.8% of the stream (C1). The gate's effect at the Phase 3 chat share may
    differ; the refresh rule (C1) applies when the pool mix exists.
  - At initialization the gate is exactly 1 (zero-init projection), so a leak routed through it is invisible on a
    fresh model; harness/selftest.py already runs its checks on a scrambled copy for this reason.
