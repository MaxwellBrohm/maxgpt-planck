WHY
  Planck's bet spends parameters on conversational skill and keeps knowledge outside. That is worth doing for
  capacity reasons only if facts held in the weights cost skill. No usable direct result exists: the one joint study
  (Barron and White, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09099) is a 1.46K-10.6M char-level toy where every model gets 0/40
  on its held-out cases once facts are added (capacity.md; arch.md F12), and arch.md lists this curve as U1,
  untested at 5M. Physics 3.3 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05405) gives the scale, about 1 bit/param at 100
  exposures, and warns that junk-like data slows fact loading. Two mechanisms predict a crowd-out without a capacity
  limit: in-context learning gives way to in-weights learning when both solve the same items (Singh et al.,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08360), and the memorization-to-ICL transition is set by the relative learning rates of
  sub-circuits "rather than capacity constraints" (Nguyen and Reddy, https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00104). K1 keeps
  skill entities disjoint from the fact base (Singh) and adds a kinetics probe (K1-X).

QUESTIONS
  Q1 At equal total tokens and equal skill tokens, does raising the memorizable fact load from zero to about the
     100-exposure capacity lower held-out in-context skill (S_ID, SPEC 9)?
  Q2 (reported) Do skill and language data lower fact storage (FH vs K0-cap)? Does skill peak and fade? How are FB
     keys answered when the context states a new value (CONFLICT)?

KNOWN RISKS
  - 7,630 steps may not load facts (K0-cap). The manipulation is then weaker than nominal and the verdict says so.
  - Filler is same-format junk: all of F0's FACT stream, about 3/4 of FL's. It is constant in tokens, not in
    content, and Physics 3.3 Result 10 says same-format junk slows fact loading, so FL may load less per entity than
    FH; loads are therefore read as measured bits, never nominal.
  - Equal skill dose is bought with filler: F0 is a zero-fact model that still reads about 124M filler tokens.
  - Vocabulary is not equal across arms: FH's FACT stream trains 336 F-pool name rows, F0's 120 Z-pool rows, and a Z
    token sits in 1/40 of filler docs against 1/112 for an F token. Kept on purpose: a disjoint filler pool tells
    filler from FB data at the first token, the role of Physics 3.3 Result 12's domain token (an analogy, untested
    here), which cut the same-format-junk loss from up to 20x to 2x at 100 exposures (capacity.md); filler names from
    F-pool triples outside FB_high would equalize the vocabulary and drop that protection. Named, not neutralized.
  - Pool cue: FB names and skill names come from disjoint pools, so a model can learn "FB name: memory; skill name:
    context". That is the Singh safeguard's price; CONFLICT is out of distribution for K1.
  - One world (W = 7, uniform values, uniform exposure); world-draw variance is not measured. Kim et al.
    (https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02370) found a Zipfian fact base shifts the balance toward context use; K1's
    uniform base is the harder case for skill.
  - df 4 makes thr about 1.6 x SD_ref; a small real effect will read UNRESOLVED.
  - Minimal slot frames: a verdict is about associative recall, update, binding, reference, abstention and
    persistence in this world, not about natural chat. The family shares were set by hand, not tuned.
  - Loss is not a behaviour proxy (SCREENS, citing research/lanes/arch.md 1.4), so no K1 reading rests on bpb.
