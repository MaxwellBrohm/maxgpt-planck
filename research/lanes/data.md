# Lane report: data that makes a sub-350M model conversational

Research sweep for MaxGPT-Nano (target: about 150M parameters, real back-and-forth chat with multi-turn coherence). Date: 2026-09-23. Scope: pretraining data, mid-training and annealing, SFT data, synthetic dialogue generation, repetition, and knowledge versus skill. Every number carries a source URL and the scale it was measured at. "Vendor" means the number comes from the model's own card or blog and was not independently reproduced. "Derived" means I computed it from sourced inputs; the inputs are named. Evidence that exists only at 1B or above is flagged **[>=1B only]**.

---

## Bottom line

1. The premise needs one correction: small chat models below 400M already exist, and the best of them got there through data, not parameters. Falcon-H1-Tiny-90M-Instruct (Jan 2026) reports MT-Bench 4.33 and IFEval 66.08 on TII's harness; SmolLM2-360M-Instruct reports MT-Bench 3.66 against 4.16 for Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct on HF's harness (vendor numbers, different harnesses, and MT-Bench is only two turns). What nobody has published is a sub-400M model trained, or measured, for deep multi-turn coherence. That is the open lane.
2. The strongest data result at this scale is **"anti-curriculum"**: put chat/SFT-format data into pretraining from step 0 instead of saving it for a short SFT. At 90M params / 800B tokens, 25% SFT data in the pretraining mix, repeated about 100 or more times, beat pretrain-then-SFT on IFEval (66.08 vs 53.47, both after DPO). MiniCPM (1.2B to 2.4B) and Instruction Pre-Training (500M, 100B tokens) point the same way; a controlled 70M to 1B study says mid-training helps in proportion to how close its data is to the post-training target.
3. Max's current 100B build (FineWeb-Edu, Cosmopedia, code, math, Wikipedia) contains close to zero dialogue. Conversational text is only about 2.7 to 3.7% of raw web, and nothing in an education filter pushes that share up. For a 150M model that can only take a short SFT, that distribution gap is itself a big part of "the wall".
4. The other big part of the wall is **knowledge, and data cannot fix it**: about 2 bits per parameter at best (1 bit at 100 exposures), so a 150M model holds at most roughly 15 to 40 MB of facts (derived, section 1.9). SmolLM2-135M scores 4.1% on TriviaQA after 2T tokens. A 150M chat model should be trained knowledge-light and grounded: dialogue that reads facts out of the context (retrieval, the user's own words, earlier turns) and says "I don't know" otherwise.
5. Public multi-turn SFT data is shallow. smol-smoltalk averages 4.6 messages (about 2.3 turns) per conversation; UltraChat has only 1.45 context-dependent user queries per session versus 4.62 in real ShareGPT logs, because simulated users write information-complete queries. No existing small model has been trained on data dense in back-references, corrections and instruction persistence. That is the most promising place for a new technique.
6. Repetition is much less of a constraint at this size than the "4 epochs" rule suggests: a 100M model forgets a sample within about 1 to 5B tokens, and spaced repetition of a small high-quality set worked for 100 or more passes in two TII studies. A curated dialogue pool of a few hundred million to a few billion tokens is enough to fill 20 to 25% of a 100B-token run.
7. Cheap generation: use Apache-2.0 open teachers (Qwen3 / Qwen3.5 at 4B to 9B) on the Mac now and the 5070 later; rephraser quality saturates around 3B. Do not use the Claude subscription to generate training data: Anthropic's consumer terms list training AI models among prohibited uses. Budget reality: the M5 can produce on the order of 5 to 10M tokens per day (derived estimate), enough for a targeted multi-turn set, not for pretraining-scale rephrasing.

## 1. Detailed findings

### 1.1 Tiny-model data: TinyStories, SimpleStories, TinyDialogues, BabyLM

- **TinyStories** (Eldan and Li 2023): GPT-3.5/GPT-4 generated stories restricted to words a 3 to 4 year old understands; models "below 10 million total parameters" or with one transformer block produce fluent, consistent multi-paragraph stories ([arXiv 2305.07759](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759)). Scale: the headline claim is for models under 10M params. Lesson: shrinking the world (vocabulary, knowledge, register) lets a tiny model be coherent. Max saw the same thing: MaxGPT-1 (23M, TinyStories + OASST) wrote coherent narrative but could not chat (`WRITEUP_NOTES.md`).
- **SimpleStories** (Apr 2025): 2M stories each in English and Japanese, generated with prompts parameterized at several levels of abstraction for syntactic and semantic diversity; reports better sample efficiency than TinyStories on a new model suite ([arXiv 2504.09184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09184)). Lesson: parameterized prompting beats a single template for diversity.
- **TinyDialogues / child-directed speech** (Feng, Goodman, Frank, EMNLP 2024): GPT-2 and RoBERTa trained on 29M words; synthetic child-directed dialogue (TinyDialogues) beat natural CHILDES for GPT-2; child-directed speech was "not uniquely valuable"; **local discourse ordering helped but global developmental ordering did not** ([arXiv 2408.03617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03617), data MIT-licensed at [styfeng/TinyDialogues](https://huggingface.co/datasets/styfeng/TinyDialogues)). Scale: about 29M words, GPT-2-small class.
- **BabyLM 2023** (10M and 100M words): curriculum learning was the most popular approach (13 of 31 teams, 41.9%) and "did not tend to produce high scores"; winners changed architecture (LTG-BERT) or used teacher distillation ([findings pdf](https://aclanthology.org/2023.conll-babylm.1.pdf)).
- **BabyLM 2024**: a hybrid causal-masked model won; the best submissions changed data, objective and architecture together; strong relationship between training FLOPs and score ([arXiv 2412.05149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05149)).
- **BabyLM 2025**: new interaction track (student learns from teacher feedback); "no complete correlation" between FLOPs and performance ([findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28/)). A directly relevant 2025 paper, "Dialogue Is Not Enough to Make a Communicative BabyLM", found **dialogue-only pretraining underperformed on most standard benchmarks but excelled at dialogue-continuation minimal pairs**; PPO had mixed-to-adversarial effects, DPO helped dialogue continuation ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.29/)). Scale: BabyLM word budgets (10M to 100M words).

**Takeaway:** at tiny scale, simple register and local coherence matter; developmental curricula mostly do not; dialogue-only data makes a model good at dialogue form and bad at everything else. Dialogue has to be mixed with general text.

### 1.2 Textbook and fully synthetic pretraining: Phi, Cosmopedia, SYNTH

- **phi-1 / phi-1-small** (2023): phi-1-small, **350M**, trained with the same "textbook quality" pipeline (6B filtered web tokens plus 1B GPT-3.5 synthetic textbook/exercise tokens), reached 45% HumanEval ([arXiv 2306.11644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11644)). Narrow domain (Python), but it shows a sub-400M model can reach a high skill level on targeted synthetic data.
- **phi-1.5** (1.3B): synthetic textbook data, natural-language performance "comparable to models 5x larger"; still hallucinates ([arXiv 2309.05463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05463)) **[>=1B only]**.
- **Cosmopedia v1**: over 30M files, 25B tokens, generated by Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct with over 10,000 GPU hours; 4 audiences x 3 styles for diversity, under 1% duplicate content; Cosmo-1B beat TinyLlama-1.1B on ARC, OpenBookQA and MMLU but trailed phi-1.5 ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/cosmopedia)) **[>=1B only]**.
- **Cosmopedia v2** (in SmolLM-Corpus): 28B tokens, 39M documents, 34,000 BISAC topics; textbooks written for **middle-school students scored best on all benchmarks except MMLU** versus college-level ([SmolLM blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/smollm)). Measured with SmolLM ablations (small models). Lesson: simpler register is better for small models.
- **PleIAs SYNTH** (Nov 2025, CC-BY-4.0): 79,648,272 samples, about 41B words / 75B tokens, amplified from 58,698 Wikipedia/Wikibooks seed pages, each amplified at least 100 times; exercises include memorization, RAG with grounding, arithmetic, editing, extraction, creative writing and multi-turn conversations derived from single-turn examples; deliberately "enough negative queries" to limit hallucination; no code ([dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PleIAs/SYNTH), [blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/Pclanglais/synth-data-frontier)).
  - Baguettotron 321M trained on 200B SYNTH tokens: MMLU 40, GSM8K 39, HotPotQA 51, versus SmolLM2-360M at 25 / 11 / 16 and Qwen3-0.6B at 44 / 40 / 64 in the same vendor table ([card image](https://huggingface.co/PleIAs/Baguettotron/resolve/main/figures/table_evaluation.png)). Final runs used under 1,000 H100 hours; the project used 20,000 ([blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/Pclanglais/synth-data-frontier)).
  - Caveats that matter for chat: every SYNTH answer carries a reasoning trace in a custom notation; the card says turning thinking off "significantly decreased performance for most tasks, especially memorization"; Monad has no multi-turn support. SYNTH is a benchmark-reasoning corpus, not a conversational one. It proves that **a narrow, dense, synthetic distribution can move a 56M to 321M model a long way with only 200B tokens**, which is Max's token regime.
- **Instruction Pre-Training** (Cheng et al., EMNLP 2024): 200M instruction-response pairs synthesized from raw text by a fine-tuned Mistral-7B, mixed into pretraining; **a 500M model on 100B tokens scored 46.6 average versus 45.7 for Pythia-1B on 300B**, and gained more from later instruction tuning ([arXiv 2406.14491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14491), Table 2 of the [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.14491v2)). The synthesizer's own fine-tuning data (0.2B tokens) was repeated 4 times in pretraining.

### 1.3 Filtered web: FineWeb-Edu, DCLM, and what each one selects for

- **FineWeb-Edu**: 1.3T tokens filtered from 15T FineWeb with a classifier trained on 460,000 Llama-3-70B-Instruct educational-value annotations; at **1.71B params / 350B tokens**, MMLU 33 to 37% and ARC 46 to 57% versus FineWeb ([arXiv 2406.17557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17557)). The threshold was chosen as a trade-off against HellaSwag, i.e. the education filter costs everyday/commonsense register. **[>=1B only]**
- **DCLM**: the best quality filter was a fastText classifier whose **positive examples are instruction data (OpenHermes-2.5) plus r/ExplainLikeImFive posts**. At 7B-1x scale, CORE 41.0 versus 35.7 for Wikipedia positives and 34.7 for OpenWebText2; at 1B-1x it beat perplexity filtering, AskLLM, top-k logits and semantic dedup (30.2 CORE vs 27.1 to 29.2) ([arXiv 2406.11794](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11794v1), Tables 4 and 5). In other words, **the best general-purpose web filter already selects for question-answer, explanatory, conversational register.**
- **SmolLM2 ablations**: FineWeb-Edu wins MMLU / ARC / OpenBookQA, DCLM wins HellaSwag and CommonsenseQA; 60/40 FineWeb-Edu/DCLM captured most of both. For the 135M and 360M models they re-ran ablations at the target length and found "filtering DCLM with the FineWeb-Edu classifier, removing samples with score 0, and downsampling those with scores 1 and 2 worked best" ([arXiv 2502.02737](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02737v1)).
- Max's 100B build is about 55% FineWeb-Edu-dedup, 22% Cosmopedia-v2, 10% code, 8% OpenWebMath, 5% Wikipedia by tokens, with **no DCLM and no dialogue source** (`docs/research_2026-09-22.md`). For chat, that is the wrong half of the SmolLM2 trade-off.

### 1.4 Rephrased web: WRAP, Nemotron-CC, BeyondWeb

- **WRAP** (Maini et al. 2024): Mistral-7B-Instruct rephrases C4 into Wikipedia-like and **question-answer ("conversation question-answering") styles**; measured at **128M, 350M and 1.3B**. About 3x faster pretraining, perplexity better by over 10% across Pile subsets, zero-shot QA up over 2%; a **350M model trained on real plus rephrased data built from only 15% of C4 beat a 1.3B model trained on all of C4** (on Pile perplexity, the paper's Figure 1c discussion). Synthetic-only training hurt some tasks, so real data stays in the mix ([arXiv 2401.16380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16380), [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.16380v1)). This is one of the few rephrasing results measured at Max's scale.
- **Nemotron-CC**: 6.3T tokens (4.4T real, 1.9T synthetic rephrasings); +5.6 MMLU over DCLM at 8B / 1T tokens ([arXiv 2412.02595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02595)) **[>=1B only]**.
- **BeyondWeb** (DatologyAI, Aug 2025): beats Cosmopedia by up to 5.1 points and Nemotron-Synth by 2.6 on a 14-task average; 3B on 180B tokens beats 8B on Cosmopedia at the same budget. Two findings that matter here, both measured with Llama-3.2-1B ablations: (a) web is only about 2.7% conversational (GPT-4o labels on 10k RedPajama docs; 3.67% by style tags); **upsampling conversational content to 10 / 20 / 50% moved the 20B-token 5-shot average from 43.2 to 43.5 / 44.0 / 44.1, saturating after 20%**; (b) **rephraser size shows diminishing returns beyond 3B**, 8B only marginally better ([arXiv 2508.10975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10975), section 4.5 and 4.8 of the [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.10975v2)). The conversational result is on benchmarks, not chat quality.

### 1.5 Dialogue in pretraining: does it help multi-turn ability?

Direct evidence is thin, and all of it is either old chit-chat work or benchmark-level. What exists:

- **DialoGPT** (2019): GPT-2 architecture trained on 147M Reddit exchanges (1.8B words) at **117M, 345M, 762M**; "performance close to human" in automatic and human evaluation **for single-turn** responses; 345M beat 117M across the board and 762M was comparable to 345M ([arXiv 1911.00536](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00536)). Social chit-chat at 345M was already near-human on single turns six years ago.
- **Meena** (2020): 2.6B trained on 40B words (341GB) of social-media conversations; human-judged Sensibleness and Specificity Average correlates with test perplexity at **R^2 = 0.93** ([arXiv 2001.09977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09977)) **[>=1B only]**. If conversational quality tracks loss on conversational text, then putting that distribution into pretraining is the most direct lever.
- **BlenderBot** (2020): 90M vs 2.7B generative models after Blended Skill Talk fine-tuning, self-chat ACUTE-Eval engagingness 43 vs 57, "not statistically significant"; the paper still reports large gains from 90M to 2.7B overall and no clear win from 2.7B to 9.4B despite lower perplexity ([arXiv 2004.13637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13637)). The 90M was Reddit-pretrained.
- **BabyLM dialogue-only** (2025, above): dialogue-only pretraining gives dialogue form but weak general ability ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.29/)).
- **BeyondWeb** (above): more conversational text helps benchmarks a little and saturates at about 20% ([arXiv 2508.10975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10975)).
- **Max's own MaxGPT-3** (235M): pretrained on a 4.6B-token mix that was about 60% chat (UltraChat 29.2%, OpenOrca 17.9%, WildChat 11.4%, plus OASST and HH-RLHF) for 20B token-passes. It learned chat format but showed low-entropy loops and confident factual nonsense (`WRITEUP_NOTES.md`). This is an uncontrolled anecdote, but it fits the pattern: chat-heavy pretraining buys format, not knowledge, and noisy chat data brings its own loops.

**Takeaway:** dialogue data in pretraining reliably teaches dialogue form and lowers loss on dialogue; there is no published controlled measurement of whether it improves *multi-turn coherence* at sub-400M. The best current evidence for chat-format data in pretraining is the instruction/SFT-format work in section 1.6.

### 1.6 Chat and instruction data inside pretraining, mid-training and annealing

- **Falcon-H1-Tiny "anti-curriculum"** (TII, Jan 2026), the most directly relevant result in this lane:
  - 90M English models, 800B tokens with a 100B-token decay. Five mixes with 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100% of the SFT mixture tested. Final: **25% SFT data in pretraining**, described as a model that "can act as a base and instruct model at the same time".
  - Anti-curriculum + DPO vs classical pretrain-then-SFT + DPO: **IFEval 66.08 vs 53.47, MMLU 27.31 vs 24.81, GSM8K 19.18 vs 20.24**.
  - SFT sources such as Tulu 3 were repeated **"≳100"** times during the 800B tokens.
  - For the curriculum baseline, the best SFT stage was about **10B tokens** (best checkpoint from an 8B-to-16B sweep), far longer than a typical SFT.
  - Keeping 20% web data (vs 10%) gave slightly better English commonsense at little cost elsewhere.
  - Source: [TII blog](https://tiiuae-tiny-h1-blogpost.hf.space/). Vendor, no paper, harness is TII's own. Measured at 90M.
- **MiniCPM**: 2.4B and 1.2B, exponential decay over 5,000 steps (about 20B tokens). Mixing high-quality + SFT data into the decay beat decay-on-pretraining-data followed by SFT (2.4B: GSM8K 42.3 vs 27.7, HumanEval 30.4 vs 27.7), and doubling SFT tokens after a plain decay did not close the gap ([arXiv 2404.06395](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.06395)) **[>=1B only]**.
- **OLMo 2 Dolmino** anneal mix (50B version): DCLM 47.2%, FLAN 16.6%, math 20.8%, Wikipedia 7.1%, peS2o 5.85%, StackExchange 2.45% ([dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/dolmino-mix-1124)) **[>=1B only]**.
- **nanochat** (Karpathy, Oct 2025): d20, 561M params, 11.2B FineWeb-Edu pretraining tokens; mid-training on 568K rows (SmolTalk 460K, MMLU auxiliary-train 100K, GSM8K 8K) teaches the conversation special tokens, multiple choice and tool use; final ChatCORE 0.0884 after SFT ([discussion #1](https://github.com/karpathy/nanochat/discussions/1)). Scale: 561M, only about 20 tokens per parameter, so it is a floor, not a ceiling.
- **"Midtraining Bridges Pretraining and Posttraining Distributions"** (Liu, Neubig, Xiong, Oct 2025, rev. Feb 2026): controlled Pythia **70M to 1B** on 128B C4 tokens; mid-training helps in proportion to a "proximity advantage" (how much closer the mid-training data is to the SFT target than C4 is); code mid-training helped code, math helped math, **FLAN (generic instructions) gave minimal effect on those targets** ([arXiv 2510.14865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14865)). For a chat target, the implication is that the mid-training data should look like the target chats, not like generic instruction sets.
- **SmolLM2 small models**: 135M and 360M used "a single-stage training approach with consistently high-quality data" rather than 1.7B's multi-stage schedule ([arXiv 2502.02737](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02737v1)). Consistent with anti-curriculum: at small scale, do not save the good data for the end.

### 1.7 SmolLM2's per-size choices and SmolTalk

- Tokens: 135M on 2T, 360M on 4T, 1.7B on about 11T ([arXiv 2502.02737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02737)).
- Pretraining mix for the small models: FineWeb-Edu, DCLM (filtered with the FineWeb-Edu classifier), Stack-Edu from the start, InfiMM-WebMath, FineMath, Cosmopedia ([arXiv 2502.02737](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02737v1)).
- Full SmolTalk (1.7B): MagPie-Ultra 1M three-turn conversations from Llama-3.1-405B, Smol-Constraint 36k, Smol-Summarization about 1M, Smol-Rewrite about 600k, NuminaMath-CoT + MetaMathQA (20%), Self-OSS-Starcoder2-Instruct 50k, SystemChats2.0 30k, APIGen function calling 80k, LongAlign 3.7k, OpenHermes2.5 100k, Everyday-Conversations 2.2k ([arXiv 2502.02737](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02737v1)).
- **smol-smoltalk** for 135M/360M: 460,341 train conversations; shorter Magpie conversations; no function calling, less rewriting/summarization, no advanced math, "since these smaller models have limited capacity" ([dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceTB/smol-smoltalk)). Composition I pulled from the datasets-server statistics endpoint: smol-magpie-ultra-short 270,838 (59%), self-oss-instruct 48,071, openhermes-50k 47,492, smol-constraints 34,433, rewrite 26,657 + 3,017, summarize 24,021, longalign 3,560, everyday-conversations 2,252; **messages per conversation mean 4.64, median 6, min 2, max 58** ([statistics API](https://datasets-server.huggingface.co/statistics?dataset=HuggingFaceTB/smol-smoltalk&config=default&split=train)). So the typical small-model SFT conversation is two to three turns.
- DPO for 135M and 360M: UltraFeedback ([cards](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-360M-Instruct)).
- **Everyday-Conversations** (2.2k Llama-3.1-70B dialogues, 3 to 4 short exchanges, all starting with a greeting): made because "when training only on the publicly available instructions datasets the models failed to answer basic prompts such as 'Hi' ... and 'Who are you'" at 135M, 360M and 1.7B ([dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceTB/everyday-conversations-llama3.1-2k)). A small, targeted set fixed a failure that looked like a capacity problem. Useful precedent.

### 1.8 Repetition: how many times can a small model see the same data?

- **Muennighoff et al.** (400+ models, 10M to 9B params, up to 900B tokens): up to **4 epochs** is nearly as good as unique data; the fitted decay constant R*_D is about 15.4, i.e. repeated tokens have lost 1/e of their value around **16 epochs**; around **40 epochs** repeating is worthless; mixing in code gives up to **2x effective tokens** even for natural-language tasks ([arXiv 2305.16264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16264), fit in the [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.16264) appendix). This is for globally shuffled repetition of a whole corpus.
- **Memorization window** (TII, ICLR 2026 DATA-FM workshop, arXiv Jul 2026): **100M Transformer** trained 500B tokens on FineWeb: loss on a seen sample drops after exposure and the retention gap decays to near zero between 1B and 5B tokens later (retention window 1 to 5B tokens). With 0.8B unique high-quality tokens (OpenMathInstruct2) spaced by FineWeb, MATH500 peaked after about **125 to 190 epochs** when the epoch size was at least 1.6B tokens; with enough spacing, repeated data gave the same marginal loss improvement as fresh data. Adding just **6% low-quality web** to the high-quality set "dramatically" outperformed pure high-quality repetition ([arXiv 2607.04969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04969)). Limits stated by the authors: one domain (math), one scale, one HQ budget. MATH500 accuracies are around 0.10 at this size, so absolute effects are small.
- **Falcon-H1-Tiny**: scales a conservative 500B-token window measured on FalconMamba-7B linearly down to about **5B tokens** for 100M models, and repeated Tulu 3 SFT sources ≳100 times at 90M ([blog](https://tiiuae-tiny-h1-blogpost.hf.space/)).
- **Derived implication**: in a 100B-token run, a dialogue pool of size P at share s recurs every P/s tokens. With P = 0.5B and s = 15%, each dialogue recurs every 3.3B tokens (inside the 1 to 5B window) for 30 passes. That sits well inside the regime both TII sources report as safe for about 100M models.

### 1.9 Knowledge versus skill: where a small model's capacity goes

- **Knowledge capacity** (Physics of LMs 3.3, Allen-Zhu and Li 2024): GPT-2-family models store about **2 bits of knowledge per parameter** with 1,000 exposures per fact and about **1 bit per parameter with 100 exposures**; with a 1:7 ratio of useful to "junk" tokens, capacity for the useful knowledge drops **20x** (100 exposures), improving to 3x / 1.5x / 1.3x at 300 / 600 / 1,000 exposures; **prepending a source tag (like a domain name) to the useful data cuts the 20x loss to 2x** ([arXiv 2404.05405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05405), [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.05405v1)). Their parameter counts exclude embeddings (GPT-2 small "counted as having only 88M").
  - Derived: a 150M model with about 28M tied embedding params (49,152 x 576) has about 120M non-embedding params, so at most about 240M bits (30 MB) of facts at 1,000 exposures and about 120M bits (15 MB) at 100 exposures.
- **Memorization capacity** (Morris et al. 2025, 500K to 1.5B params): GPT-style models memorize about **3.6 bits per parameter**, and generalization rises once capacity is full ([arXiv 2505.24832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24832)).
- **Observed factual floor**: TriviaQA 4.1 for SmolLM2-135M (2T tokens), 16.9 for SmolLM2-360M (4T), 4.3 for Qwen2.5-0.5B base on the same card, 15.4 for Gemma 3 270M PT (6T) ([135M card](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-135M-Instruct), [360M card](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-360M-Instruct), [Gemma card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_3)). Trillions of tokens do not buy closed-book recall at 135M.
- **Knowledge can be supplied instead of stored**: Pleias-RAG-350M, mid-trained on synthetic retrieval-grounded data with citations, is reported to outperform SLMs below 4B on HotPotQA and 2wiki and be competitive with Qwen-2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-3-4B ([arXiv 2504.18225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18225)). Vendor. The model had to be trained to read sources; see the mechanics lane for evidence that untrained small models do not use retrieved passages well.
- **Is capacity "wasted" on facts?** No direct experiment removes facts from a small model's pretraining and measures chat skill. The indirect evidence: TinyStories and SimpleStories show that shrinking world knowledge lets tiny models be coherent; the junk-data result shows low-density text crowds out useful knowledge; SYNTH's "negative queries" and Monad's own warning ("hallucinations have to be expected") show the design problem is teaching the model when it does not know. This is an open question worth a controlled test (section 5).

Known quality problems, with evidence:

1. **Simulated users write information-complete queries, so there is little to remember.** Parrot (ACL 2024) counted context-dependent queries (anaphora, ellipsis) per session: **ShareGPT 4.62 in 6.67 turns, UltraChat 1.45 in 3.85, Baize 1.75 in 4.54**; their Parrot-40K reaches 3.42 in 8.71 turns and gave +0.41 to +0.47 MT-Bench over UltraLM/Vicuna baselines ([arXiv 2310.07301](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.07301v2)) **[>=1B only]** for the gains. The cause stated: prompting ChatGPT to simulate users "usually produce[s] information-complete queries".
2. **Small generators lose coherence in long dialogues; self-chat is worse than cross-model chat.** DeepDialogue paired 9 models (4B to 72B) to make 65,600 conversations and found "smaller models fail to maintain coherence beyond 6 dialogue turns" and "cross-model interactions produce more coherent dialogues than same-model conversations" ([arXiv 2505.19978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19978)).
3. **Utterances are too long and too hard for a small student.** UltraChat averages 309 tokens per utterance. Small students (at or below 3B, math reasoning) learn better from shorter, simpler chains and from smaller teachers than from long CoT by strong teachers ([arXiv 2502.12143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12143)); SmolLM2 cut Magpie conversations and hard tasks for 135M/360M; Cosmopedia found middle-school register best.
4. **Diversity collapses without explicit parameterization.** Cosmopedia needed audience x style x topic seeding to get under 1% duplicates ([blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/cosmopedia)); SimpleStories beat TinyStories by parameterizing prompts ([arXiv 2504.09184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09184)); PersonaHub exists for this reason.
5. **Repetitive training text produces degenerate loops.** Degeneration correlates strongly with repetitions in the training data, and penalizing training-data repetitions is the common factor behind other fixes, including after instruction tuning ([arXiv 2310.10226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10226)). Max's MaxGPT-2/3 "Bitcoin.com" loops are consistent with this (his notes attribute the pattern to spam in UltraChat).
6. **Even real multi-turn data does not make frontier models reliable across turns.** Across 15 LLMs, performance on the same tasks drops 39% on average when instructions arrive in shards over several turns, driven by unreliability (+112%) more than aptitude loss (-15%) ([arXiv 2505.06120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120)) **[>=1B only]**. Hy-MultiTurn (Jul 2026, 209 tasks of 12 to 76 turns): the best configuration, GPT-5.5, satisfies all requirements in only 41.1% of responses ([arXiv 2607.29196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29196)) **[>=1B only]**. The target is hard at every scale; a 150M model should aim at short-horizon reference tracking, not 50-turn projects.

### 1.11 What Max can generate cheaply

- **Licensing.** Anthropic's Consumer Terms (effective Oct 8, 2025), section 3, list among prohibited uses developing competing products, explicitly including developing or training AI/ML models ([consumer terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)). The same section bars automated access except via an API key or where explicitly permitted. Reading: the Claude subscription is fine for writing code, designing prompts and reviewing samples by hand, but it should not be a source of training text. Open teachers with clean terms: Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507, Qwen3.5-4B, Qwen3.5-9B and OLMo-3-7B-Instruct are all Apache-2.0 on the Hub ([HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B)); Gemma uses its own license; Llama-generated sets (Magpie-Llama, Smol-Magpie-Ultra via 405B) carry Llama-license terms or the dataset's stated license.
- **Teacher size.** For rephrasing, gains saturate past about 3B ([BeyondWeb](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10975)). For long coherent dialogues, 4B-class generators break down past about 6 turns ([DeepDialogue](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19978)), so use the largest teacher that fits for the multi-turn set, and pair two different models as user and assistant.
- **Throughput on the Mac (derived estimate).** vllm-mlx on an M4 Max (546 GB/s) measured single-request 4-bit throughput of 159 tok/s for Qwen3-4B and 93 tok/s for Qwen3-8B, with batching gains of 3.7x (0.6B) and 2.6x (8B) at 16 concurrent requests ([arXiv 2601.19139](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19139v2)). The M5 has 153 GB/s ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/10/apple-unleashes-m5-the-next-big-leap-in-ai-performance-for-apple-silicon/)). Scaling decode by bandwidth gives roughly 45 tok/s single-stream for a 4B (about 130 tok/s batched, about 11M tokens/day) and roughly 26 tok/s for an 8B (about 70 tok/s batched, about 6M tokens/day). These are derived, not measured on Max's machine. Over a summer that is 0.5 to 1B tokens: enough for a few hundred thousand targeted multi-turn dialogues, not for pretraining-scale rephrasing.
- **5070 and Titans.** No sourced vLLM throughput for TITAN RTX (sm_75) or the 5070 (unsourced; measure it). The 5070 is lent out and the sustainable Titans are running Ultra, so the Mac is the only generator available now.
- **What is already free on disk or on the Hub**: smol-smoltalk (460k), SmolTalk, UltraChat (about 2.15B tokens, derived from 1,468,352 x 1,467.4), SODA (300M tokens), WildChat-4.8M (3.2M real conversations), OASST2, Everyday-Conversations, TinyDialogues, SimpleStories, SYNTH (75B tokens), Cosmopedia v2 (28B), DCLM. Max's own SFT set (108k rows) and preference set (60k pairs) are small next to these. **The raw material for a several-billion-token dialogue pool already exists at zero cost**; what does not exist is back-reference-dense, short-utterance, knowledge-light, grounded multi-turn data, which is the part worth generating.

## 2. What this implies for a ~150M chat model

### 2.1 Token budget reality

- Measured on Max's hardware: the 124M A/B ran at about 37,500 tokens/s on one TITAN RTX with loaded neighbours (`docs/ab_2026-09-21/normuon_arch.metrics.jsonl`, step 7610), up to about 53k with idle neighbours (`LAMBDA.md`). MaxGPT-3 (235M, older architecture) ran 0.23 s per 8x1024-token step on the 5070, about 35,600 tokens/s (`WRITEUP_NOTES.md`).
- Derived: a 150M model gets roughly 2.7B tokens per Titan-day or roughly 4.8B tokens per 5070-day. 100B tokens is about 20 to 40 card-days; Falcon-H1-Tiny's 800B would be about 170 to 300; SmolLM2-135M's 2T would be over 400.
- So Max lives in the **50 to 200B token regime**, the same regime as SYNTH/Baguettotron (200B) and Instruction Pre-Training (100B), not the 2T to 10T regime of SmolLM2, Gemma 3 270M and LFM2. That makes data efficiency the main lever, and it means density of the target skill in the data matters more than raw diversity.

### 2.2 A data recipe hypothesis to test (not a validated recipe)

Every share below is a starting point for small-scale ablation, derived from the sources above.

| Component | Share of ~100B tokens | Why | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| General text backbone: FineWeb-Edu + DCLM (SmolLM2-style, DCLM filtered by FineWeb-Edu score) + some Cosmopedia v2 | 45 to 55% | Language, commonsense, diversity; TII kept 20% web for commonsense and found 6% low-quality data prevents overfitting to repeated HQ data | Moderate |
| Chat/SFT-format dialogue from existing open sets (smol-smoltalk, SmolTalk subsets, UltraChat, SODA, OASST2, WildChat user turns with shortened assistant turns, Everyday-Conversations), deduped, with repetitive n-grams filtered | 15 to 20% | Anti-curriculum; puts the target distribution in pretraining; SODA-style social dialogue covers casual chat | Moderate (90M IFEval result; no multi-turn metric) |
| Targeted synthetic multi-turn set: back-references, ellipsis, corrections ("no, the other one"), instruction persistence, topic return, plus grounded dialogues where the answer is in a provided passage or earlier turn, plus explicit "I don't know" when it is not | 5 to 10% (0.3 to 1B unique tokens, repeated 10 to 30 times) | The gap no public set fills; spaced repetition makes a small set go far | Speculative at 150M (Parrot evidence is at 7B+) |
| Code and math | 10 to 15% | Up to 2x effective tokens (Muennighoff); state tracking (see mechanics lane) | Moderate |
| SYNTH (optional, no-think or trace-stripped segments) | 0 to 10% | Dense grounded QA and negative queries; proven at 56M to 321M on benchmarks | Weak for chat |
| Decay phase | last 10 to 20% of tokens | Upweight the best dialogue sets and the targeted set (MiniCPM, Dolmino) | Moderate at 1B+, untested at 150M |

Register rules for all dialogue data: short assistant turns (tens of tokens, not 300), simple vocabulary for the knowledge-light portion, one chat template used identically in pretraining and SFT, conversations packed without cross-document attention, and role tokens trained from step 0.

### 2.3 What to measure

MT-Bench and IFEval will not show multi-turn coherence. The data ablations need a cheap, automatic multi-turn probe: synthetic conversations where turn k refers back to a fact, entity or instruction from turn k-d, scored by exact match or likelihood, reported as accuracy versus back-reference distance d. Run it on the base model too: if anti-curriculum works, the base model should already hold the thread.

## 3. Evidence about the "wall" (why chat quality degrades below ~400M)

From the data side, the wall looks like three separate walls that get blamed on one thing:

1. **A knowledge wall that is real and parameter-bound.** 2 bits/param at best, 1 bit/param at 100 exposures, 20x worse with 1:7 junk dilution ([arXiv 2404.05405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05405)); TriviaQA 4.1 at 135M after 2T tokens ([card](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-135M-Instruct)); Max's MaxGPT-2 photosynthesis answer. Users experience wrong facts as "it can't chat". No data recipe removes this; only offloading does.
2. **A distribution wall that is mostly a recipe choice.** Web is about 2.7 to 3.7% conversational ([arXiv 2508.10975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10975)), education filters push that lower, and most small models see chat only in a short final SFT. Every controlled comparison found moves chat-format data earlier: 90M anti-curriculum IFEval 53.47 to 66.08 ([TII](https://tiiuae-tiny-h1-blogpost.hf.space/)); MiniCPM decay vs post-hoc SFT ([arXiv 2404.06395](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.06395)); mid-training gains scale with proximity to the target at 70M to 1B ([arXiv 2510.14865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14865)); Instruction Pre-Training 500M/100B matching Pythia-1B/300B ([arXiv 2406.14491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14491)). Meena's R^2 = 0.93 between perplexity and human-judged sensibleness ([arXiv 2001.09977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09977)) says conversational quality follows loss on conversational text, which a model cannot minimize on text it never saw.
3. **A multi-turn data wall that nobody has attacked at this size.** Small-model SFT sets average about 2.3 turns ([smol-smoltalk stats](https://datasets-server.huggingface.co/statistics?dataset=HuggingFaceTB/smol-smoltalk&config=default&split=train)); synthetic users rarely refer back (UltraChat 1.45 context-dependent queries per session vs ShareGPT 4.62, [arXiv 2310.07301](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.07301v2)); smaller generators lose coherence past 6 turns ([arXiv 2505.19978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19978)). A 150M model trained on this has almost no gradient signal for cross-turn reference. Whether it could learn that skill given dense data is untested; the mechanics lane suggests the needed circuits exist at 160M but state tracking crosses human level only around 410M on Pile-era data.

Counter-evidence that the wall is not purely about size:
- Falcon-H1-Tiny-90M MT-Bench 4.33 vs Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct 4.16 (different harnesses) ([TII](https://tiiuae-tiny-h1-blogpost.hf.space/), [SmolLM2 card](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-360M-Instruct)).
- DialoGPT 345M near-human on single-turn Reddit replies in 2019 ([arXiv 1911.00536](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00536)); BlenderBot 90M vs 2.7B self-chat engagingness 43:57, not significant ([arXiv 2004.13637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13637)).
- SmolLM "Hi" and "Who are you" failures at 135M/360M/1.7B fixed by 2.2k targeted conversations ([card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceTB/everyday-conversations-llama3.1-2k)).
- Baguettotron 321M at 200B synthetic tokens beating SmolLM2-360M at 4T on MMLU / GSM8K / HotPotQA (vendor, [card](https://huggingface.co/PleIAs/Baguettotron)).

Evidence that the wall is real regardless of data:
- Knowledge capacity, above.
- Degeneration loops are more visible in small models and are fed by repetitive data ([arXiv 2310.10226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10226)); Max hit them at 110M and 235M.
- Frontier models also lose 39% going multi-turn ([arXiv 2505.06120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120)), so some of what looks like a small-model wall is a multi-turn wall at every scale.

## 5. Open questions

1. Does anti-curriculum improve **multi-turn** coherence (back-reference accuracy versus turn distance), or only single-turn instruction following? No published measurement at any size below 1B.
2. Is 150M multi-turn failure data-limited or capacity-limited? Test: two 150M runs at equal tokens, one with the targeted back-reference set and one without, scored on the automatic back-reference probe. If the curve moves, it is data.
3. What is the right chat share for a 50 to 200B-token budget? TII chose 25% at 800B; BeyondWeb saw benchmark gains saturate at 20% conversational at 1B/20B. Neither measured multi-turn quality.
4. Does removing low-value factual text (knowledge-light pretraining) free capacity for conversational skill at 150M? Physics 3.3 predicts junk crowds out knowledge; nobody has tested the reverse effect on skill.
5. How short should assistant turns be for a 150M student, and does a smaller teacher (4B) produce better training targets than a larger one (9B+) for this student, as the small-student CoT result suggests for math?
6. Can a 4B to 9B teacher on the Mac generate coherent 8 to 12 turn dialogues with real back-references, or does it need cross-model pairing and turn-by-turn generation to avoid the 6-turn coherence cliff?
7. Is the 49,152-token vocabulary right for a narrow-register chat model? Monad cut embeddings from about 20M to 2M with an 8k tokenizer trained on its own corpus; at d=576, Max's vocab costs about 28M of 150M parameters (derived).
8. Does SYNTH's reasoning-trace style help a chat model or pull it toward a benchmark register? Baguettotron degrades without thinking; Monad has no multi-turn.
9. Can the idle TITAN RTX cards generate data without thermal throttling the Ultra run, and what vLLM throughput does sm_75 give for a 4B AWQ teacher? Unsourced; needs a measurement that does not touch the running job.
10. Harness consistency: vendor IFEval and MT-Bench numbers disagree across harnesses (Gemma 3 270M IFEval 51.2 vs 27.44). Max will need his own fixed harness before comparing against any published small model.
