# Lane: post-training a tiny model into a multi-turn conversationalist

Date: 23 Sep 2026. Scope: SFT, preference tuning, distillation, RL, multi-turn methods, degeneration fixes, persona consistency and decoding, judged by what they do for a ~150M decoder. Every number carries its source and the scale it was measured at. "(>=1B only)" flags evidence that does not exist below 1B. Vendor numbers are vendor numbers: harnesses differ (for example Gemma-3-270m-it scores IFEval 51.2 on Google's card but 27.44 on TII's harness), so compare within one table, never across tables.

---

## Bottom line

1. The premise is half wrong. Sub-400M instruct models already match or beat 0.5-0.8B models on published instruction-following benchmarks and on some multi-turn ones: SmolLM2-360M-Instruct (IFEval 41.0 vs Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct 31.6, though MT-Bench 3.66 vs 4.16), LFM2.5-230M (IFEval 71.7 vs Qwen3.5-0.8B 59.9), LFM2.5-350M (Multi-IF 44.9 vs Qwen3.5-0.8B 41.7), and a 90M model, Falcon-H1-Tiny-90M-Instruct (IFEval 66.1, MT-Bench-101 4.33, above SmolLM2-360M-Instruct's 3.8 on the same harness). Casual chit-chat at ~90M has been human-rated as competitive since BlenderBot (2020).
2. What those models spent to get there: 0.8T to 28T pretraining tokens (about 8,900 to 83,000 tokens per parameter), logit distillation from 7B to 235B teachers, millions of SFT rows, on-policy preference data, and in the newest ones multi-stage RL. The "wall" is mostly a compute, teacher and recipe wall, not a parameter wall. Max's 100B-token budget is 8x below even the 90M Falcon run.
3. What still lags at sub-400M, even in the best recipes, is exactly Max's target. Multi-turn instruction retention (LFM2-350M ties Qwen3-0.6B on IFEval, 65.1 vs 64.2, but trails on Multi-IF, 32.9 vs 45.1), factual knowledge, and degeneration. In Max's own Daimax bench SmolLM2-360M looped in 4 of 33 answers at T=0.7 against 0 of 33 for Qwen2.5-0.5B, with similar pass rates. Loops, not raw accuracy, are the visible boundary.
4. The strongest post-training lever with sub-1B evidence is on-policy distillation: the student generates, a teacher scores every token (reverse KL). It beat SFT and off-policy KD for 77M to 800M T5 students (GKD) and for 120M GPT-2 (MiniLLM: GPT-4 score 38.6 SFT to 44.7), stopped exposure-bias error from accumulating, and at 8B beat RL at one tenth the GPU hours (Qwen3). Its multi-turn form (student rollouts on its own "dirty" histories) directly targets the compounding-error mechanism behind multi-turn failure, but multi-turn OPD evidence exists only at >=1.7B.
5. The second strongest lever is moving chat data earlier. Falcon-H1-Tiny put 25% SFT data into the whole 800B-token pretraining run and got about +10 IFEval over classic pretrain-then-SFT at 90M. SmolLM3 (3B) showed single-turn SFT data does not generalize to multi-turn behavior, so the chat data must itself be multi-turn.
6. Preference tuning helps at 90M (Falcon-H1-Tiny DPO: IFEval +12.7 to +16.0, MT-Bench-101 +1.2), but only for 1 epoch (more epochs degraded the model while DPO reward kept rising), and the choice of loss barely matters (about 1 point; at 0.5B SFT and DPO tie at 33.97 on GSM8K, SimPO drops to 26.08). RL with verifiable rewards is the weakest lever below 1B (TinyZero 0.5B fails; nanochat 561M GSM8K 4.55 to 7.58; OXRL 0.5B online RL at or below SFT for 46x the cost).
7. For Max specifically, the binding post-training decision is the teacher, and it is a tokenizer decision: MaxGPT-Ultra (1.1B, same 49k tokenizer) is a free same-tokenizer teacher once it is post-trained, while strong open teachers (Qwen3, LFM2.5) need cross-tokenizer distillation (GOLD, byte-prefix marginalization), which is proven only for >=1B students.
8. Diagnosis for the next phase: what limits a ~150M chat model after post-training is (a) exposure bias compounding across turns, (b) a learnability gap that makes teacher-quality multi-turn transcripts partly unimitable, (c) capacity-driven "errors in learning" that show up as loops, (d) knowledge capacity, which no post-training method adds. (a) to (c) are attackable with on-policy, student-sized, multi-turn training; (d) needs retrieval.

Reading: on benchmarks, 90M to 350M models reach or pass the 0.5-0.6B tier on instruction following. Every one with published chat or IF numbers reached it with a pretraining budget (0.8T+ tokens) and, in the strongest cases, teacher access far beyond a hobby run. The gaps that remain are named by the vendors themselves: knowledge-intensive tasks, creative writing, "complex conversational use", and multi-turn (TII's future-work note: "across small models, the multi-turn scores were relatively low compared to Qwen-0.6B").

Max's own measurement agrees with this picture: on his Daimax harness (programmatic grading, temperature 0.7, 3-run means) SmolLM2-360M scored pass 0.536 vs Qwen2.5-0.5B 0.582 vs Qwen3-0.6B 0.668, but looped in 4/33 answers vs 0/33 and 1/33 (`~/Documents/Projects/daimax/docs/BENCHMARK.md`, section "2026-09-03b"). Single-run differences under ~0.10 were noise on that harness.

### 2. SFT recipes and data for <=360M models

**SmolLM2-135M/360M (scale: 135M on 2T, 360M on 4T).** SFT on smol-smoltalk, a SmolTalk subset "adapted for smol models with less than 1B parameters": shorter Smol-Magpie-Ultra conversations, no function calling, less rewriting and summarization, no advanced math, 460,341 train conversations (https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceTB/smol-smoltalk). The paper states hard MagPie-Ultra examples and complex instruction following were removed "to better align with the models' capacity" (https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02737v1). Hyperparameters from the official recipe: 2 epochs, lr 1.0e-3 (comment: "3e-4"), cosine, warmup 0.1, seq 8192, global batch 128; DPO on UltraFeedback, beta 0.5, lr 1e-6, 2 epochs, max length 1024 (https://github.com/huggingface/alignment-handbook/tree/main/recipes/smollm2). Token count of smol-smoltalk is not published; its 1.8 GB text size suggests roughly 0.4-0.5B tokens (my estimate, unsourced).

**What small models could and could not absorb (SmolTalk and SmolTalk2).** SmolTalk2's non-reasoning SFT subsets carry the multi-turn material: smol-magpie-ultra (406,843 rows, 6 messages average, 619M tokens), systemchats-30k (33,997 rows, 6.27 messages average), everyday-conversations (2,260 rows, 7.75 messages average), plus a Multi-IF-style multi-turn IF set only in the reasoning ("think") half (https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceTB/smoltalk2). For the small models HF dropped tool calling, advanced math and long samples; no published ablation measures what 135M lost by keeping them, so "could not absorb" is HF's judgment, not a measured curve. The learnability-gap literature supports the direction: models <=3B learn better from shorter, simpler targets than from long teacher chains (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12143, >=0.5B to 3B).

**LFM2 (scale: 350M to 2.6B share one recipe).** 5.39M SFT samples from 67 sources: general 26.6%, instruction following 17.1%, RAG 13.2%, real-world chats 10.1%, tool use 10.1%, chosen responses from preference data 8.2%, math 7.4%, long context 6.7%. Filtering by LLM-jury quality scores, refusal and cliche filters, and semantic dedup ("most impactful"). Difficulty curriculum: 12 models from 350M to 235B answer each item; items are ordered from most-solved to least-solved. Training: 3 epochs, 32k context, lr 3e-5 cosine to 1e-7, AdamW, 10% embedding input dropout (https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23404v1).

**How many SFT tokens a tiny model can use (scale: 90M).** Falcon-H1-Tiny swept SFT duration after an 800B-token pretraining run: 2, 4, 16, 32 GT at lr 128e-4 to 512e-4. The model "continues to learn up to" about 10 GT of SFT and plateaus after; they shipped the 10 GT checkpoint (https://tiiuae-tiny-h1-blogpost.hf.space/). That is one to two orders of magnitude more SFT than Max's current 108k-row set. Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct used over 1M SFT examples for 2 epochs, 150k offline DPO pairs and online GRPO with a reward model, on an 18T-token base (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.15115).

**Anti-curriculum: put SFT data into pretraining (scale: 90M, 100B-800B tokens).** Falcon-H1-Tiny compared classic SFT after pretraining with "SFT-pretraining" (25% SFT data in the whole 800 GT run). IFEval: classic 40.77, SFT-pretrain 50.11; after DPO 53.47 vs 66.08. In a 100 GT sweep with 0/25/50/75/100% SFT data, 25% and 50% gave the best instruction following and 25% kept base-model scores; mostly-SFT mixes were worse overall, "suggesting that pretraining data is essential to develop even instruction-following capabilities". No memorization artifacts even with 2 GT SFT epochs repeated; an extra SFT stage on top gave "no clear signal". Their rationale: a memorization window that scales down with model size (~5 GT extrapolated for 100M), so a small SFT set can be repeated many times (>=100 repetitions of Tulu 3 sources) without overfitting (https://tiiuae-tiny-h1-blogpost.hf.space/). Consistent evidence: Instruction Pre-Training's 500M model on 100B tokens matched Pythia-1B on 300B (https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14491); MiniCPM's SFT-in-decay results in Max's prior sweep (`maxgpt-ultra/docs/research_2026-09-22.md`, section 2).

**Loss masking and templates.** Assistant-only loss gave "small but consistent" gains, mainly IFEval, at 3B (https://huggingface.co/blog/smollm3, >=1B only). The opposite result, loss on instructions helping, holds when instructions are long and outputs short or data is tiny, measured at 7B-13B (https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14394, >=1B only). No sub-1B masking ablation was found. Packing: 3-5x throughput, but at the same effective batch, packing hurt IFEval once batch >32 on a small SFT set (SmolLM3 playbook, 3B, https://huggingfacetb-smol-training-playbook.hf.space/). Max's current `posttrain/sft_data.py` masks correctly but packs without intra-document attention masking; the prior sweep already flagged intra-document masking as a correctness issue in SFT.

**Single-turn data does not produce multi-turn behavior (scale: 3B).** SmolLM3's hybrid baseline, trained mostly on single-turn data, failed "abysmally" to keep the requested mode past the first turn; adding IFThink (Tulu 3 IF prompts expanded into 3-turn exchanges by Qwen3-32B) fixed it: "it stayed consistent across turns, followed instructions" (SmolLM3 playbook, 3B). Also from that playbook: a formatting bug made the model ignore system-prompt personas and "randomly switch character mid-conversation" while evals looked fine; their rule is to vibe-test every checkpoint.

**Forgetting and alignment tax at small scale.** Base to instruct, same model: SmolLM2-360M ARC 53.0 to 43.7, HellaSwag 54.5 to 52.1; SmolLM2-135M ARC 43.9 to 37.3 (model cards above). OLMo-1B pretrained on 3T tokens was over 2% worse after instruction tuning than its 2.3T checkpoint ("catastrophic overtraining", https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19206, 1B). Mitigations with evidence: pretraining-data replay (1.87x target-data efficiency, in the prior sweep), weight-space soups toward the base, and on-policy distillation, which restored Qwen3-8B IFEval from 45% to 83% after domain mid-training (https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/, 8B).

**Historical note on "instruction tuning hurts small models".** FLAN (2021) found instruction tuning hurt held-out tasks for models of 8B and smaller, tested at 422M to 137B (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.01652). Flan-T5 later improved even T5-Small (80M) with a better mixture (normalized average -9.2 to -3.1, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.11416). Recipe, not size, set that early wall.

### 3. Preference tuning at small scale

**It helps at 90M, for one epoch (scale: 90M).** Falcon-H1-Tiny DPO: IFEval 40.77 to 53.47 (classic SFT) and 50.11 to 66.08 (SFT-pretrain); MT-Bench-101 3.17 to 4.40 and 3.08 to 4.33; AlpacaEval win 3.45 to 10.44. Running DPO for more than 1 epoch "led to a considerable performance degradation straight after the first epoch, despite the DPO reward being increased during the entire training run"; they used lr 1e-6 or 3e-6, 1 epoch, cosine (https://tiiuae-tiny-h1-blogpost.hf.space/). TII also reports that a small DPO stage helped at every scale they train, 0.5B to 34B.

**Reward-model-judged evidence at 125M to 1.3B.** ORPO paper, OPT students, win rates judged by a 1.3B reward model: on HH-RLHF, ORPO beat SFT 84.0% at 125M and 82.7% at 350M, but lost to DPO at 125M (41.7%) and roughly tied at 350M (49.4%) (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.07691). So preference optimization of either kind beats SFT alone at 125M; the specific loss is secondary.

**The loss function is the lowest-leverage choice.** A controlled study (8 algorithms, 4 scales, 20 DPO variants x 5 seeds at 1.5B) decomposes variance as scale ~50 pp, training paradigm ~10 pp, online vs offline ~9 pp, loss ~1 pp; 0 of 20 DPO variants beat vanilla DPO after correction. At 0.5B (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, GSM8K): base 19.56, SFT 33.97, DPO 33.97, IPO 34.50, KTO 33.81, SimPO 26.08, online SGRPO 32.45 at 46x the cost (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19335). KTO vs DPO showed no significant difference among Pythia 1.4B-12B, "suggesting that a minimum model capacity is needed for these differences to emerge" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01306). Reference-free SimPO is the one variant that is clearly worse at small scale.

**Production recipes at small scale.** SmolLM2 small: vanilla DPO, beta 0.5, lr 1e-6, 2 epochs, UltraFeedback. LFM2 (350M+): ~1M prompts, N=5 on-policy samples from the SFT model plus reference responses, scored by an LLM jury, best vs worst as the pair, chosen responses for IF refined with CLAIR, ~700k pairs; length-normalized DPO/APO-zero family, beta 5.0, lr 8e-7, 2 epochs, context 1024 (https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23404v1). SmolLM3 (3B): APO-zero improved IFEval over SFT by 15-20 points; best preference lr about 10x below SFT lr; results were stable from 2k to 340k pairs, and small sets still beat the SFT checkpoint (playbook, >=1B only). "Strong vs weak" pairs (chosen from Qwen3-32B, rejected from Qwen3-0.6B) are free to generate locally.

**Turing numerics (Max-specific, from the prior sweep).** DPO's signal is a difference of log-prob differences; under fp16 autocast on sm_75 it can drown in rounding. Precompute reference log-probs and accumulate the loss in fp32 (`maxgpt-ultra/docs/research_2026-09-22.md`, section 3.2).

### 4. Distillation: the lever with the best sub-1B evidence

**On-policy distillation (student samples, teacher scores each token).**
- GKD (ICLR 2024): T5-Small 77M, T5-Base 250M, T5-Large 800M students, T5-XL 3B teacher. Averaged across student sizes, on-policy GKD's gains over the initial student were 2.1x (summarization), 1.7x (translation) and 1.9x (arithmetic) the gains of baseline KD methods; task-agnostic instruction distillation gave +2% BBH and +1% MMLU (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13649).
- MiniLLM (ICLR 2024): reverse-KL on-policy distillation, GPT-2 1.5B teacher. At 120M, GPT-4-judged score on DollyEval: SFT 38.6, KD 40.3, SeqKD 41.2, MiniLLM 44.7; Rouge-L on S-NI 16.3 to 25.3. At 125M the excess accumulated error (exposure bias) of SFT/KD baselines "continuously grow[s] during generation" while MiniLLM's "stops accumulating in long-text generation (> 150 tokens)" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.08543).
- Qwen3 (>=1B for the compute comparison): all small Qwen3 models including 0.6B were built by off-policy then on-policy distillation from Qwen3-32B/235B. At 8B from the same checkpoint: RL reached AIME'24 67.6 at 17,920 GPU hours; on-policy distillation reached 74.4 at 1,800, and raised pass@64 where RL did not (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.09388).
- Thinking Machines (Oct 2025, 8B): per-token reverse KL with discount zero, teacher log-probs on student samples; reaches teacher level "7-10x faster than RL", 9-30x cheaper than SFT on 2M prompts; restores IFEval after domain training (https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/).
- Rethinking OPD (Apr 2026, 1.5B-7B only): OPD works only if teacher and student share "thinking patterns" and the teacher has knowledge the student lacks; a stronger teacher "can completely fail to improve a student, even when a weaker teacher succeeds"; an off-policy SFT cold start on teacher outputs fixes pattern mismatch; top-16 or sampled-token (k=1) losses work, top-1 greedy fails; teacher signal degrades with trajectory depth (https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13016v1).
- Teacher hacking: with fixed offline data the student over-fits an imperfect teacher; online data and diversity prevent it (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02671).

**Multi-turn OPD (>=1.7B only).** SMOPD (Aug 2026) names the core multi-turn problem: "once a student emits an erroneous intermediate reply, later turns are conditioned on that reply". Dropping the 20% lowest-entropy tokens of middle turns from the loss gave +1.0 to +2.5 pp on Lost-in-Conversation sharded accuracy (Qwen3 1.7B/4B/8B; +1.7 pp mean at 4B, p=0.022; neutral at 1.7B) (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14647). Found in Conversation (May 2026, 3B-14B): self-distillation where the teacher sees the single-turn fully specified view and the student sees the multi-turn sharded view recovers "at least 92% of single-turn performance" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24432).

**Teacher size and the capacity gap.** Distillation scaling laws (students 143M-12.6B): with an existing teacher, distillation beats supervised training up to a compute level that grows with student size; a too-strong teacher can produce a worse student (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08606). MiniMA's fitted law for <3B LMs: optimal teacher size is about 2.5x the student (https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07052). A 1.1B teacher for a 150M student is about 7x, past that sweet spot; whether this matters for on-policy (mode-seeking) distillation is untested at this scale.

**The tokenizer constraint.** Standard OPD needs identical tokenizers. GOLD (HF, Oct 2025) extends it across tokenizers by merging token spans and mapping vocabularies; on Countdown with >=1B students, same-tokenizer KD beat GRPO about 2x and cross-tokenizer GOLD still beat GRPO by about 20%; outcome tracked vocabulary similarity (Llama-3.2-1B mapped similarity to Qwen 0.64 worked; Gemma-3-1B at 0.063 was the weakest student); in the same-tokenizer case the student recovered over 80% of teacher performance (https://huggingface.co/spaces/HuggingFaceH4/on-policy-distillation). Byte-prefix marginalization (Jul 2026) re-expresses teacher distributions in the student vocabulary at the byte level, +3.7 to +6.6 avg@8 over the strongest baselines with Qwen3-32B and other teachers (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22334; student sizes not stated in the abstract). Max's options: (a) MaxGPT-Ultra 1.1B shares his 49,152 byte-level BPE, so same-tokenizer OPD needs zero alignment code, but its chat quality after 100B tokens is unknown; (b) a strong open teacher through GOLD/BPM (unproven below 1B); (c) adopt a teacher family's tokenizer. Qwen (151,936) and Gemma (262,144) vocabularies are too large for 150M (Qwen's alone would be ~87M parameters at d=576, computed from the configs); LFM2's 65,536 vocabulary (teachers LFM2/LFM2.5 up to 2.6B) is the only strong family found with a small-model-sized vocabulary (config.json of each model).

**Off-policy (sequence-level) distillation is what open SFT data already is.** SmolTalk (Llama-3.1-405B, Qwen2.5-72B generations), SmolTalk2 (Qwen3-32B/235B) and UltraChat are teacher outputs. MiniLLM's 120M table shows this SeqKD gives only +2.6 over SFT where on-policy gives +6.1.

### 5. RL below 1B

- Qwen2.5-0.5B base "fails to learn reasoning" on Countdown with R1-Zero-style RL; 3B+ succeeds (https://github.com/Jiayi-Pan/TinyZero).
- nanochat d20 (561M, 11.2B tokens): GSM8K 2.50 (mid-train) to 4.55 (SFT) to 7.58 (simplified GRPO/REINFORCE); ChatCORE 0.0730 to 0.0884 through SFT (https://github.com/karpathy/nanochat/discussions/1).
- OXRL at 0.5B: online RL 32.45 vs SFT/DPO 33.97 at 46x cost; at 1.5B online RL wins (58.0 vs 54.4) because "the model generates sufficient correct solutions" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19335).
- Label-free RL "often degrad[es] below baseline levels for weaker models" (0.5B-7B, https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04902).
- MobileLLM-R1 (140M/360M/950M) used SFT only: Tulu-3 SFT 866k rows then 6.2M reasoning rows; same recipe, 140M vs 360M: MATH 6.2 vs 28.4, GSM8K 4.1 vs 24.5 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.24945).
- Multi-turn RL with GPT-2-class policies exists for games and dialogue tasks (LMRL-Gym: all experiments use GPT-2 sizes, https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18232; ArCHer: GPT-2 actor beats prompted GPT-3.5 on WebShop, https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19446), but not for open-ended chat quality.
- The only sub-400M chat models that claim RL (LFM2.5-230M/350M) publish no RL details (https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm2-5-230m).

Verdict: RL rewards what the model can already sometimes do. A 150M model rarely produces the target behavior unaided, so dense per-token teacher signal (OPD) beats sparse reward. RL is a late polish at best.

### 6. Multi-turn specific evidence

- Lost in Conversation (ICLR 2026 outstanding paper, smallest model tested Llama-3.1-8B): sharding a fully specified task across turns drops performance 39% on average; decomposition: aptitude -15%, unreliability +112%. Concatenating the shards into one turn keeps 95.1% of performance, so the loss is about turn structure, not information. Models "make assumptions in early turns and prematurely attempt to generate final solutions, on which they overly rely". Temperature 0 does not fix it; recap-style agent fixes recover only 15-20%. Smaller models (8B, 13B, Haiku) showed larger paraphrase sensitivity, keeping 86-92% in the concatenated setting (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06120, >=8B only).
- Fixes so far, all >=1B: RLAAR curriculum RL with accuracy and abstention rewards, a reported multi-turn performance figure of 62.6% raised to 75.1% (https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18731, sizes not stated in abstract); CollabLLM multiturn-aware rewards from forward-simulated users, +18.5% task performance, +46.3% interactivity (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00640); FiC and SMOPD above.
- Multi-IF (3 turns, verifiable constraints): accuracy falls every turn even for o1-preview (0.877 turn 1 to 0.707 turn 3) (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15553). This is the benchmark where sub-400M models trail most (section 1).
- MT-Bench-101 (13 multi-turn tasks, models 6B+): "Model performance improves as the model size increases", but "neither utilizing common alignment techniques nor chat-specific designs has led to obvious enhancements in the multi-turn abilities"; on context memory and anaphora tasks scores fall after the first turn. Caution: it feeds the golden history, not the model's own earlier replies, so it cannot see error compounding (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14762).
- Instruction and persona drift appears "within eight rounds" even in LLaMA2-chat-70B and GPT-3.5, attributed to attention decay toward the system prompt; split-softmax helps (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10962, >=70B only).
- BlenderBot's own failure analysis (90M to 9.4B): the bots "contradict or repeat themselves on occasion", show "forgetfulness", and 14-turn evaluations understate long-conversation problems (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.13637).

### 7. Degeneration and repetition

- Max's history: MaxGPT-2 (110M) "Bitcoin.com - Bitcoin.com" loops, patched with repetition_penalty 1.2 (`~/Documents/Projects/Max's AI Model/WRITEUP_NOTES.md`); Daimax SmolLM2-360M loops 4/33 at T=0.7, measured with a repeated-5-gram fraction (0.914 on a looping transcript vs 0.000 on prose).
- Loops scale inversely with capacity: at low temperature 76% looping for Qwen-1.5B vs 37% for Qwen-32B reasoning models; a distilled student loops far more than its teacher (OpenThinker-3 1.5B 30% vs QwQ-32B 4%). Cause: "errors in learning", where a hard-to-learn correct action loses probability to an easy cyclic one; higher temperature is "a stopgap rather than a holistic solution" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12895, ICML 2026, 1.5B-32B). LFM2.5's card reports Qwen3.5-0.8B Thinking could not finish MMLU-Pro "due to doom looping" (https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-350M).
- Training-data repetition is a root cause; penalizing repetitions in training data is the common factor behind unlikelihood, DITTO and similar fixes (https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10226). Self-reinforcement: each repeat makes the next more likely (DITTO, 750M on Wikitext-103, https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02369).
- Unlikelihood training in a small dialogue model (8-layer encoder + 8-layer decoder, 512-d, BlenderBot-90M class; exact count not stated): ELI5 label repetition 0.617 to 0.055 with F1 0.130 to 0.183 and ppl 21.0 to 21.4; contradiction handling (Dialogue NLI), preferring a neutral over a contradicting reply 18% to 78% with ConvAI2 ppl 11.4 to 11.9 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.03860). In BlenderBot, unlikelihood was preferred to MLE 54-46 on engagingness at 2.7B (no significance marker reported).

### 8. Persona and self-consistency

- Grounding the model in a persona context gave a small engagingness win in BlenderBot and is the standard way PersonaChat-style bots stay consistent (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.13637).
- Unlikelihood against Dialogue-NLI contradictions (above) is the only sub-100M trained-in consistency fix found with numbers.
- Context distillation trains the model to behave as if a prompt were present when it is absent (+9% over plain gradient descent on SPIDER; model size not stated in the abstract, https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15189). For a small model that attends weakly to distant tokens, baking the persona into weights removes a long-range dependency. Untested for persona at small scale.
- DECODE provides human-written contradiction data for training a contradiction detector usable as a reranker or reward (https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13391).

### 9. Decoding for small models

- Vendor defaults for tiny chat models are conservative: LFM2.5-230M recommends temperature 0.1, top_k 50, repetition_penalty 1.05 (model card). The looping paper warns low temperature raises loop rates, so a repetition penalty or n-gram blocking must accompany low temperature.
- Contrastive search relies on isotropic representations; "the anisotropic problem only exists in the two specific English GPT-2-small/medium models", which are the small ones (https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14140).
- Contrastive decoding needs an amateur much smaller than the expert, or the expert itself with a truncated context window (conditioning the amateur only on the last token makes CD reward coherence) (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.15097, GPT-2 XL/OPT-13B experts).
- Context-aware decoding (contrast the output with vs without context) gave consistent faithfulness gains on OPT from 125M to 30B (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14739). Using "with full history" vs "with the last user turn only" as the two contexts is an untested but cheap way to force a small model to use earlier turns.
- Min-p's claimed quality and diversity gains did not survive reanalysis of the original human and benchmark evidence (https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13681).
- BlenderBot at 2.7B: forcing a minimum beam length of 20 gave large, significant engagingness gains (83-17 vs no minimum); 3-gram blocking of context and response scored best among blocking variants but not significantly (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.13637).

## What this implies for a ~150M chat model

A concrete post-training plan, ordered by evidence strength. Items marked (spec) are speculative.

1. **Move multi-turn chat data into pretraining or the decay.** Target 10-25% SFT-style data by tokens, most of it multi-turn (smol-magpie-ultra, systemchats, everyday-conversations, OASST threads, UltraChat, the SmolTalk2 multi-turn IF prompts regenerated without reasoning). Evidence: 90M Falcon (+10 IFEval), 500M Instruction Pre-Training. Coordinate with the pretraining lane, since this changes the mixture.
2. **SFT that fits a 150M student.** Short to medium responses, 3 or more exchanges per conversation, explicit back-references ("as I said earlier", entity carried from turn 1 to turn 4), no tool calling, no long CoT, no long context; difficulty ordering; semantic dedup and repetition filtering. Assistant-only loss on every assistant turn, intra-document attention masking when packing, 10% pretraining replay, NorMuon to match pretraining. Budget: sweep 0.5 to 5B tokens; Falcon's 90M kept improving to ~10B.
3. **On-policy distillation on multi-turn prompts.** The student writes every assistant turn itself, so later turns are conditioned on its own imperfect history, and the teacher scores every token (reverse KL, sampled-token or top-16). Start with an off-policy cold start on teacher outputs if patterns differ. Default teacher: MaxGPT-Ultra after its SFT/DPO (same tokenizer, available in ~70 days). Compute is trivial: 100k conversations x 1k tokens is about 3e17 FLOPs including teacher forwards (my estimate), hours on one Titan.
4. **One epoch of on-policy preference tuning.** Sample 4-8 replies per prompt from the student; choose pairs with a local judge (teacher log-likelihood, or a small open reward model such as Skywork-Reward-V2-Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B, https://huggingface.co/Skywork) plus programmatic vetoes: repeated-5-gram loop detector, back-reference correctness, persona-contradiction check. Length-normalized DPO or APO-zero, lr about 10x below SFT, 1 epoch, fp32 log-probs on Turing. Keep the SFT checkpoint and soup toward it.
5. **Trained-in anti-degeneration.** Unlikelihood on repeated n-grams in the student's own samples, and on contradiction pairs (Dialogue NLI/DECODE-style) (spec at decoder scale, measured on a 90M-class seq2seq).
6. **Decoding defaults.** Temperature 0.3-0.7, top-k 40-50, repetition penalty 1.05-1.15 or 3-gram blocking, never pure greedy. Optionally CAD-style self-contrast between full history and last turn (spec).
7. **Skip for now:** long chain-of-thought or thinking modes (loops, learnability gap), SimPO and other reference-free losses, multi-epoch DPO, GRPO on math as a chat lever, LLM-judged MT-Bench as the main metric.
8. **Evaluation that can see the failure.** Programmatic, own-history (not golden-history) multi-turn probes: a planted-fact recall test at turn distance 1-8, Multi-IF, a loop rate (repeated 5-gram fraction at T=0.7 and T=0.1), persona hold across 8+ turns, IFEval. Three runs per checkpoint to learn the noise floor (Daimax saw 0.57 vs 0.75 on identical configs).

Realistic ceiling: with ~100B pretraining tokens a 150M model will not match LFM2.5-230M (19T) on knowledge. It can plausibly match SmolLM2-135M/360M-Instruct on IF and multi-turn probes if post-training is on-policy and multi-turn, because those models' post-training was off-policy SFT plus 2-epoch UltraFeedback DPO. Beating them on loop rate and back-reference retention is the realistic "new" result, and it is measurable.

## Evidence about the "wall" (why chat quality degrades below ~400M)

1. **Single-turn instruction following has no wall at 90-350M.** IFEval 66-77 at 90-350M vs 60-64 for Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3.5-0.8B on vendor harnesses (LFM2, LFM2.5, Falcon-H1-Tiny sources above).
2. **Multi-turn retention does show a size gradient.** LFM2 family, one recipe: Multi-IF 32.85 (350M), 40.92 (700M), 45.28 (1.2B). LFM2-350M equals Qwen3-0.6B on IFEval but trails it by 12 points on Multi-IF. Closing it took 28T tokens plus multi-stage RL (LFM2.5-350M, 44.92).
3. **Degeneration is capacity-sensitive.** Loops fall with capacity (76% vs 37% at 1.5B vs 32B), distilled small students loop more than teachers, and Max measured 4/33 loops for SmolLM2-360M vs 0/33 for Qwen2.5-0.5B. Loops read to a user as "cannot chat" even when pass rates are close.
4. **Exposure bias compounds.** At 125M, SFT and KD students accumulate error with generation length while on-policy training stops the accumulation (MiniLLM). Multi-turn chat is a long generation in which the model's own earlier replies become context; SMOPD calls this "dirty history". Every open small-model recipe (SmolLM2, MobileLLM-R1, nanochat) is off-policy SFT plus at most offline DPO.
5. **Learnability gap.** HF had to strip hard, long and tool-use samples from SmolTalk for 135M/360M; models <=3B learn better from shorter chains; capacity-gap results show too-strong teachers can hurt. Teacher-quality multi-turn transcripts are partly unimitable for a 150M model, so imitation spends capacity on style it cannot reproduce.
6. **Knowledge is not a post-training variable.** MMLU-Pro sits near 20 for 230-350M models vs 37 for Qwen3.5-0.8B (LFM2.5 card); vendors steer tiny models away from knowledge-intensive tasks and creative writing; MaxGPT-2's confident nonsense on photosynthesis is the same failure at 110M.
7. **Reasoning emerges in steps.** Same recipe, MobileLLM-R1: 140M MATH 6.2 vs 360M 28.4. Multi-step follow-ups inside a conversation inherit this.
8. **Chit-chat alone was never walled.** A 90M BlenderBot beat 2.6B Meena on human-rated engagingness in 14-turn chats (2020), and its 90M vs 2.7B self-chat gap (43-57) was not significant. The wall appears when chat means instruction following, knowledge and staying consistent over many turns.
9. **Budget, not parameters, separates the winners.** Every sub-400M model with published chat or IF results that clears the bar used 0.8T-28T tokens, and the strongest used logit distillation from 7B-235B teachers (LFM2 top-32 KD from LFM1-7B; MobileLLM-R1 KD from Llama-3.1-8B in mid-training; Qwen3-0.6B strong-to-weak OPD). Max's plan of 100B tokens for 150M is about 670 tokens per parameter vs 8,900 for Falcon-H1-Tiny-90M. The one low-token exception, Baguettotron (321M, 200B fully synthetic instruction-native tokens), reports only MMLU/GSM8K/HotPotQA, not chat or multi-turn results.

## Open questions

1. Is post-trained MaxGPT-Ultra a good enough chat teacher? Its capacity ratio (~7x) exceeds the 2.5x capacity-gap optimum from pretraining distillation, and its own chat quality after 100B tokens is unmeasured. A mid-size same-tokenizer teacher (Ultra's shakedown 124M is too small; a ~400M sibling would be a "teacher assistant") may be needed.
2. How much vocabulary overlap does Max's 49k tokenizer have with LFM2, SmolLM2 and Qwen vocabularies? GOLD's results tracked mapped similarity (0.64 worked, 0.063 barely). The tokenizer file is on the school server; measuring the string overlap is a five-minute job once it is copied.
3. Does multi-turn OPD help at 150M, or does the student's history get so dirty that teacher signal on late turns is noise (the depth degradation seen in Rethinking OPD)? SMOPD was neutral at 1.7B.
4. Is the Multi-IF gap at 350M caused by attention capacity for distant turns (architecture lane) or by training distribution (this lane)? A planted-fact recall curve by turn distance, before and after multi-turn OPD, would separate them.
5. What SFT budget is optimal at 150M when chat data is already in pretraining? Falcon found an extra SFT stage on an SFT-pretrained model gave no clear signal.
6. Does preference tuning's 1-epoch limit at 90M also bind at 150M, and does on-policy pair generation move it?
7. Can loop rate be driven near Qwen2.5-0.5B's 0/33 at 150M with training-side fixes (unlikelihood, loop negatives, OPD) rather than decoding penalties? This is the most measurable "wall-breaking" claim available.
8. No published study measures human-rated multi-turn chat quality for any 90M-400M instruct model; all sub-400M multi-turn evidence is Multi-IF, MT-Bench-101 (golden history) or MT-Bench (2 turns, LLM judge). The claim "sub-400M models cannot really chat" is therefore neither confirmed nor refuted by published data.
9. LFM2.5-230M/350M credit "multi-stage RL" without details; what those stages reward, and whether any target multi-turn behavior, is unknown.
