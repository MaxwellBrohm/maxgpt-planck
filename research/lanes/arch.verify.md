# Verification: architecture lane (50M-350M)

Checked 2026-09-23 against primary sources: arXiv PDFs converted with pdftotext (MobileLLM 2402.14905, LFM2 2511.23404, Scaling Laws with Vocabulary 2407.13623, Zoology 2312.04927, Repeat After Me 2402.01032, MTP 2404.19737, Physics 3.3 2404.05405, Ouro 2510.25741, Memory Layers 2412.09764, GRT 2608.15062, CART 2606.01495, MoR 2507.10524, Canon 2512.17351, the 20-method replication 2605.20798, Falcon-H1 2507.22448, SmolLM2 2502.02737, plus spot checks of 16 secondary papers), config.json files and HF API parameter totals fetched directly, model-card READMEs (LFM2.5-350M, SmolLM2, Gemma 3 270M), the Falcon-H1-Tiny blog HTML, the modded-nanogpt `train_gpt.py` and README, the evalchemy MTBench source, the PhysicsLM4 repo, and Max's local files (read-only).

I also re-ran the OASST1 tokenizer measurement on this Mac (scratch venv, `tokenizers` 0.23.2) against the cached OASST1 parquet: every bytes/token figure in the report reproduced to two decimals.

The first pass ran without WebSearch, so its 2026 discovery used the arXiv export API only. A second pass (same day, after the machine restarted) had WebSearch. It re-confirmed the three corrections that change the lane's conclusions against the primary text: Jelassi's "L ≥ 70" condition (2402.01032, Sec. 4), the 302M vocabulary data point (2407.13623, Sec. 4 and Figure 7 text), and evalchemy's MTBench defaults (`bench_name = "mt_bench"`, `judge_model = "gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18"`). It also re-read the Falcon-H1-Tiny blog's end-performance and BFCL tables. It then ran targeted searches for 2025-2026 work and added omissions 12 to 17 and misapplication 12 below.

**Summary.** Every number I could recompute or find in a primary source matched: parameter counts, MobileLLM tables, Memory Layers Table 1, GRT, CART, MoR, Canon costs, tokens per parameter, the speedrun's ~640M stored parameters and 0.34B tokens, and the tokenizer measurements. The problems are in what some numbers are taken to mean:

- **The Falcon-H1-Tiny multi-turn quote is about tool calling.** "Multi-turn scores relatively low compared to Qwen-0.6B" refers to BFCL-v3 multi-turn function calling, where Qwen3-0.6B itself scores 3.62% and the 90M scores 0%. It says nothing about chat coherence.
- **TII's "MT Bench (avg)" is most likely 2-turn MT-Bench, not MT-Bench-101.** TII cites MT-Bench-101, but its harness is evalchemy's "turn 1 & 2 average", which runs FastChat's 2-turn MT-Bench with a GPT-4o-mini judge by default.
- **Phone-book lookup has a length condition.** Pythia-410M beats Mamba-2.8B only once the phone book has at least 70 entries.
- **The vocabulary law has a closer data point than the 3B extrapolation.** The same paper measures an optimum directly at 302M non-vocabulary parameters: 16K compute-optimal, 24K with excess data.
- **The "working chat models" tokens-per-parameter floor is too high.** It omits MobileLLM-350M at about 2.9k tokens/param.
- **Canon real-data results do exist.** The lane says there is no quantitative text summary, but the PhysicsLM4 repo publishes one (at 1B to 8B).
- **"Stay full-attention" overreaches.** The recall evidence behind it compares attention with models that have *no* attention (gated convs, Mamba-1). An in-scale controlled study (Hymba, 300M, 100B tokens, one codebase) found that adding SSM heads in parallel with attention *raised* recall-task accuracy over a plain Llama transformer (49.90 vs 39.98).
- **A closer sub-400M data point was missed.** LFM2.5-230M (June 2026) has about 163M of non-embedding body, close to Max's target. It scores Multi-IF 37.70 and IFEval 71.71 in Liquid's harness, and it is shallow and wide (14 layers, d=1024, 65k vocab), not deep and thin.

## Scale misapplications

1. **A tool-calling result is used as chat evidence.** TII's "multi-turn scores were relatively low compared to Qwen-0.6B" (bottom line 1, Section 0) is about BFCL-v3 multi-turn function calling by a 90M tool-calling specialist. Qwen3-0.6B scores 3.62% on it. It says nothing about conversational coherence at 90-150M.
2. **"Beats SmolLM2-360M on a multi-turn benchmark."** The 4.33 vs 3.8 is most likely 2-turn MT-Bench judged by GPT-4o-mini through evalchemy, not MT-Bench-101. Two turns is the minimum, not evidence of long-dialogue coherence.
3. **Canon layers as the "best-evidenced cheap change" for 150M.** At 50M-124M-class sizes the evidence is synthetic only (Depo/Brevo/Mano). All real-data evidence is at >=1B: 1.3B/100B, which the author calls noisy, and 1B-8B on 1-2T tokens, with +0.4 to +1.8 avg-of-10.
4. **Vocabulary extrapolated from a 3B prediction row** (outside the 33M-1.13B fit) when the same paper measured 302M directly (16K compute-optimal, 24K with excess data).
5. **Memory+ at 134M was trained on 1T tokens** (~7.5k tokens/param) and wins only on NQ/TriviaQA. At Max's 100B tokens, a ~800M-parameter sparse table's fill rate is unmeasured. The lane presents the gain as a general "knowledge wall" bypass.
6. **Multi-turn benefits of gated attention** (sink removal, RULER +10) come from 1.7B dense and 15B-A2.5B MoE. At 124M only a loss gain is measured (Max's A/B). The lane flags the scale, but still lists "multi-turn relevance" as a reason.
7. **Engram's "frees attention for global context"** (NIAH, variable tracking) is measured on a 3B-total / 568M-active MoE. The lane flags this.
8. **Joint MoE scaling laws** (up to 2.7B active, compute-optimal regime) are applied to an overtrained 150M. The lane flags the uncertainty.
9. **Task transfer of recall evidence.** Zoology measures exact repeated-bigram recall on the Pile. Jelassi measures phone-book lookup, and only for L ≥ 70. Gloeckle measures induction on repeated names with an epoch oracle. None measures recall of earlier dialogue turns. "Recall of earlier turns is not an architectural wall at 150M" extrapolates from token-level copying to dialogue behaviour.
10. **Looping and sharing framed as the lever for "use what was said three turns ago in a two-step inference".** MobileLLM-LS gains are zero-shot commonsense. Ouro and Saunshi are synthetic. GRT and CART are validation loss. None is multi-turn.
11. **The 1.2B null results (2605.20798) are used to discount the 124M A/B's gate and value-residual gains.** That is reasonable caution, but the directly relevant in-scale evidence is ResFormer at 82M-468M, which is positive.
12. **Evidence against pure recurrent models is applied to hybrids.** Section 1.8 concludes that hybrids "cost exactly the capability his goal depends on". Its evidence is Zoology (gated convolutions with no attention) and Jelassi (pure Mamba-1). Neither tests a hybrid that keeps attention. Hymba's in-scale ablation (300M, 100B tokens, same codebase) points the other way. On SWDE and SQuAD recall, Llama scored 39.98, Mamba 19.23, sequential attention+SSM 45.16 and parallel hybrid heads 49.90. That is single-seed extraction from context, not dialogue recall, but it is the only controlled hybrid-vs-transformer recall comparison near 150M. The lane's own fallback ("a short-conv/Canon hybrid that keeps attention in every layer") is consistent with it. The stronger claim is not.

## Omissions

1. **Canon Part 4.2 real-data numbers** exist in the official repo. LlamaCanon wins all 8 controlled comparisons at 1B-8B on 1-2T Nemotron-CC tokens: avg-of-10 +0.4 to +1.8, MMLU +1.5 to +3.9. This is at >=1B, but it is the only real-data Canon evidence beyond the noisy 1.3B/100B runs. https://github.com/facebookresearch/PhysicsLM4/tree/main/canon_llama_results
2. **In-scale vocabulary data point** in Tao et al.: at 302M non-vocab params, the optimum is 16K, rising to 24K with excess data. The authors recommend the compute-optimal vocab even when overtraining, to limit inference cost. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13623
3. **Learnable multipliers** (Velikanov et al., Jan 2026). A per-row and per-column learnable scale on matrix layers to escape the weight-decay/noise norm equilibrium. It is used in every Falcon-H1-Tiny model. At about 90M and 200 GT with Muon, TII reports "up to a 20% relative gain from random values" on MMLU/BBH/GSM8K over Muon alone. This is an in-scale parametrization lever the lane does not mention. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04890 ; https://tiiuae-tiny-h1-blogpost.hf.space/
4. **Falcon-H1-Tiny's MT-Bench harness** (evalchemy, 2-turn, default GPT-4o-mini judge) and the **BFCL multi-turn table** (Qwen3-0.6B 3.62%, 90M 0%). https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22448 ; https://github.com/mlfoundations/evalchemy
5. **GRT iso-FLOP result at GPT-2-small compute.** A 3-unique-layer, 35M-param GRT matches the 124M dense model (3.14 vs 3.15; 3.145 ± 0.004 vs 3.188 ± 0.056 over 3 seeds). At medium and large scale, dense leads by 0.05 to 0.06 nats. This matters if Max's "150M" means stored parameters and he can spend compute. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15062
6. **SCONE** (Google, 2025): contextualized n-gram embeddings precomputed and stored off-accelerator. A 1B accelerator-resident model beats a 1.9B baseline at about half the inference FLOPs and accelerator memory. It is at >=1B, but it is the closest published design to the lane's "sparse tables in CPU RAM" budget redefinition. Related: **Tensorized Engram** (Jun 2026) shares CP-factorized latents across n-gram orders and "matches or even outperforms Engram-style n-gram modules while requiring much fewer parameters" (scale not stated in the abstract). https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01637 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08347
7. **MobileMoE** (Meta, May 2026): on-device MoE with 0.3B to 0.9B active and 1.3B to 5.3B total, with an on-device MoE scaling law. It bears on the lane's total-vs-active budget question (Section 1.9). https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27358
8. **Attention's own recall still scales.** Zoology's attention AR-hit perplexity goes 2.41 (70M), 2.16 (125M), 1.98 (360M), 1.91 (1.4B). Recall improves most below about 360M, which is exactly Max's range. https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04927
9. **The LFM2 search result** is that attention-minority stacks (6 of 16 layers) match attention-heavier baselines at equal budget. Full attention everywhere is a cheap safe default at 150M, but it is not what the evidence requires. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23404
10. **Smaller MobileLLM sharing estimates.** Sharing on the 8-layer baseline gave +0.4 (125M) and +0.6 (350M) (Table 2), and 3-4x repetition had diminishing returns (App. E). https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14905
11. **Hourglass FFN controlled result at 113M.** Validation loss 3.766 to 3.742 at 12L/d768 with the parameter count fixed, on 2.5B tokens. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06471
12. **LFM2.5-230M** (Liquid AI, released 2026-06-24 per the HF API). HF API total 229,693,184 params, of which 65,536 x 1,024 = 67.1M (29.2%) is tied embedding, so the body is about **162.6M**, the closest shipped chat model to Max's target (my arithmetic from config.json). It has 14 layers (8 double-gated conv + 6 GQA) at d=1024 with SwiGLU 2560, and was trained on 19T tokens (about 83k tokens/param). Post-training is SFT distilled from LFM2.5-350M, then DPO, then multi-stage RL. Card scores (Liquid harness): IFEval 71.71, IFBench 38.40, **Multi-IF 37.70**. For comparison, LFM2.5-350M scores 44.92, Qwen3.5-0.8B 41.68 and Gemma 3 1B IT 44.25 on Multi-IF. It strengthens the premise correction. Its shape (shallow, wide, 29% embeddings) also runs against the lane's deep-thin and small-vocab prescription, although Liquid chose it under a device-latency search, not quality per parameter. https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-230M ; https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-230M/resolve/main/config.json ; https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm2-5-230m
13. **Hymba** (NVIDIA, ICLR 2025). The only in-scale controlled hybrid-vs-transformer recall ablation. At 300M on 100B tokens (Table 1), recall accuracy (SWDE and SQuAD average) was: Llama 39.98, Mamba 19.23, sequential attention+SSM 45.16, parallel hybrid heads 49.90, plus meta tokens 51.79. Commonsense accuracy moved only from 44.08 (Llama) to 45.59 (Mamba alone 42.98). Released Hymba-125M (24 blocks, d=512, only 3 full-attention layers with 1k sliding windows elsewhere, 1.3T tokens) scores 49.35 LightEval average vs SmolLM-135M 48.44 (Table 6). This is single-seed, and "recall" is extraction from a given passage, not multi-turn memory. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13676
14. **MobileLLM-R1-140M** (Meta, Sep 2025). Meta's own successor at this size went **shallow with a large vocabulary**: 15 layers, d=576, 9/3 heads, FFN 2048, 140.2M total (Table 3), with the Llama 3.2 128k tokenizer, tied. By my arithmetic (assuming 128,256 entries; the config is gated) that is about 74M of embedding (53%) and a ~66M body. It was trained on 4.2T tokens (~30k tokens/param). It is a math and code reasoner, not a chat model, and it does not report the choice as an ablation. Still, it is a counterexample to "the MobileLLM lesson is 30 layers" from the same group. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24945
15. **Nemotron-Flash** (NVIDIA, NeurIPS 2025). "Deep-thin models generally achieve better accuracy under the same parameter budget, [but] may not lie on the accuracy-latency trade-off frontier". This matters for the lane's throughput warning on Turing without FlashAttention-2. The models are 1B and 3B (>=1B). https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18890
16. **Inverse Depth Scaling From Most Layers Being Similar** (ICML 2026). Analysis of Pythia (up to 12B), Qwen2.5 and OPT hidden states plus a width/depth loss fit. Loss falls roughly as 1/depth "probably due to functionally similar layers reducing error through ensemble averaging rather than compositional learning". The fit implies width and depth should scale proportionally at fixed parameters. This challenges the lane's framing that depth is "the knob for sequential composition". The analysis is mostly on existing checkpoints at >=410M. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05970
17. **Convolution for Large Language Models** (Jul 2026). A controlled search over 17 convolution positions in a Qwen3 block found a residual depthwise conv (k=3) on projected Q, K and V (Canon-B-like) works best. It adds under 0.01% parameters and improves a 7-benchmark average by +1.99 (1.7B, 30B tokens) and +1.57, +1.91 and +3.76 (4B at 30B, 60B and 100B tokens). This is independent real-data support for the Canon/short-conv A/B, but only at >=1B. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18413
18. **Search coverage caveat.** The first pass used the arXiv export API only. The second pass ran targeted web searches (tiny 2026 chat models, depth vs width, hybrids, MobileLLM successors), but a full sweep of vendor blogs and HF-only releases was not repeated.
