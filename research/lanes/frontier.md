# Frontier lane: candidate "wall-breakers" for a ~150M chat model

Research date: 2026-09-23. Scope: published and emerging ideas that could give more conversational capability per parameter, ranked by evidence x relevance to multi-turn chat x testability on Max's hardware. Every number carries a source URL and the scale it was measured at, written as [scale: params, tokens]. "Unsourced" marks my own estimates. Parameter splits for released models were computed from their `config.json` files (fetched 2026-09-23), not from blogs.

---

## Bottom line

1. **The premise is half right.** Sub-400M models that follow instructions across turns now exist: LFM2.5-230M (June 2026, about 163M non-embedding params by my count from its config) scores 71.7 IFEval and 37.7 on the 3-turn Multi-IF benchmark, within 4 points of Qwen3.5-0.8B (41.7), and Falcon-H1-Tiny-90M reports MT-Bench 4.33. What does not exist yet below ~400M is open-domain, knowledge-grounded chat: every vendor in this size class says so in its own model card, and knowledge scores sit near chance (LFM2.5-230M MMLU-Pro 20.3 vs 37.4 for Qwen3.5-0.8B).
2. **The binding limit is parametric knowledge, not conversational machinery.** Physics of LMs measures a ceiling of about 2 bits of knowledge per parameter (1 bit at 100 exposures, up to 20x worse with junk-heavy data), so a 150M model holds at most ~37 MB of facts. Multi-turn format, instruction adherence and context use are learnable at 90-230M; facts are what run out.
3. **The second limit is training budget, and it is Max's real constraint.** The sub-400M models that chat were trained on 2T-28T tokens with 7B-class teachers and RL. Max's measured throughput (31-53k tokens/s for a 124M model on one Titan RTX) puts a realistic budget at 0.1-0.3T tokens, 10-100x less. Techniques that raise signal per token matter more for him than anything architectural.
4. **The best-evidenced wall-breaker is to keep skills in the 150M dense weights and move facts out**, into a sparse memory bank (Memory+ at a 134M base lifted TriviaQA F1 from 7.7 to 18.8, matching a 373M dense model; Apple's 160M model with fetched memories matched a 410M model on long-tail knowledge) or into retrieval. Nobody has published a chat or multi-turn evaluation of a memory- or retrieval-native model at this size. That gap is the most credible "never been done" target.
5. **The cheapest big lever is a denser training signal:** logit distillation from a same-tokenizer teacher (a 200M model distilled from 7B lands halfway to a 400M one in Gemma 2's ablation), SFT data mixed into pretraining (Falcon-H1-Tiny-90M: IFEval 40.8 to 66.1), multi-turn synthetic data, and on-policy distillation in post-training (MiniLLM at 120M: +6.1 judge points over SFT). Max's own 49k tokenizer has no teacher; SmolLM2's 49,152 tokenizer is shared token-for-token by SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct and Ouro-1.4B/2.6B.
6. **Looping and recursion are overrated for this goal.** The only fitted law (30M-1.1B) says a loop is worth r^0.46 of a unique block, looping does not raise knowledge capacity, and Mixture-of-Recursions loses to a vanilla model at 135M. Looping buys reasoning depth for extra FLOPs; it does not fix what breaks small chat models.
7. **Tokenizer tricks, test-time compute, latent reasoning and hypernetworks have no evidence at 150M for chat.** Low-bit (ternary) only wins if the real constraint is bytes on disk rather than parameter count, and even then by modest margins at this size.

Read of the table:
- The frontier of "chat-shaped" behavior (format, instruction adherence, 3-turn adherence) has moved well below 400M, and it moved through tokens (2T to 28T), teachers and RL rather than through new architecture. - IFEval is a weak proxy for conversation: a 90M model reaches 66 after SFT data is repeated ~100 times in pretraining. Choose multi-turn evaluations that test memory of earlier turns and factual grounding, not format constraints.

## 1. Detailed findings

### 1.1 The physics of what a small model can hold

- **Knowledge capacity is ~2 bits per parameter at best.** At 1000 exposures per fact, capacity ratio consistently exceeds 2 bits/param; at 100 exposures it is ~1 bit/param. Int8 quantization has negligible impact; int4 cuts capacity by more than 2x. MoE with 32 experts loses only 1.3-1.5x capacity despite 11.3x fewer active params, so knowledge scales with total, not active, params. [scale: GPT-2 style models 1M-0.5B on synthetic biographies] https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05405 , details https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05405v1
- **Junk data is the silent killer at small scale.** With 7/8 of data being junk at 100 exposures, capacity degrades 20x; prepending a domain token (e.g. the source website) reduces the loss to 2x, and at 300 exposures fully recovers. [same scale] https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05405v1 . Max's pretraining mix is web-heavy (55% FineWeb-Edu); source-tag prepending is a zero-cost change.
- **Arithmetic for 150M:** 150M x 2 bits = 300 Mbit, about 37.5 MB of recallable facts in the best case, about half that at realistic exposure counts. Physics 3.3 estimates a 7B model at 14B bits, "surpassing English Wikipedia and textbooks combined"; a 150M model can hold roughly 2% of that. This is the mechanism behind MaxGPT-2's "confident nonsense" on photosynthesis (WRITEUP_NOTES.md).
- **Knowledge must be augmented to be extractable.** Facts seen in one phrasing are memorized but not retrievable in QA form, even after instruction tuning; the authors recommend rewriting pretraining data and moving instruction data earlier into pretraining. [synthetic biographies, small GPT-2] https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14316
- **Canon layers** (a residual weighted sum of the previous ~3 token states, i.e. a tiny causal conv) raise synthetic "reasoning depth" 2x and lift NoPE and linear attention to RoPE and Mamba2 level. Cost: 0.0063% extra params at 1.3B, 5.8-20.8% runtime overhead with naive kernels. Real-world pretraining at 1.3B/100B was "high noise and limited resolution", all models failed 2-hop reasoning, and linear models still lag full attention on retrieval-heavy tasks even with Canon. [synthetic: small; real: 1.3B, 100B] https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17351 (v2 July 2026). Pattern worth noting: every strong sub-1B chat model in section 0 contains short causal convolutions (LFM2 conv blocks with kernel 3, Falcon-H1 Mamba conv, Qwen3.5 gated-delta conv kernel 4), per their config files.

### 1.2 Putting knowledge outside the dense parameters (attacks: knowledge storage)

This is the family with the strongest small-scale evidence and the most direct link to the diagnosed limit.

- **Memory layers (product-key memory, Meta, ICML 2025).** Replacing ~3 FFNs with a trainable key-value lookup (1M value embeddings) at a **134M base trained to 1T tokens**: TriviaQA F1 7.7 to 18.77, NaturalQuestions 0.91 to 3.16, HotpotQA 5.18 to 9.35, versus a 373M dense model at 17.68 / 2.58 / 10.06. At equal total params, Memory+ beat MoE (TriviaQA 18.77 vs 13.08) and matched PEER. Non-factual gains were small (PIQA 62.1 to 65.9). Total params grow to 937M, but FLOPs barely change; beyond ~3 memory layers performance drops. Also: "memory helps models learn facts faster" (visible at 200B tokens). [134M-1.3B base, 1T tokens] https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09764 , code https://github.com/facebookresearch/memory
- **Hierarchical memories (Apple, ICLR 2026).** A **160M anchor model** plus an 18M-param memory block fetched per document from a 4.6B-param bank (hierarchical k-means over Sentence-BERT embeddings, 4 levels) and co-trained for +1.1T tokens: specific-knowledge average 34.1 to 40.3 (410M baseline trained 1.1T: 40.9), common-knowledge 45.9 to 48.7 (410M: 52.3), Wikipedia perplexity 17.2 to 14.2. So it matches ~2.5x params on long-tail knowledge but not on common knowledge. Inference FLOPs overhead x1.1 versus x1.7-2.3 for RAG. No chat or instruction evaluation. [160M-1.4B anchors, 1.1T-2.2T tokens, DCLM] https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02375 , table verified in https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02375 , code https://github.com/apple/ml-memory-pretraining
- **Engram (DeepSeek, Jan 2026).** Hashed 2-gram/3-gram lookup tables with a context gate; optimal to give ~20-25% of the sparse parameter budget to Engram and the rest to MoE; at 27B iso-params/iso-FLOPs +3.0 MMLU, +5.0 BBH, +3.7 ARC-C; CKA shows layer 5 of the Engram model resembles layer 12 of the baseline, i.e. lookups free early layers for computation. Smallest run: a 3B-total, 0.56B-active MoE ablation. **No evidence below ~0.5B active.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07372 , https://github.com/deepseek-ai/Engram
- **STEM (Jan 2026):** replaces the FFN up-projection with a layer-local token-indexed embedding lookup; ~3-4% average accuracy gains with a third of FFN params removed. [350M and 1B] https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10639
- **Per-layer embeddings (Gemma 3n, Gemma 4):** each decoder layer gets its own token embedding table kept off-accelerator; Gemma 3n E2B has ~5B raw params but a ~2B accelerator footprint. [2B-8B] https://huggingface.co/blog/gemma3n , https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02770 . Small-scale cousin: modded-nanogpt "value embeddings" (extra token-indexed tables added into attention values) are part of the speedrun record recipe, but the measured win from adding two more was small (about 21 s of ~1436 s to the 2.92 loss target). [~GPT-2 medium scale] https://snimu.github.io/2025/10/07/modded-nanogpt-value-embeddings.html
- **Retrieval-augmented pretraining (RETRO).** Reproduced RETRO at **148M**: validation perplexity 17.76 to 12.99 (-27%) with a 330B-token database, shrinking to -14.5% at 9.5B; downstream gains concentrate on knowledge-intensive tasks, "on par with GPT on other tasks", and RETRO degenerates (repeats) less. [148M-9.5B] https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06762 . Caveat: the original RETRO paper itself attributes part of its gains to overlap between evaluation chunks and the database https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426 . A controlled small study found retrieval-augmented pretraining stores "substantially less world knowledge in the weights" (LAMA ConceptNet P@1 12.97 to 9.30) while improving local syntax (LAS 77.9 to 80.3) and hurting global context. [8.5M-98M encoder-decoders, ~400M words] https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10939 . A May 2025 study finds retrieval is ignored below ~32 shared tokens between input and neighbor, and paraphrased neighbors cut the data needed to learn to use retrieval by ~40% https://pith.science/paper/2505.14309 (secondary summary; primary not fetched).
- **Retrieval at inference, trained as a skill:** Pleias-RAG-350M claims to beat all SLMs under 4B on HotpotQA/2wiki and be competitive with Qwen-2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B when given sources. Vendor claim, RAG-specific tasks. https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18225
- **MoE with small active params.** Physics 3.3 (above) says knowledge tracks total params. MobileMoE-S (May 2026) is 272M active / 1.3B total, ~6T tokens; sub-billion-active MoE is described as "largely unexplored". https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27358 . At 134M, memory layers beat an equal-total-params MoE on factual QA (table above). A tiny-scale TinyStories study finds dense and MoE comparable at matched active params https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13769 (weak evidence).

What is unproven for Max's goal: none of these papers evaluates chat, multi-turn coherence or instruction following. The memory-bank models were trained on 1T-2.2T tokens; whether the benefit survives at 0.1-0.3T tokens is unknown (the "learns facts faster" observation at 200B tokens is mildly encouraging).

### 1.3 Parameter reuse: looping, recursion, adaptive depth (attacks: in-context computation depth)

- **The one fitted scaling law is sobering.** 116 runs, 30M-1.1B unique params, r in {1,2,4,8}: each extra recurrence counts as r^0.46 of the shared block (R^2 = 0.997). "A 410M looped model performs on par with a 580M non-looped model, but pays the training cost of a 1B non-looped one", and replacing unique blocks with recurrences raises loss at matched training compute. [30M-1.1B] https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21106 . For 150M with 2 loops: ~2^0.46 = 1.38x effective params on the looped part, at ~2x its FLOPs.
- **Knowledge capacity does not grow with looping.** Ouro's own Physics-of-LMs test: looped and non-looped models store ~2 bits/param alike; the gain is knowledge manipulation (modular composition task at difficulty 24: 78.0% for a 2-layer block looped 6x vs 7.5% for 2 layers once, iso-params, 6x compute). [1M-40M synthetic] Real models: Ouro 1.4B (24 layers x 2048, 4 loops) and 2.6B, 7.7T tokens; performance drops when looping beyond the trained depth (ARC-C 60.92 at T=4 to 58.96 at T=5); no chat or multi-turn evaluation. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25741 . Note that a 1.4B model looped 4x spends roughly the FLOPs of a ~5.6B dense model per token, so "matches 4B" is close to FLOP-matched.
- **Mixture-of-Recursions (NeurIPS 2025)** with 2 recursions and expert-choice routing: 167M MoR beats 315M vanilla at 16.5e18 FLOPs (NLL 2.7511 vs 2.7824; few-shot 43.1 vs 42.3), but "it underperforms the vanilla model at the smallest model size (135M), likely due to a recursive capacity bottleneck". [135M-1.7B, up to 20B tokens] https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10524
- **MobileLLM immediate block-wise sharing** (run each block twice): +0.7 / +0.8 average accuracy at 125M / 350M with no parameter increase. This is the cleanest looping result at Max's size, and it is small. [125M, 350M] https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14905
- **Relaxed Recursive Transformers** (layer-wise LoRA on tied blocks, uptrained from Gemma 2B): recursive Gemma 1B up to +13.5 points over a vanilla 1B. [>=1B, uptraining from a bigger model] https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20672 . **Retrofitted recurrence** into TinyLlama/OLMo/Llama improves math per training FLOP [>=1.1B] https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07384 ; a July 2026 retrofit into Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct installs a pointer-chasing mechanism that extrapolates ~1.5x beyond trained depth, but zero-shot transfer is only ~16-20% https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11233 . Looped models help compositional multi-step tool calling at matched SFT [Ouro 1.4B/2.6B, OLMo-2-1B, Llama-3.2-1B] https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18171 . Stability fixes allow up to 12 loops https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18797 . Looped MoE "Loopie" at 6B-A0.6B and 20B-A2B beats a 30B-A3B baseline at equal compute https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16051 . All at >=0.5B.
- **Huginn-3.5B** (latent recurrence at test time): a decoding study finds more recurrence only marginally helps GSM8K and falls far short of explicit CoT https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02199 . [3.5B]
- **Latent reasoning (Coconut)** on GPT-2 124M: large wins on synthetic logic (ProsQA 97.0 vs CoT 77.5) but loses to CoT on GSM8K (34.1 vs 42.9); pause tokens did nothing (16.4 vs 16.5 no-CoT). [124M, task-specific fine-tuning] https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06769 . No chat relevance demonstrated.
- **TRM (7M) and HRM (27M)** reach 45% / 8% on ARC-AGI-1 / -2, but they are per-task solvers trained on the evaluation task family; ARC Prize's ablation found HRM's hierarchy "not an important factor", the outer refinement loop drove gains, and performance came mostly from memorizing the evaluation tasks' solutions. https://arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis , https://alexiajm.github.io/2025/09/29/tiny_recursive_models.html . Not evidence about language or chat.

Net: looping attacks depth-limited computation (multi-hop, state tracking, composition), which is a real but secondary limit for chat. At ~150M the best published numbers are +0.7 points (MobileLLM-LS) and a loss to vanilla (MoR at 135M).

### 1.4 Training signal: distillation, chat-native pretraining, multi-turn data (attacks: training signal and data efficiency)

- **Logit distillation during pretraining works at 200M.** Gemma 2 ablation with a 7B teacher: validation perplexity 23 (200M from scratch) vs 21 (200M distilled) vs 19 (400M from scratch); 17 vs 15 at 1B; and a 2B model over 500B tokens averages 60.3 from scratch vs 67.7 distilled. [200M-2B; 500B tokens for the 2B run] https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00118 (Tables 6, 7). Reading: distillation moves a 200M model roughly halfway to a 2x-larger one in perplexity.
- **LFM2's recipe** (the family that produced the best sub-400M chat numbers): "tempered, decoupled Top-K" KD with K=32 teacher logits per token from an internal LFM1-7B, during 10-12T tokens of pretraining. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23404
- **Distillation scaling law:** with an existing teacher, distillation beats supervised training up to a compute level that grows with student size; if the teacher must also be trained, supervised is usually better; teacher quality helps until a capacity gap. [Apple, ICML 2025] https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08606
- **On-policy distillation in post-training at 120M.** MiniLLM (reverse-KL, student samples, teacher GPT-2 1.5B): at a 120M student, GPT-4 feedback on DollyEval 38.6 (SFT) / 40.3 (KD) / 41.2 (SeqKD) / 44.7 (MiniLLM); at 340M the gain nearly vanishes (51.9 to 52.2); the paper also reports lower exposure bias and better long generation. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08543 . Qwen3 used off-policy then on-policy "strong-to-weak" distillation for its 0.6B-14B models and reports it beats RL at 1/10 of the GPU hours. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388 . Exposure bias is exactly the mechanism behind small-model repetition loops.
- **Capacity gap for reasoning traces:** models <=3B learn better from shorter, simpler CoT; mixing long and short traces helps. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12143 . Falcon-H1-Tiny adds that CoT traces are "toxic" for tool calling at 90M (they cause repetition loops) and the 90M reasoning variant hit a "repetition trap". https://tiiuae-tiny-h1-blogpost.hf.space/
- **Tokenizer determines which teachers are usable.** Logit KD needs a shared vocabulary. I checked token-to-id maps (2026-09-23): SmolLM2-135M and SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct share an identical 49,152 vocabulary, and Ouro-1.4B matches 49,150 of 49,152 ids (only `<think>`/`</think>` differ). Max's own 49k BPE matches none of them. Cross-tokenizer KD exists (ULD https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12030 ; approximate likelihood matching https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20083 ) but adds risk. Re-tokenizing 100B tokens is cheap on the Lambda box (Max measured 1.8M tokens/s on 64 cores, so ~15 hours; TECHNIQUES.md).
- **SFT data inside pretraining ("anti-curriculum").** Falcon-H1-Tiny-90M: IFEval 40.77 (pretrain then SFT) vs 50.11 (SFT data mixed into pretraining, 25% of the mix, about 100 repetitions of sources like Tulu 3 over 800B tokens), 53.47 vs 66.08 after DPO; MT-Bench 4.33 vs 2.68 for SmolLM2-135M-Instruct; one DPO epoch was optimal, more degraded. They also define a "memorization window" (for FalconMamba-7B, ~100-500B tokens) inside which repeated high-quality data is not overfit. [90M, 800B tokens; single vendor, not peer-reviewed] https://tiiuae-tiny-h1-blogpost.hf.space/ . Consistent with Physics 3.1's recommendation to move instruction data into pretraining.
- **Multi-turn data closes most of the multi-turn gap.** "Lost in Conversation": across 15 LLMs, sharded multi-turn versions of the same tasks lose 39% on average, driven by unreliability (+112%) more than aptitude (-15%), and models that take a wrong early turn do not recover; smaller models are more sensitive to rephrasing. [>=8B] https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120 . TurnWise: adding 10k synthetic multi-turn conversations to SFT raises Olmo 3 7B's multi-turn self-score by 12.8 points, and with preference tuning the multi-turn deficit falls to -1.3. [7B] https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16759 . No sub-1B replication exists.
- **Data rewriting/selection via a teacher without logits:** MiniPLM's "difference sampling" (reweight the corpus by teacher vs small-reference probability) improved 200M, 500M and 1.2B students with a 1.8B teacher and needs only offline teacher inference. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17215

### 1.5 Architecture primitives that matter at tiny width (attacks: representation bottleneck)

- **Softmax bottleneck / saturation.** In Pythia, models of 160M and below "saturate": late in training on 300B tokens their loss rises and plateaus, final checkpoints ~8% worse than the scaling law predicts; performance drops when LM-head rank is below ~1000 "regardless of model size". [Pythia 14M-410M] https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07647 . A March 2026 follow-up argues the head also destroys 95-99% of the gradient norm and reports up to 16x faster convergence with a maximal head https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10145 ; an August 2026 causal test finds the geometric compression real but not established as a harmful optimization bottleneck (backward-only rank cut +0.059 loss vs forward low-rank head +0.180, WikiText-2-scale models) https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16671 . Practical reading: keep d near or above ~1000 if possible, which is what LFM2.5-230M does (d=1024, 14 layers).
- **Width vs depth is unsettled at this size.** MobileLLM favored deep-thin (30 layers at 125M) https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14905 ; Baguettotron is 80 layers x 576 (config.json); Falcon-H1-Tiny found 24 layers x 512 beat both 50 x 384 and 11 layers because the deep model trained at half the throughput https://tiiuae-tiny-h1-blogpost.hf.space/ ; LFM2.5-230M is 14 x 1024. These conflict because they optimize different budgets (quality per step vs per GPU-hour). A cheap, decisive A/B for Max.
- **Embedding share.** With a 49k vocab, embeddings cost 25.2M (d=512) to 37.7M (d=768) of a 150M budget (arithmetic). Gemma 3 270M is 62% embedding. The vocabulary scaling law says smaller models want smaller vocabularies [33M-3B] https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13623 ; Monad uses 8,192 at 56M, Falcon-H1-Tiny 32,768 at 90M (configs). Tying saves ~16M at 125M for a 0.2-point loss that extra depth recovers https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14905 .
- **Max's measured recipe** (NorMuon + cautious WD + gated attention + value residual + 1/sqrt(depth) norm scaling) gave 6.9% lower held-out loss than plain AdamW blocks at 124M / 1.1B tokens (docs/ab_2026-09-21/README.md). That is a larger measured effect at his scale than any architecture paper in this lane shows at 125-150M.

### 1.6 Tokenization (attacks: context coverage and per-token difficulty)

- **SuperBPE:** 33% fewer tokens at 200k vocab; +4.0 average over 30 tasks and +8.2 MMLU at 8B. At 50k vocab it reaches 5.55 bytes/token vs ~4.4 for BPE. But at **680M in the over-trained regime** (Max's regime), SuperBPE at matched parameters has worse bits-per-byte than BPE; only the larger, inference-compute-matched SuperBPE model wins; downstream evals were "too noisy for our small models". [680M, 1.9B, 8B] https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13423 . Upside for chat: more conversation fits in a fixed context and fewer forward passes per turn. Downside: harder per-token prediction for a tiny model.
- **Byte Latent Transformer:** FLOP-matched parity with Llama 3 tokenization, 61.1 vs 60.0 average at 8B; scaling studies from 400M; entropy-model returns diminish beyond 50M params. [400M-8B] https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09871 . **H-Net:** byte-level one-stage beats BPE Transformer when compute and data matched; two stages match a token Transformer of twice its size (reported at ~1B scale). https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07955 . **Bolmo** byteifies existing OLMo 3 checkpoints (1B, 7B) rather than training from scratch https://allenai.org/blog/bolmo . None has evidence at 150M, and all add engineering risk on Turing (no FlashAttention-2).
- **Verdict:** for Max, tokenizer choice matters mostly because it decides which teachers can supply logits (section 1.4), and because vocab size sets the embedding tax.

### 1.7 Params vs bytes vs FLOPs (attacks: the definition of "small")

If the real constraint is on-device size or latency rather than parameter count, the comparison changes:

- **Ternary at equal bytes (Spectra, all trained on 300B tokens):** FloatLM 190M is 3.05 Gbit (~381 MB) and averages 46.7 on six commonsense tasks; TriLM 560M is 2.76 Gbit (~345 MB) and averages 48.4; TriLM 830M is 3.55 Gbit and averages 50.7; FloatLM 390M (6.28 Gbit) averages 48.5. On knowledge-flavored tasks the equal-bytes pair roughly ties: LAMBADA accuracy 31.1 (Float 190M) vs 32.4 (Tri 560M), SciQ 77.3 vs 78.7, consistent with "knowledge scales with bits". TriLMs match FloatLMs at equal params only at 3.9B. [99M-3.9B, 300B tokens] https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12327 (Tables 4, 6-8). Cost: ~3x training FLOPs per token for the 560M vs the 190M, plus QAT overhead.
- **ParetoQ:** 2-bit and ternary sit on the size-accuracy Pareto frontier; a ternary 600M beats the previous best ternary 3B; a 2-bit MobileLLM-1B beats a 4-bit MobileLLM-600M by 1.8 points while being smaller; best results spend ~90% of training in full precision and ~10% in QAT. [125M-3B] https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02631
- **BitNet b1.58 2B4T:** 0.4 GB non-embedding memory, average 54.19 vs 55.23 for Qwen2.5-1.5B (2.6 GB). [2B, 4T] https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12285 . At tiny scale, 1.58-bit needs about double the hidden size to match 16-bit [100K-48M] https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09527 .
- **Post-training int4** cuts knowledge capacity >2x (Physics 3.3), so a 150M bf16 model (300 MB) and a 600M int4 model (~300 MB, arithmetic) are not equivalent in facts; QAT or ternary-from-scratch behave better than PTQ.
- **Per-layer embeddings / memory banks** reframe "params" again: the Apple 160M model carries a 4.6B-param bank on disk but computes like ~178M.

Implication: decide early whether "150M" means dense compute params, total params in RAM, or bytes on disk. The most promising architectures (memory banks, MoE) only look good under the first definition.

### 1.8 Growth, hypernetworks, adapter banks

- **Progressive growth (G_stack):** depthwise stacking of a smaller trained model gave a 54.6% pretraining speedup for 7B at 300B tokens, with gains persisting past 8T tokens. [validated from ~400M up] https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15319 . For Max it is a compute saver (grow 75M to 150M), not a capability ceiling breaker.
- **Hypernetworks (Text-to-LoRA):** generates task LoRAs from a text description; demonstrated on Mistral-7B. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06105 . No small-model or chat evidence. Speculative use: per-persona or per-domain adapters swapped at conversation start.

### 1.9 Test-time compute for tiny models

- "1B beats 405B" and "0.5B beats GPT-4o" hold on MATH-500/AIME24 only with a compute-optimal search guided by a separate process reward model (7B-class and up), and the optimum depends on the policy, the PRM and problem difficulty. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06703 . Conversation has no verifier, so none of this transfers directly.
- Falcon-H1-Tiny-R-0.6B's AIME24 75.0 pass@1 is math-only, from heavy reasoning distillation plus GRPO, and the 90M variant "plateaus earlier" with repetition traps. https://tiiuae-tiny-h1-blogpost.hf.space/
- Adaptive per-token depth (MoR, Ouro exit gates) is the only test-time lever that applies to chat tokens, and it is weak at 135M (1.3).

## 2. What this implies for a ~150M chat model

1. **Split the job.** A 150M dense core has enough capacity for language, dialogue format, instruction adherence and in-context tracking (LFM2.5-230M's 163M non-embedding core does 3-turn instruction following at 37.7 Multi-IF). It does not have enough capacity for broad facts. Any design that asks the 150M core to also be an encyclopedia hits the Physics 3.3 ceiling.
2. **The credible novel contribution** is a chat model whose facts live in a sparse memory bank (product-key memory layers, Apple-style fetched memory blocks, or Engram-style n-gram tables) and/or in retrieved text, with the dense core trained chat-first. Every ingredient has small-scale evidence; the combination, and any multi-turn chat evaluation of it, has not been published. That is a defensible "first" with a real chance of working, unlike a new looping scheme.
3. **Spend compute on signal per token, not on FLOPs per token.** Given 0.1-0.3T affordable tokens, logit KD from a same-tokenizer teacher, source-tag prepending, SFT data mixed into pretraining, and multi-turn synthetic conversations are the levers with measured effects at 90-200M. Looping multiplies FLOPs per token, which is the resource Max has least of.
4. **Pick the tokenizer for the teacher.** Switching to SmolLM2's 49,152 tokenizer unlocks SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct (MT-Bench 6.13 per the SmolLM2 paper) and Ouro-1.4B/2.6B as logit teachers. Keeping Max's own tokenizer means sequence-level distillation only (teacher-generated text), which is weaker at 120M (SeqKD 41.2 vs MiniLLM 44.7).
5. **Architecture: borrow, then A/B.** Start from Max's measured 124M recipe (-6.9% loss), add short causal convs or Canon layers and keep several full-attention layers for exact recall of earlier turns (Canon's own result: linear mixing lags on retrieval), and run one width-vs-depth A/B (d ~1024 x ~12 layers vs d ~640 x ~24 layers) because the softmax-bottleneck evidence and the deep-thin evidence point in opposite directions.
6. **Define "150M" before building.** If it means total bytes on a phone, a ternary or 2-bit model of ~500-600M is the fair comparison and wins modestly on commonsense. If it means dense compute, memory banks and MoE are allowed and are where knowledge scaling lives. If it means total parameters in RAM with everything counted, neither trick applies and the ceiling is data and distillation.
7. **Measure what fails, not IFEval.** Use a 3-turn benchmark (Multi-IF), a "refer back to turn 1" probe, a factual-grounding probe with and without retrieval, and a repetition-loop rate. Max's own failure log (Bitcoin.com loops, confident nonsense) maps onto exposure bias and knowledge capacity respectively.

## 3. Evidence about the "wall" (why chat quality degrades below ~400M)

- **Knowledge ceiling.** ~2 bits/param at 1000 exposures, ~1 at 100, 20x worse with 7/8 junk data (synthetic, 1M-0.5B) https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05405 . Observed: MMLU-Pro is flat between LFM2.5-230M (20.25) and LFM2.5-350M (20.01) despite RL and 9T more tokens https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm2-5-350m-no-size-left-behind ; SmolLM2-135M-Instruct MMLU 24.64 and Falcon-H1-Tiny-90M-Instruct 27.31 (4-choice chance is 25) in Falcon's evaluation https://tiiuae-tiny-h1-blogpost.hf.space/ ; Gemma 3 270M pretrained TriviaQA 15.4 https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_3 .
- **Vendors concede open-domain chat is out of reach below ~300M.** Gemma 3 270M "not designed for complex conversational use cases" https://developers.googleblog.com/en/introducing-gemma-3-270m/ ; LFM2.5-230M "not recommended for ... creative writing" https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-230M ; Falcon-H1-Tiny multi-turn tool scores "relatively low compared to Qwen-0.6B" https://tiiuae-tiny-h1-blogpost.hf.space/ .
- **But format and multi-turn adherence are not walled.** LFM2.5-230M Multi-IF 37.70 vs Qwen3.5-0.8B 41.68 and Gemma 3 1B 44.25 https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm2-5-230m ; Falcon-H1-Tiny-90M MT-Bench 4.33 vs Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct 4.16 (different judges, indicative only).
- **Representation saturation below d~1000.** Pythia <=160M saturate late in training, ~8% above scaling-law loss (300B tokens) https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07647 ; the gradient-bottleneck extension is contested https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16671 .
- **Context tracking improves with scale and is human-level by 410M in Pythia.** Implicit entity-tracking accuracy rises from 53.5% (70M) to 89.6% (12B); human-level at 410M on 300B tokens; instruction tuning adds up to +52.4 points on the explicit (generation) version https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18083 (June 2026). Pythia is undertrained by 2026 standards, so the 410M threshold is an upper bound for well-trained models.
- **Unreliability, not inability, dominates multi-turn failure**, and it is universal (39% average drop, +112% unreliability) with smaller models more fragile to rephrasing https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120 ; targeted multi-turn data largely fixes it at 7B https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16759 .
- **Degeneration.** Small models fall into repetition loops: Falcon-H1-Tiny 90M reasoning variant "repetition trap", CoT data causes loops in 90M tool models https://tiiuae-tiny-h1-blogpost.hf.space/ ; RETRO reduces degeneration https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06762 ; on-policy distillation reduces exposure bias https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08543 ; Max's MaxGPT-2 "Bitcoin.com" loops (WRITEUP_NOTES.md).
- **The wall is a moving budget line, not a physical constant.** Capability density doubles roughly every 3.5 months https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04315 ; the 2024 "floor" (Qwen2.5-0.5B) is matched on instruction metrics by 2026 models at 90-230M trained on 0.8T-19T tokens. Gemma 2's authors note small-model gains had come "largely" from longer training and propose distillation as the alternative https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00118 .
- **Looping does not lift the ceiling.** Recurrence adds no knowledge capacity (Ouro, 1M-40M synthetic) https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25741 ; MoR loses at 135M https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10524 ; r^0.46 value per loop https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21106 .

## 4. Levers, ranked by evidence x relevance to multi-turn chat x testability

Cost basis (sourced from Max's logs): a 124M model trains at ~53k tokens/s (plain AdamW) or ~31k tokens/s (NorMuon + gated attention recipe) on one TITAN RTX in fp16 (docs/ab_2026-09-21 metrics), so 1B tokens takes ~5-9 hours per card and 10B tokens ~2.2-3.7 days; MaxGPT-3 (235M) ran ~20B tokens in 6-7 days on the RTX 5070 (~35k tokens/s). Scaling to 150M is my estimate. Availability caveat: the 4 thermally viable Titans are busy with Ultra for ~70 days and the 5070 is lent out, so near-term experiments must be Mac-sized (1M-40M on the Physics of LMs synthetic suites https://github.com/facebookresearch/PhysicsLM4 ) until a card frees up.

## 5. Open questions

1. Does a memory bank trained on 0.1-0.3T tokens still give the 2-3x factual gain measured at 1T-2.2T tokens? The "learns facts faster at 200B tokens" remark in Memory Layers is the only hint.
2. Does offloading facts to memory or retrieval actually improve multi-turn coherence, or only factual QA? No paper evaluates a memory- or retrieval-native model on chat or multi-turn benchmarks at any size below 1B.
3. At what size does entity and state tracking reach human level when a model is trained on 1T+ modern tokens rather than Pythia's 300B? The 410M threshold is from an undertrained suite.
4. Width vs depth at 150M: does the softmax-bottleneck argument (d>=1000, LFM2.5-230M) beat the deep-thin argument (MobileLLM, Baguettotron) once Max's gated-attention and value-residual changes are in?
5. How much of Falcon-H1-Tiny's IFEval jump is general chat skill vs repeated exposure to IFEval-like constraint data? A held-out multi-turn eval would settle it.
6. Can fp16 teacher inference (Turing, no bf16) run SmolLM2-1.7B without overflow? Unmeasured.
7. Is there a cheaper teacher-free substitute for logit KD (MiniPLM-style data reweighting, teacher-written rephrasings for knowledge augmentation) that recovers most of the gain on Max's budget?
8. Does multi-turn synthetic SFT data (TurnWise-style, 10k conversations) transfer at 150M as it does at 7B? Needs a sub-1B replication.
9. Which definition of "150M" does Max want to claim (dense compute, total RAM, bytes on disk)? The ranking of levers 4, 8 and 9 flips depending on the answer.
