Verified 2026-09-23 against primary sources (arXiv PDFs pulled and grepped with pdftotext, model config.json and safetensors headers fetched from Hugging Face, Max's own A/B logs parsed). Scope: the 13 load-bearing claims plus other numbers the lane's recommendations lean on.

Method limits, stated plainly: in the first pass the session's WebSearch budget was exhausted and the arXiv search API returned HTTP 429, so omissions 1-8 came from direct fetches of known papers. A second pass (after the machine shut down mid-run) re-ran targeted 2026 web searches, re-checked the LFM2.5-230M config.json, the looped-law numbers (2604.21106) and Gemma 2 Table 7 (all unchanged), and added omissions 9-12 from PDFs pulled and grepped the same way. ## 2. Scale misapplications

1. **Memory+ "learns facts faster at 200B tokens"** (sections 1.2 and 5.1) is from the 8B-base run, yet it is offered as the only hint that memory survives Max's 0.1-0.3T budget at 150M. There is no small-base, short-budget evidence in that paper.
2. **Apple hierarchical memory** is summarised as a 1.1T-token result. The 160M row that matches 410M on long-tail knowledge actually trained on 2.2T tokens total. That is 7-22x Max's budget, not 4-11x.
3. **Gemma 2 distillation** used a 7B teacher (35x a 200M student). The lane turns this into "worth ~1.5-2x params" for teachers of 360M-1.7B (2.4-11x the student). Teacher strength is part of the measured effect, and the capacity-gap law (see Omissions) says the gain depends on the teacher-student ratio.
4. **Qwen3 on-policy distillation vs RL** was measured on Qwen3-8B, math and code only. The lane presents it as covering 0.6B-14B.
5. **Falcon memorization window** of 100-500B tokens is for a 7B model. The blog's own scaling gives ~5 GT at ~100M, and the lane also inverted what the window means.
6. **Looped-model law (phi = 0.46)** was fitted on compute-optimal runs of at most ~7.8B tokens. It is used to judge looping for an over-trained 150M model at 100B+ tokens, a regime the fit never saw. The direction probably holds; the constant is untested there.
7. **Canon layers** are recommended in lever 6. Real-data evidence exists only at 1.3B/100B and is described by its own authors as noisy; everything else is synthetic.
8. **Lost in Conversation "smaller models are more sensitive to rephrasing"** and **TurnWise +12.8** come from multi-billion models (the lane's own [>=8B] and [7B] labels). They still count as "moderate-strong" evidence for lever 2 in the ranking table. The lane flags that no sub-1B replication exists, but the ranking does not discount for it.
9. **MiniLLM "helps most at the smallest size"** reads a scale trend into one data point. The same table shows larger gains at 760M and 1.3B.

## 3. Omissions (primary sources checked)

1. **UltraMem (ICLR 2025), counter-evidence at Max's size.** At a 151M dense base with 12x sparse params (~2.0B total), MoE-151M-2in32 beat both product-key memory and UltraMem: TriviaQA 33.27 vs 24.66 (PKM) and 28.97 (UltraMem), avg 33.20 vs 28.64 / 29.99, val loss 2.63 vs 2.76 / 2.67. Memory only overtakes MoE from 680M up ("as the model capacity increases"). This weakens the lane's "memory layers beat MoE at 134M" and the ranking of lever 4 over lever 9. [151M-6.5B, RedPajama; 151M ablations at 500B tokens] https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12364 (PDF Table 1, Sec 5.4)
2. **Mixture of Lookup Experts (MoLE), evidence at Max's params AND token budget.** 160M active, trained on 100B Pile tokens: dense avg 38.8, MoLE-16E 41.9 (7.4B params offloaded as lookup tables, ~0.15M loaded per token), matching MoE-34E (41.8) with dense-like VRAM and speed. This is the closest published match to "150M compute, facts in cheap lookup tables, 100B tokens", and the lane never cites it. [160M-1B active, 100B tokens] https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15798 (PDF main results table, "160M Activated & In-VRAM Parameters" block)
3. **Over-Tokenized Transformer (ICML 2025).** Hashed multi-gram input embeddings, a direct precursor to Engram. Trained at OLMo2-151M on 400B tokens with consistent loss gains at every size; 400M + OE-12.8M matches the 1B baseline. This refutes the lane's "no evidence below ~0.5B" for the Engram/n-gram-table family. [151M-1B dense, 400B-1T tokens] https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16975
4. **Law of capacity gap.** Optimal teacher ≈ 2.5 x student (fit T* = 2.498 S - 11.498, R^2 0.9957, GPT-2/Pythia under 3B, pruning-then-distillation setup). For a 150M student that points to a ~375M teacher (SmolLM2-360M-Instruct) rather than 1.7B, which bears directly on lever 1's teacher choice and cost. https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07052
5. **GKD (ICLR 2024).** On-policy distillation with T5-small (77M), T5-base (250M) and T5-large (800M) students from a ~3B teacher on summarization, translation and GSM8K. This is more small-student on-policy evidence than MiniLLM alone, but encoder-decoder and task-specific. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13649
6. **TinyStories.** Coherent multi-paragraph text below 10M params when the data distribution is narrow. This matters for the premise check: coherence at tiny scale depends on how broad the domain is, not only on params. A narrower conversational domain is an untested lever the lane does not list. https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759
7. **Minitron (prune + distill).** Pruning a trained model and retraining with distillation on under 3% of the original tokens. Evidence only at 4B-8B from 15B. For Max, the analogue would be pruning SmolLM2-360M to ~150M on the shared tokenizer. It is untested at this size, but it is cheap relative to training from scratch. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14679
8. **Same-source items the lane skipped:** Falcon-H1-Tiny found no clear anti-curriculum gain for its multilingual 100M model, and its curriculum control won MT-Bench and AlpacaEval (see row 3). Apple's Table 4 shows memory added post-hoc to Gemma 3 270M (SK 34.3 to 38.2, 1.1T DCLM tokens), a cheaper route than co-training from scratch.
9. **How much do language models memorize? (Morris et al., FAIR, 2025).** Measures raw memorization capacity of GPT-style models at 3.5-3.6 bits/param in bf16 (3.83 in fp32, Table 1) by training on uniformly random bitstrings. This is a different quantity from Physics 3.3's extractable-knowledge bits, but it means the lane's "at most ~37.5 MB for 150M" is the Physics 3.3 figure, not a hard ceiling: raw storage at 3.6 bits/param would be ~67 MB (arithmetic). The paper itself calls its number "a slightly larger estimate than Allen-Zhu & Li". [capacity table: 80K-6.9M params, synthetic bitstrings; membership-inference laws validated up to 1.5B] https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24832
10. **Fast-weight Product Key Memory (Sakana AI, Jan 2026), the closest thing to "episodic memory for earlier turns" at Max's size.** 12-layer ~112M baselines on 10B tokens (5B LongContext64 + 5B FineWeb-Edu, 4K sequences). FwPKM (a PKM whose slots are rewritten at inference by chunk-level gradient steps) cuts long-context perplexity for Gated DeltaNet and sliding-window backbones and reaches 128K-token needle retrieval from 4K training. Two findings bear on the lane: plain slow-weight PKM gave its largest gains on FineWeb-Edu ("semantic memory for general knowledge") at ~112M, which supports lever 4 at Max's exact scale; and with unrestricted full attention "PPL improvements are marginal" because the model learns to ignore the memory. For chats that fit in a 4K window, full attention already does the "remember turn 1" job; episodic fast-weight memory only matters if Max goes linear or sliding-window. [~112M, 10B tokens] https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00671 , code https://github.com/SakanaAI/fast-weight-product-key-memory
11. **Midtraining Bridges Pretraining and Posttraining Distributions (CMU, v2 Feb 2026), controlled evidence at 70M-1B on lever 2.** Pythia-architecture models trained from scratch on C4 for 128B tokens, with midtraining mixes introduced at 12B-105B tokens. "Broad instruction data (FLAN) also shows minimal effect"; benefits concentrate in domains far from web text (code, math). At 160M, LIMA SFT validation loss was 3.828 (C4 only), 3.836 (FLAN 5%), 3.736 (KnowledgeQA 20%, a general-knowledge and dialogue mix), 3.792 (DCLM 20%), Table 8. So at Max's size, instruction-formatted data in pretraining helped chat-like SFT loss only a little, and only for the dialogue-flavoured mix. This is a peer-comparable counterweight to Falcon-H1-Tiny's single-vendor IFEval jump (row 3). It is loss, not a chat benchmark. [70M-1B, 128B tokens] https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14865
12. **Scaling Embeddings Outperforms Scaling Experts (Meituan LongCat, Jan 2026), n-gram embeddings at 280M activated.** From-scratch runs at 280M, 790M and 1.3B activated. At 280M activated, n-gram embedding beats a parameter-equivalent MoE only while it stays below about half of total params (crossover slightly above a 20x total-to-activated ratio; it "consistently underperforms its MoE counterpart once the ratio exceeds 30"), and the advantage grows with width and shrinks with depth. Together with omission 3 this refutes the lane's "no evidence below ~0.5B active" for the Engram family, and it says a narrow, deep 150M model is the configuration where n-gram tables help least. [280M-1.3B activated, MoE base] https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21204 . Related 2026 variants (Tensorizing Engram https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08347 , Tokenizer-Agnostic Engram https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29065 ) were found but not read in depth.

## 4. Net effect on the lane's conclusions

- The premise check survives: LFM2.5-230M's numbers and config are exactly as reported, and sub-300M multi-turn instruction following is real on vendor benchmarks.
- "Knowledge is the binding limit" survives on the Physics 3.3 evidence. The MMLU-Pro row used to illustrate it does not (row A).
- Lever 2 (SFT data inside pretraining) is weaker than stated. The measured gain is on IFEval and LiveBench; on MT-Bench and AlpacaEval the ordinary pretrain-then-SFT control did as well or better. The one controlled study at 160M (omission 11) found FLAN-style instruction midtraining did almost nothing for downstream SFT loss, and a dialogue-flavoured mix helped only modestly.
- The "~37.5 MB of facts" ceiling is the Physics 3.3 extractable-knowledge figure. Raw memorization capacity measured by a different method is ~3.6 bits/param (omission 9), so treat 37.5 MB as an order of magnitude, not a bound.
- Multi-turn "memory" at chat lengths is an attention job, not a memory-layer job: FwPKM at ~112M adds almost nothing on top of full attention (omission 10). This supports the lane's advice to keep several full-attention layers.
- Lever 4 vs 9 ordering is contested at 150M. UltraMem has MoE beating memory layers at 151M, and MoLE shows lookup-table experts working at 160M on 100B tokens. MoLE and Over-Tokenized, both unmentioned, are the best-matched evidence for "facts outside the dense core" at Max's scale and budget. LongCat (omission 12) adds that n-gram tables at 280M activated only beat MoE below about a 20x total-to-active ratio and help narrow, deep models least.
- Lever 1 teacher choice: the capacity-gap law favors SmolLM2-360M over 1.7B, which also costs less teacher compute on Turing.
