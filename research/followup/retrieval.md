# Follow-up track: knowledge outside the model, for a 10M-150M chat model

Research date 2026-09-23. Builds on the first run's lanes (`research/lanes/*.md` and their `.verify.md` fact-checks); where a fact-check corrected a lane claim, the corrected version is used here. Every number carries a source URL or says "unsourced", and the scale it was measured at. **[>=1B only]** marks evidence that exists only at 1B parameters or above. "Vendor" means a model maker's own claim, not reproduced. Numbers marked "(fetched summary)" were read through a web summarizer from the paper's HTML and should be spot-checked before a design decision rests on them. Nothing was trained or run for this report (the Mac is busy); everything below is reading plus arithmetic.

---

## Bottom line

1. Using supplied text instead of weights is a **trained skill that small models learn well**, and the best evidence is older than the first run's sources: in knowledge-grounded dialogue with 5 retrieved Wikipedia passages, a **90M** BlenderBot used the passages about as much as or more than a **3B** one (Knowledge F1 23.9 vs 20.2), and a 400M BART's human-rated hallucination rate fell from **68.2% to 7.9%** once it was trained with retrieval ([arXiv 2104.07567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07567)). The 0% "SmolLM2-360M ignores the gold passage" result from the first run is an untrained, quantized, off-the-shelf model, not a capacity limit.
2. Small models are, if anything, **more** context-faithful than big ones once trained as readers: the rate at which a QA reader overrides a substituted passage with its memorized answer rises from under 15% at T5-60M to 50% or more at 11B ([arXiv 2109.05052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05052)). Parametric knowledge fights the context; a model that stores less has less to fight with.
3. The newest directly relevant result (July 2026) pretrains SmolLM-architecture models at **135M and 360M** on text with named entities anonymized, so the weights cannot store entity facts: closed-book recall collapses (12.5% to 0.7% at 135M) while context-grounded tasks improve (FEVER 82.8 to 89.5 at 135M, HaluBench 59.8 to 65.5 at 360M) and commonsense does not drop ([arXiv 2607.12831](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12831v1), fetched summary). This is the closest published thing to "optimize a tiny model for skill and push facts out", and it is untested in chat.
4. Tool calling at 90M-350M is a data problem, not a size wall: Falcon-H1-Tiny-90M gets 94.4% BFCL relevance detection but 0% multi-turn, while FunctionGemma-270M goes from 10-39% to 90-97% on multi-turn tool calling after task fine-tuning on a few thousand synthetic conversations (vendor, [distil labs](https://www.distillabs.ai/blog/making-functiongemma-work-multi-turn-tool-calling-at-270m-parameters/)). Max's Daimax "tools are the biggest lever" result is confounded (first-run verify file) and should not carry weight on its own.
5. At Planck's context lengths, retrieved text and conversation history compete directly: with short turns, a 3-chunk lookup costs about a third of the history room at 1,024 tokens and about a sixth at 2,048 (my arithmetic, section 6), and more passages is not better (at 400M, Knowledge F1 falls from 27.6 with 1 passage to 23.9 with 50, [arXiv 2104.07567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07567)). Keep lookups small, few and placed next to the current turn.
6. For memory beyond the window, a tiny model can plausibly write **structured** notes but not reliable free-form summaries: T5-small (60M) tracks dialogue state on MultiWOZ 2.1 at 56.1% joint goal accuracy vs 56.7% for T5-base (220M) ([arXiv 2109.07506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07506)), whereas sub-1B summarizers are faithful but omit key details ([NAACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.253/)), and verbatim retrieved chunks beat extracted facts by 16-22 points on long-conversation memory ([arXiv 2601.00821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00821)). No sub-1B result exists on LoCoMo or LongMemEval as the answering model.
7. Recommendation: Planck's weights hold grammar, conversational skill and a thin layer of common knowledge; entity facts come from a zero-parameter BM25 lookup that the harness runs every turn and injects as a short block next to the current user turn; the model is trained from pretraining onward on grounded dialogues with relevant, distractor, missing and counterfactual lookups, and must pass a two-sided test (answer when the lookup has it, abstain when it does not, never override the lookup from memory). The headline curve should be reported twice, with and without lookup, on a fictional-entity knowledge base so that the with-lookup score cannot come from the weights.

## Detailed findings

### 0. What the first run already established (corrected versions, not repeated in detail)

- Knowledge capacity is about 2 bits/parameter at 1,000 exposures and about 1 bit at 100, GPT-2 family 1M-0.5B; at 100 exposures gated-MLP models (SwiGLU) store 1.3x less than GPT-2 ([arXiv 2404.05405](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.05405), per `context.verify.md`). Upper bounds for Planck: 20M x 2 = 40M bits (5 MB), 60M = 15 MB, 150M = 37.5 MB of facts (my arithmetic).
- RETRO at 172M-7.5B (non-embedding counts), about 10x-parameter-equivalent on C4 bits-per-byte ([arXiv 2112.04426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426)); RETRO reproduction at 148M: validation perplexity 17.76 to 12.99 and less repetition ([arXiv 2304.06762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06762)); kNN-LM lowers perplexity but does not improve open-ended generation ([arXiv 2305.14625](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14625)).
- Memory layers at a 134M base (937M total) and Apple's hierarchical memory at a 160M anchor improve factual QA only, trained on 1T-2.2T tokens (`frontier.verify.md`); UltraMem found MoE beating memory layers at 151M ([arXiv 2411.12364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12364)).
- "Can Small Language Models Use What They Retrieve?": SmolLM2-360M-Instruct 0.0% exact match even with the gold passage ([arXiv 2603.11513](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11513)); the verify file downgrades this (single author, 4-bit NF4 models, exact match, the 360M scored 0 in every condition, which looks as much like an answer-format failure as a reading failure).
- Pleias-RAG-350M/1B claims to beat SLMs under 4B on HotPotQA/2wiki (claim made jointly for both sizes, `data.verify.md`).
- Tool use: Toolformer's self-supervised benefit appears only around 775M; supervised traces work at 90M-350M for single calls; multi-turn BFCL is 0% at 90M and 270M (`context.md` 1.4.6, confirmed).
- Daimax: the vault retrieving chat excerpts made 0.5B-0.8B models parrot them as fact (Instant now retrieves facts and documents only); "tools are the lever" is confounded (2 tool cases, tools 30-40% of the score, no within-model tools on/off ablation, six 0.8B-3B models also called zero tools) (`context.verify.md` rows 11, 16).
- The first run's probe (preliminary, unaudited; hint only) found that recall inside about 1,700 tokens is not the wall at 90M-600M, that deflection ("I don't have access to personal information") is the largest failure class, and that updating a value after a correction fails at every size up to 0.6B (`lanes/probe.md`).

Reading: every small-scale RETRO/kNN result is measured in perplexity, and the perplexity gains come mostly from copying overlapping tokens. That is exactly the "reading" skill Planck needs, but none of these papers evaluates a chat. The perplexity-to-generation gap is real (kNN-LM, [arXiv 2305.14625](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14625)). Retro-li also shows the architectural price: chunked cross-attention added 24M parameters to a 109M model (22%) and needed a 110M-class sentence encoder, both of which would dominate a 20M model. Plain in-context retrieval (FiD-style readers and In-Context RALM) costs zero parameters.

### 2. Grounded dialogue at small scale: the strongest evidence for Planck's thesis

**Retrieval Augmentation Reduces Hallucination in Conversation** (Shuster et al., EMNLP Findings 2021; Wizard of Wikipedia; retrieval from 21M 100-word Wikipedia passages; models fine-tuned on the task) ([arXiv 2104.07567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07567), [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.07567); fetched summary):

- Human evaluation, BART-Large (400M): hallucinated responses **68.2%** without retrieval; RAG-Sequence 9.6%, RAG-Token 17.0%, FiD-RAG **7.9%** (5 passages). "Knowledgeable" 34.1% vs 96.1%, at a small cost in "engaging" (85.5% vs 78.0%).
- Model size with retrieval held constant (RAG-Token + DPR, 5 passages, WoW valid seen, Table 11): Knowledge F1 **BlenderBot-90M 23.9**, BlenderBot-400M 23.2, **BlenderBot-3B 20.2**, T5-base (220M) 25.5, T5-large (770M) 25.2, BART-large (400M) 26.0. Perplexity improves with size (13.4 to 8.2), knowledge use does not. The authors: "increasing model size leads to decreasing performance in the Knowledge F1 metric", hypothesizing the large model relies on "its conversational fluency".
- Number of passages (BART-large): Knowledge F1 27.6 with 1, 26.0 with 5, 24.5 with 25, 23.9 with 50, while perplexity and F1 barely move. More text in context means less of it gets used.
- Query: retrieving on the last turn only is worse than on the full dialogue context (Knowledge F1 23.3 vs 26.0).
- Ceiling: with gold knowledge, Knowledge F1 61.2 and F1 39.1, far above any retrieved condition, so retrieval quality, not reading, was the bottleneck at 400M.

**SeeKeR** (Shuster et al., EMNLP Findings 2022): one transformer called three times, to write a search query, to write a "knowledge" sentence from retrieved documents, then to respond ([arXiv 2203.13224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13224), Table 4, read from the PDF). On topical prompt completion (human-judged): GPT2-medium (345M) true 15%, hallucinated 68%, topical 1%; **SeeKeR-medium (345M) true 34%, hallucinated 54%, topical 13%**; InstructGPT 175B true 58%, hallucinated 62%, topical 4%. Two caveats that matter: 54% hallucination is still high at 345M, and splitting the three steps into separate ~400M BART modules was worse than one shared model "despite containing nearly ∼800M more parameters". A single small model can learn the query / extract / respond pipeline, and sharing weights helps.

Scale note: both papers fine-tune pretrained seq2seq or GPT-2 models (90M-3B) on dialogue with retrieval; nothing here was trained from scratch, and the chats are single-topic, short (WoW is about 9 turns, unsourced here).

### 3. Reading versus remembering: what decides whether a small model uses the context

- **Parametric override grows with size.** Longpre et al. substitute the answer entity in a QA passage and measure how often the reader still outputs the memorized original. With T5 readers from 60M to 11B, "the memorization ratio rises from <15% to ≥50%" with size; training on substituted passages drops it on NQ-train from 29.5% to 2.6% and improves out-of-distribution F1 by 7.1 points (NQ dev, no answer overlap) ([arXiv 2109.05052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05052), [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.05052); fetched summary). Scale: 60M-11B, fine-tuned readers.
- **Knowledgeless LMs (KLLM, July 2026).** Pretraining text is run through Flair's 18-class OntoNotes NER and named entities are replaced by typed, document-local placeholders (numbers and dates kept); at inference the input is anonymized the same way and outputs are de-anonymized. Models: SmolLM-architecture 135M, 360M, 1.7B and LLaMA-architecture 1B, 3B; 2.5B, 10B and 20B pretraining tokens; each model fine-tuned on each downstream benchmark ([arXiv 2607.12831](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12831v1); all numbers fetched summary, verify before relying on them):
  - 135M at 2.5B tokens, baseline vs KLLM: closed-book 12.5% to **0.7%**; context-grounded LAMA 15.8 to 18.6, SQuAD 15.2 to 16.1, FEVER F1 82.8 to 89.5, HaluBench 58.5 to 61.2; CommonsenseQA 26.5 to 31.1, PIQA 65.9 to 69.6.
  - 360M at 2.5B tokens: closed-book 20.8% to 1.2%; FEVER 83.6 to 90.1, HaluBench 59.8 to 65.5, PIQA 67.8 to 71.2.
  - With BM25 retrieval over Wikipedia (10B-token models; size not stated in my fetch): NQ 35.7 to 44.9, TriviaQA 52.8 to 63.4.
  - Anonymization strength at 1.7B is a monotonic trade: p = 0, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0 gives closed-book 34.1 / 20.9 / 9.0 / 2.9 and factual-QA-with-context 43.1 / 44.5 / 47.3 / 48.1.
  - Final eval loss is much higher (2.26 vs 0.95, on anonymized text) yet downstream grounded tasks improve. Limitations the authors list: at most 20B tokens, NER recall 87%, and worse behavior when good context is missing. Code "will be released upon publication".
  - Scale: exactly Planck's range (135M-360M) and budget order (2.5-20B tokens), but task-specific fine-tuning, not chat.
- **New facts in fine-tuning teach hallucination.** Fine-tuning examples whose answers the model does not already know are learned slower and, once learned, "linearly increase the model's tendency to hallucinate" ([arXiv 2405.05904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05904)) **[measured on a large model; size not in the abstract]**. For Planck: SFT or synthetic chat data that asks the model to state facts that are neither in the context nor reliably in its weights trains confabulation. Every factual answer in chat training data should be backed by text in the context.
- **Retrieval-augmented pretraining stores less knowledge in the weights** (8.5M-98M, section 1). For Planck this is the goal, not a side effect.
- **Pleias-RAG** (350M and 1B): mid-trained on 3,126,691 synthetic RAG examples (about 9.47B tokens, about 3,000 tokens each), 4,096-token context, with a structured reasoning pass (query analysis, source analysis, draft) before an answer with literal-quote citations; refusal was taught by swapping the query for an unrelated one in about 5% of examples ([arXiv 2504.18225](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18225); fetched summary). No refusal accuracy is reported, and the paper says the model still "would drift into discussing his general biography rather than acknowledging the information gap". Two lessons: 5% negatives was not enough to make abstention reliable, and the recipe relies on a reasoning trace that Planck excludes. Scale: vendor, 350M-1B.

Synthesis: whether a small model reads is set by training, not size. Off the shelf, instruction-tuned small models ignore context (2603.11513); trained on grounded data, a 90M dialogue model uses passages as well as a 3B, and 60M readers override context less than 11B ones. The first run's "train the reader from day one" conclusion stands, now with evidence at 60M-400M instead of only RETRO perplexity.

### 4. Training a tiny model to say it does not know

- **Why models guess.** After pretraining, the hallucination rate on a fact type is at least roughly the fraction of those facts seen exactly once ("singleton rate"), and accuracy-only evaluation rewards guessing over abstaining ([arXiv 2509.04664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04664)). A 20M-150M model has a high effective singleton rate for almost every entity fact, so without an explicit abstain path it will guess.
- **A dedicated [IDK] token collapsed at 70M and 160M.** IDK-tuning (continued pretraining on 1B Pile tokens, moving probability mass to a new [IDK] token when the model is wrong) worked from Pythia-410M up to Mistral-7B, but at Pythia-70M and 160M "the predicted distributions collapse towards a uniform distribution", with 0% correct predictions at the worst point for 160M; the cause given is that the new token's initial probability "gets rounded to zero even when using float32 precision", producing huge gradients ([arXiv 2412.06676](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06676)). This is an optimization failure of a bolt-on token, not evidence that tiny models cannot abstain; a plain-text "I couldn't find that" trained from pretraining onward avoids it.
- **Answerability can be judged by small encoders.** Deciding whether a passage supports a query, fine-tuned: RoBERTa-large (355M) 90.2% on SQuAD v2.0, ModernBERT-base 86.4%, vs Llama-3.1-8B fine-tuned 91.1% and GPT-4o zero-shot 95.5% ([arXiv 2506.21288](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21288v1); fetched summary). Scale: 110M-355M encoders. The skill Planck needs for abstention is in reach at its size, as a classification.
- **Prompt-based abstention does not handle misleading context.** Small frozen RAG models (3.8B-8B) abstain when context is absent but not when it is misleading ([arXiv 2608.22228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22228)) **[>=1B only]**. Counterfactual and distractor lookups must be in training, not just empty ones.
- **The opposite failure is already the biggest one.** The first-run probe (hint only) found deflection was the largest failure class in 90M-600M chat models (36 of 95 failed user-fact checks), including Qwen2.5-0.5B deflecting on 45% of its final user-fact turns. Abstention training that is not conditioned on "the fact is really absent from context and history" will make this worse. Any abstain metric must be two-sided.

### 6. Context budget: retrieved text vs conversation history

Evidence:
- More passages lowers knowledge use at 400M (27.6 to 23.9 Knowledge F1 from 1 to 50 passages, section 2).
- Untrained small models lose 42-64% of previously correct answers when any context is added, "driven by the presence of context rather than its quality" ([arXiv 2603.11513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11513), 360M-8B; weak evidence per the verify file).
- In multi-turn RAG, models do better on first-turn than later-turn questions ([MTRAG, arXiv 2501.03468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03468): 110 conversations, 7.7 turns average, 842 tasks) **[>=1B only]**.
- Retrieval queries built from the full dialogue beat the last turn alone (section 2), and conversational query rewriting into a standalone question is a standard small-model job (T5-base fine-tuned on CANARD/QReCC, e.g. [castorini/t5-base-canard](https://huggingface.co/castorini/t5-base-canard); accuracy numbers not rechecked).
- RECOMP compresses retrieved documents to as little as 6% of their tokens with "minimal loss", and can return an empty string when documents are irrelevant ([arXiv 2310.04408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04408); compressor and reader sizes not in the abstract).

Arithmetic (mine; assumptions: short-register turns of about 30 user + 50 assistant tokens plus about 10 template tokens, so about 90 tokens per exchange; system prompt 64; current user turn 40; reply reserve 128; lookup = 3 chunks x 96 tokens = 288):

| Context | History room, no lookup | Exchanges | History room, 3-chunk lookup | Exchanges | Share of history lost to lookup |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,024 | 792 | about 8.8 | 504 | about 5.6 | 36% |
| 2,048 | 1,816 | about 20 | 1,528 | about 17 | 16% |
| 4,096 | 3,864 | about 43 | 3,576 | about 40 | 7% |

Attention cost (Kaplan-style forward estimate, extra FLOPs per token = 2 x n_layer x n_ctx x d_model): a 150M model at 30 layers x d576 adds about 71M FLOPs/token at 2,048 (+24% over 2N = 300M) and about 142M at 4,096 (+47%); a 20M model at, say, 12 x d256 adds about 12.6M at 2,048 (+31% over 2N = 40M) and +63% at 4,096 (my arithmetic; the 20M shape is illustrative). Context is relatively more expensive the smaller the model, which argues for 2,048 tokens and a lookup capped around 256-320 tokens rather than a long context.

Where the lookup goes: attention to early tokens decays over a conversation at 7B-70B ("When Attention Closes", [arXiv 2605.12922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12922); [arXiv 2402.10962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10962)) **[>=1B only]**, so a lookup block pinned to the start of the context is the worst place for it. Placing it immediately before the current user turn, and dropping old lookups as turns age (keeping only facts the model actually used in its replies, which stay in history), is my recommendation; it is unmeasured at small scale.

Architecture alternative: RETRO-style chunked cross-attention and FiD keep retrieved text out of the decoder's self-attention window, so history room is untouched. The price is parameters (Retro-li: +24M on 109M, plus a 110M-class encoder) and complexity; at 20M-60M that price is larger than the history it saves at 2,048 tokens. In-context retrieval is the default; cross-attention is a later A/B at 150M only.

Can a tiny model do its own summarizing? The evidence splits by output type:
- **Structured state (slot: value), yes.** DST is exactly "read the conversation, write the current value of each slot, overwrite on correction", and 60M does it as well as 220M on MultiWOZ. A 0.6B extractor matches GPT-4o-mini at memory extraction. This is also the skill the probe says small chat models lack (updating after a correction), so training it as an explicit write step may help in-window coherence too (my inference).
- **Free-form summaries, risky.** Sub-1B summarizers are mostly faithful but drop key details, and any detail a summary drops is gone for good. Verbatim retrieval beats extraction on long-conversation memory.
- **Self-managed paging, no.** It fails at 70B.

So Planck's memory beyond the window should be: keep verbatim turns in a store; when the conversation exceeds the window, retrieve **user turns** (never the model's own past replies, per Daimax) by BM25 against the current turn; and optionally keep a short "notes" block of slot: value facts that the model writes in a trained, schema-constrained memory-write turn. Notes augment verbatim retrieval; they never replace it.

## What this means for Planck at 10M-150M

**The split.**
- *In the weights:* grammar, turn-taking, reference and state tracking over the context, reading a lookup block, choosing between "answer from lookup", "answer from what the user said", "answer from common knowledge" and "say it's not there", and writing structured notes. Plus a thin layer of high-frequency common knowledge (what a dog is, that Paris is a city), because chat without any world knowledge is unusable and high-frequency facts are cheap (they are seen thousands of times; Physics 3.3's 2 bits/param regime).
- *Outside the weights:* named-entity facts, numbers, anything long-tail or changeable, and conversation content older than the window.
- *Parameter accounting:* use BM25 so the retriever has zero learned parameters and the headline "total parameters" stays honest. If a dense encoder is ever used, report its parameters next to Planck's.

**Interface.**
1. The harness retrieves on every user turn (no "should I look it up" decision at first; Falcon's 61% irrelevance score says the not-calling decision is the weak one at 90M). Query = the current user turn plus entity words from the last few turns (Shuster: full context beats last turn). A trained model-generated query (SeeKeR style) is a later ledger item.
2. The lookup is 1-3 chunks of about 64-96 tokens, at most about 300 tokens, inserted as its own role (for example `<|lookup|>`) immediately before the current user turn. Old lookups are dropped from later turns; whatever the model said about them stays in history.
3. Context 2,048 tokens. Beyond the window: BM25 over stored user turns plus an optional notes block, both injected the same way.
4. A tool-call route (the model emits `<|lookup|>query<|/lookup|>`) is the second stage, trained with multi-turn traces and plenty of no-call chit-chat, and compared against always-on retrieval.

**Training.**
1. Grounded dialogue from pretraining onward, not only in SFT (the anti-curriculum finding at 90M and Instruction Pre-Training at 500M, from the first run). Lookup-bearing conversations mix: relevant lookup (answer in it), distractor lookup (same-type wrong entity alongside the right one), missing (lookup irrelevant: reply "I couldn't find that" and, where natural, ask), counterfactual (lookup contradicts real-world memory: follow the lookup; Longpre's substitution), and user-over-lookup cases (the user's own stated facts win for questions about the user).
2. Build most grounded examples on fictional entities so the answer cannot be in the weights; generate with the teacher and auto-verify, as already planned.
3. Every factual claim in chat training data must be supported by the context (Gekhman); strip or rewrite teacher replies that add unsupported facts.
4. Consider partial entity anonymization of the pretraining text (KLLM, p around 0.5-0.75) as a bet on freeing capacity from facts; keep numbers and dates.
5. Keep abstention in plain text; do not bolt on a special [IDK] token at 20M-160M without an initialization fix (collapse at Pythia-70M/160M).
6. No chain-of-thought or "source analysis" reasoning traces in grounded data (Falcon 90M loops; Planck is not a reasoning model). Pleias shows the reasoning-trace route; Planck should test the direct-answer route.

**The multi-turn test, with-lookup variant.** Run the core battery (the eval lane's F1-F8, no lookup) and a with-lookup battery (LK) on the same models, and report both curves.

| Family | What it tests | Example | Pass | Must fail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LK1 Grounded answer | reading the lookup | fictional "Harlow Bridge opened in 1932" in lookup; "when did it open?" | right value, no other in-lookup value | empty; distractor value; invented value |
| LK2 Follow-up on an earlier lookup | using lookup content later from history | turn 2 lookup about a museum; turn 5 "and how much was the ticket you mentioned?" with an irrelevant new lookup | value from turn 2 | new lookup's value; "I couldn't find that" |
| LK3 Distractors | picking the right chunk | 3 chunks, 2 about same-type entities | right entity's value | any distractor value; shotgun listing |
| LK4 Missing | abstaining when absent | lookup irrelevant, fact never stated | hedge pattern and no candidate value | any confident value |
| LK5 Counterfactual | lookup beats memory | lookup says a real, well-known fact is different (for example a fictional-world rule) | lookup's value | the real-world value (parametric override rate) |
| LK6 User vs lookup | whose fact wins | user said "my flight is at 6"; lookup is a generic timetable | user's value for user questions; lookup's for world questions | swapped sources |
| LK7 Correction over lookup | state update | lookup says 9 pm; user says "they changed it to 8" | 8 later in the chat | stale 9 |
| LK8 Anti-parrot | not asserting retrieved chat | memory retrieval returns an earlier wrong assistant claim | does not restate it as fact | restating it |
| LK9 Beyond the window | memory harness | fact stated at turn 2, asked at turn 40 of a 3,000+ token chat | right value via turn retrieval or notes | wrong or IDK |
| LK10 No-call chit-chat (tool route only) | not calling tools needlessly | "thanks, that helps!" | no lookup call | any call |

Conditions: no lookup (LK1-LK3 should then abstain), oracle lookup, BM25 lookup, shuffled lookup (another conversation's). Shortcut rows: copy the lookup's first sentence; always "I couldn't find that"; regex oracle. Metrics: grounded accuracy; unsupported-value rate (any slot value found in neither context nor history); false-abstain rate; parametric-override rate (LK5). The composite must be two-sided: a model that always abstains and a model that always answers must both score low. Grounded answers are checked against the lookup text, not against the real world. Also check interference: adding LK data must not lower the core (no-lookup) battery.

**The curve.** For each size (for example 20M / 60M / 150M), report core score, with-lookup score, and closed-book factual score. The claim Planck can make: at size X, with-lookup grounded chat passes, while closed-book factual knowledge is at floor, and the with-lookup pass size is lower than the no-lookup pass size on a matched knowledge-bearing chat set.

## Ledger ideas

Cheapest-test costs use the compute lane's Mac measurement (about 10.6-11.8k tokens/s at 18.5M non-embedding, about 4-5k at 60M; roughly 10 h per 18.5M run at 20 tokens/param, `lanes/compute.md` section 3; the verify file marks it unverifiable). All tests wait until the Mac is free. Each comparison is 2-3 seeds against the same baseline, scored on the LK and core batteries.

1. **Grounded data in pretraining vs SFT-only.** Hypothesis: putting lookup-bearing dialogues (about 5% of tokens) into pretraining from step 0 gives a better reader than the same data in SFT only. Why: Falcon 90M anti-curriculum; Instruction Pre-Training at 500M; KLLM shows pretraining-time choices change reading. Cheapest test: two 18.5M runs at about 0.4B tokens, identical data except placement. Win: at least +5 points LK composite with core battery within 2 points.
2. **Counterfactual substitution fraction.** Hypothesis: 10-20% counterfactual lookups (answer entity swapped) cut parametric override to near zero without hurting LK1. Why: Longpre, 29.5% to 2.6% at T5 scale. Cheapest test: SFT-only sweep (0 / 10 / 25%) on one pretrained 18.5M checkpoint, about 20M tokens each (under 1 h each). Win: LK5 override under 5% with LK1 not lower than baseline.
3. **Partial entity anonymization of pretraining text (KLLM).** Hypothesis: anonymizing named entities in 50-75% of pretraining documents frees capacity from entity facts and improves grounded chat at 20M-60M. Why: KLLM at 135M/360M, grounded tasks up, commonsense up. Cheapest test: 18.5M, p = 0 vs 0.5 vs 1.0, about 0.4B tokens each; NER preprocessing runs on CPU. Win: LK composite +3 or more at p = 0.5 with core battery not worse and closed-book dropping (the expected sign).
4. **Fictional-entity grounded dialogues.** Hypothesis: grounded examples about invented entities teach reading better than real-entity ones, because the weights cannot shortcut. Why: KLLM and Longpre both work by breaking the weights-to-answer shortcut. Cheapest test: SFT on 18.5M checkpoint, real vs fictional vs 50/50, same token count. Win: higher LK1 on held-out fictional and on real-entity items, lower LK5 override.
5. **Lookup placement.** Hypothesis: a lookup block just before the current user turn beats one at the start of the context, and the gap grows with turn count. Why: attention to early tokens decays across turns (>=7B evidence). Cheapest test: eval-only, one trained model, both placements (if trained with random placement) or two short SFT runs. Win: at least 5 points better LK1/LK2 at 8+ turns.
6. **Lookup budget.** Hypothesis: 1-2 short chunks beat 5 at 20M-60M. Why: Knowledge F1 falls from 27.6 to 23.9 from 1 to 50 passages at 400M; history room shrinks. Cheapest test: eval sweep 1 / 3 / 5 chunks x 64 / 128 tokens on one model. Win: a clear optimum at 3 chunks or fewer, reported per size.
7. **Query construction.** Hypothesis: harness query from current turn plus recent entity words beats last-turn-only BM25, and a model-written query (SeeKeR-style, same weights) beats both at 60M+. Why: 26.0 vs 23.3 Knowledge F1 for full context vs last turn; SeeKeR at 345M. Cheapest test: retrieval-only recall@3 on the LK set (no model run for the first two variants), then a short SFT for the model-written query. Win: recall@3 up 10+ points, carried into LK accuracy.
8. **Always-on retrieval vs model-invoked lookup tool.** Hypothesis: at 20M-60M always-on retrieval wins; the tool route only pays off at 150M. Why: 94% relevance but 61% irrelevance at 90M; multi-turn tool use needs dedicated traces. Cheapest test: two SFT variants on the same 60M checkpoint, LK plus LK10. Win (for the tool route): LK within 2 points of always-on and fewer lookups per turn.
9. **Plain-text abstention vs a special token.** Hypothesis: "I couldn't find that" as plain text trains stably at 20M, while a new [IDK] token needs an initialization fix. Why: IDK-tuning collapsed at Pythia-70M/160M from float32 rounding. Cheapest test: SFT at 18.5M, plain text vs [IDK] token (random init vs init to the embedding of "unknown"). Win: plain text reaches LK4 at least 80% with false-abstain under 10%; confirms or refutes the collapse at 20M.
10. **Answerability head.** Hypothesis: a linear head on Planck's final hidden state predicting "the lookup or history contains the answer" gates answer vs abstain better than the LM's own choice. Why: 110M-355M encoders reach 86-90% answerability; the probe found facts retrievable but the reply policy wrong. Cheapest test: freeze an 18.5M checkpoint, train a logistic probe on LK hidden states (minutes on CPU), then a decode-time gate. Win: false-abstain and unsupported-value rates both drop by a third.
11. **Structured memory-write turn.** Hypothesis: training Planck to emit a notes block (slot: value, overwrite on correction) after each user turn improves LK7 and core correction items, and supports LK9. Why: T5-small 60M DST matches T5-base; the probe's correction failure at every size to 0.6B. Cheapest test: SFT on synthetic DST-style chats at 18.5M and 60M; score notes accuracy (joint slot accuracy) and LK7. Win: notes joint accuracy at least 60% at 20M and LK7 +10 points.
12. **Verbatim user-turn retrieval vs notes vs both, beyond the window.** Hypothesis: verbatim retrieval of user turns beats notes alone, and both together beat either. Why: verbatim beats extraction by 16-22 points at large scale; MSC summaries near gold at 2.7B. Cheapest test: eval-only on LK9 with one trained model and three harness settings. Win: "both" at least 5 points over the best single setting.
13. **Never retrieve assistant turns.** Hypothesis: excluding the model's own past replies from memory retrieval removes parroting without losing recall. Why: Daimax 0.5B/0.8B parroting. Cheapest test: eval-only LK8/LK9 with and without assistant turns in the store. Win: LK8 failures near zero with LK9 unchanged.
14. **In-context vs chunked cross-attention retrieval at 150M.** Hypothesis: RETRO-style cross-attention helps only when history room is binding, and never pays at 20M-60M. Why: Retro-li's +24M parameters on 109M; RETRO's perplexity gains are overlap copying. Cheapest test: not on the Mac; a 60M pair on a Titan after December. Win: cross-attention beats in-context on LK9 at equal total parameters (counting the added blocks).
15. **The headline experiment: does lookup move the pass size down?** Hypothesis: the smallest size that passes a knowledge-bearing multi-turn chat test is at least 2x lower with lookup than without. Why: FiD-base 220M beats T5-11B closed-book; BlenderBot-90M uses knowledge as well as 3B. Cheapest test: the size sweep itself, scored twice. Win: a clear leftward shift of the pass threshold, with core (knowledge-free) scores matched.
16. **Multi-turn tool traces.** Hypothesis: the 0% multi-turn tool score at 90M-270M disappears with a few thousand multi-turn traces. Why: FunctionGemma 270M 10-39% to 90-97% (vendor). Cheapest test: 60M SFT with 5,000 synthetic multi-turn lookup-tool conversations. Win: at least 70% multi-turn call accuracy on held-out schemas with LK10 false calls under 10%.

## Open questions

1. Does grounded-reading skill survive at 20M? The smallest trained evidence is 60M (T5-small readers and DST) and 90M (BlenderBot); nothing tests a 10-30M decoder.
2. Is the KLLM result real at chat level, and how much common knowledge must be kept for chit-chat to feel normal? The paper fine-tunes per benchmark and its numbers came through a summarizer here; its code is not released yet.
3. What is the right abstain rate? Too little gives confabulation (Kalai), too much gives the deflection failure the probe found. The LK battery needs a calibration target, perhaps false-abstain under 10% at grounded accuracy above 80%.
4. Does training on lookup-bearing data hurt the core battery (interference), for example by making the model look for a lookup when the answer is in an earlier user turn?
5. Can a model this size write notes and reply in the same turn without derailing, or does the memory write need to be a separate harness call (costing a second prefill)?
6. BM25 over a fictional knowledge base is clean for evaluation; what real knowledge base should the deployed Planck use (Wikipedia chunks, SYNTH seeds), and how does retrieval quality on real queries limit it (Shuster's gold-knowledge gap: 61.2 vs 26.0 Knowledge F1)?
7. Where does the probe's "correction fails at every size up to 0.6B" come from, and does an explicit notes step fix it inside the window, not just beyond it?
8. Do the retrieval results transfer from encoder-decoder readers (FiD, T5, BART) to a small decoder-only model with a lookup block in its context? Most of the small-scale grounded-dialogue evidence is seq2seq.
