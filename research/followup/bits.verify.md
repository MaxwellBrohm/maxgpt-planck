# Fact-check: followup/bits.md (information per parameter)

Checked 2026-09-23. Method: the primary PDFs were downloaded from arXiv and converted with pdftotext, then searched for each number (Physics 3.3 2404.05405, Morris 2505.24832, Kumar 2411.04330, Canon 2512.17351, Gu 2505.18091, IKP 2604.24827, Revisiting BF16 2010.06192, adapter capacity 2607.21351, Zucchet 2503.21676, DFloat11 2504.11651, Behrens 2506.14457). Also read: the MLX data-types page (fetched raw), the NVIDIA Turing architecture whitepaper, PyTorch MPS source (`aten/src/ATen/native/mps/OperationUtils.mm`), and arXiv abstract pages for every 2025-2026 citation plus the omission candidates below. Nothing was run or trained.

Limits of this check: the session's WebSearch budget ran out partway through, and later arXiv search and Semantic Scholar requests were rate-limited (HTTP 429). The novelty searches for soft-label distillation of facts and for low-rank bits/param are therefore incomplete. Where I found nothing, I say "no prior found, search incomplete", not "untested".

## Summary

All ten load-bearing claims point in the right direction, and the core numbers (2 bits/param, the 2.3 maximum, 1 bit at 100 exposures, 3.51/3.83, int8/int4, Canon +10-15% / +40% / 10x, Gu's 3000 exposures and 1.152, IKP's +0.0013/month) match the papers. The six corrections:

1. **Kumar et al. precision law (claim 4).** The fitted weight-precision sweep only covers 3 to 12 bits (integer fake-quant in the forward pass), with a BF16 run used as the "infinite precision" reference. So "16-bit = 99.75%" and "32-bit = 99.9994%" are extrapolations. More importantly for Planck, the paper's own Finding 3 says that when N is fixed and the model is heavily overtrained, compute-optimal precision grows with log(D/N) and "higher precision during training may be compute-optimal". The report's "7-8 bits, i.e. lower, not higher" therefore points the wrong way for Planck's regime. fp64 is still unmotivated.
2. **Revisiting BF16 (claim 9).** The report undersells this source. Its seven applications include BERT-Base (about 110M) language modelling on Wiki103, where pure 16-bit weights with nearest rounding gave perplexity 56.88 against 5.50 for fp32. That is an LM at Planck scale. Stochastic rounding matched fp32 on 5 of 7 tasks, not all, and Kahan summation closed the rest. Whether this mechanism explains Morris's bf16 gap is still unverifiable: Morris never says whether master weights were fp32.
3. **IKP (claim 8).** The time trend is fitted on 100 dated open models, not the 93 calibration models. The "reasoning improves about 2 points per month" figure comes from 30 models with vendor-reported GPQA Diamond scores (R² 0.52). The paper is a single-author preprint.
4. **TITAN RTX fp64 (claim 5).** The Techgage page does not state 1/32 or 0.51 TFLOPS; it only says FP64 is not unlocked. The 1/32 figure is in NVIDIA's Turing whitepaper. The MLX quote is verbatim.
5. **Geometric memory (not in the claim list, but used in the Planck section).** 2605.12426 studies random bijections, and its result is a logarithmic embedding **dimension**. It does not show that structured knowledge needs "far fewer parameters", so that sentence is unsupported.
6. **Behrens et al.** is ICLR 2026 (main conference). ICML 2025 was the MOSS workshop version.

Three ledger ideas marked "untested" have in fact been tried, at least in part: exposure scheduling (Zucchet et al. ran uniform vs Zipf vs subset warm-up), capacity under Muon (Kim et al. 2026, in theory and synthetic tasks), and QAT at 4 bits (Kumar et al., measured in loss). Two more (low-rank factorized models and memory layers) have loss-level prior results.

## Scale misapplications

1. **IKP as "the strongest new evidence for the Planck bet".** It is measured on 135M-1.6T models, with the time trend dominated by models of 1B and up. The "skill" side is GPQA Diamond (graduate-level science QA, 30 vendor-reported scores, much of the gain likely from reasoning post-training), not multi-turn conversational coherence. It says nothing direct about 10M-150M.
2. **Kumar et al. applied to Planck.** "Compute-optimal is 7-8 bits, lower not higher" holds only for jointly optimized N, D, P. For a fixed small N trained far past Chinchilla (Planck), the same paper predicts the optimal precision **rises** with log(D/N). Also, the 16-bit and 32-bit N_eff values extrapolate beyond fits that stop at 12 bits.
3. **DFloat11's "about 11 bits of entropy per weight"** is measured on multi-billion-parameter models. It is used to explain why tiny models fill 2-4 bits, but nobody has measured it at 1M-150M.
4. **Gu et al.'s 1.152 exponent** was fitted on 70M-410M Pythia models. The direction "higher threshold at 20M than at 150M" is supported; the magnitude at 20M is extrapolated.
5. **Geometric memory (2605.12426)** is cited as showing structured knowledge costs "far fewer parameters". It shows a logarithmic embedding dimension for random bijections in a one-layer model. That is neither structured data nor a total-parameter saving.
6. **Chang et al. (OLMo 1B/7B)**: power-law forgetting and the batch-size effects are cited in the mechanism list for tiny models without flagging that they come from 1B-7B checkpoints.
7. **Spectra ternary** ("ternary may do better per stored bit", Sec 5): the report flags ≥ 2.4B in Sec 1.9 but drops that flag in Sec 5.

## Novelty overclaims (ledger ideas marked "untested")

1. **Exposure scheduling (uniform vs Zipf vs subset warm-up vs spacing).** Three of the four arms were already run. Zucchet et al. (COLM 2025) compared uniform, inverse-power-law (alpha = 1 is Zipf) and subset warm-up schedules at a fixed training budget on synthetic biographies with a 44M model (Fig 4 right: "uniform zipf warmup"; warm-up gives "significant gains, particularly when the number of individuals is large"). Spaced review has been tried in (continual) pretraining: SRT (SM-2 scheduling, 2608.17530) and LFR (2409.06131). Gu et al.'s subsampling is also a schedule. What is new is only the bits/param metric and expanding-interval spacing in from-scratch fact pretraining.
2. **Muon vs AdamW measured as capacity.** Kim, Nichani, Wu, Bietti and Lee (Mar 2026) derive storage-capacity scaling for Muon in linear associative memory with power-law frequencies and validate it on synthetic tasks. They find Muon's capacity "significantly exceeds that of SGD", matches Newton's method, and that both converge to the information-theoretic limit at comparable speeds. That is not bioS bits/param in a transformer, but "nobody has reported capacity under Muon" is too strong. It also already predicts the ledger's hypothesis: faster at low exposure, same ceiling.
3. **QAT int4 bits/param.** Kumar et al. trained 30M-220M models with weight-only QAT at 3-12 bits and fitted N_eff. At 4 bits N_eff/N ≈ 0.78 [calc], which predicts about 1.55 bits/param if knowledge capacity scales with N_eff, right at the ledger's 1.5 win line. ParetoQ (NeurIPS 2025) finds ternary, 2-bit and 3-bit QAT on the size-accuracy Pareto front, using benchmark accuracy at 600M and up. The bits/param measurement is new; the prediction is not untested.
4. **Low-rank factorized models at equal params.** CoLA (2502.10940) pretrains LLaMA models of 60M-7B with low-rank auto-encoder layers replacing the full MLP and projection matrices, and reports full-rank-level performance with about 2x fewer parameters. That is loss-level prior evidence (partly contradicting the ledger's expectation that low rank stores less). bits/param is still unmeasured.
5. **Memory-layer bits per memory parameter.** No one reports bits per memory parameter, but memory-parameter efficiency at small scale has been measured in loss and benchmark terms. Pouransari et al. (ICLR 2026, 2510.02375): a 160M model plus an 18M-parameter memory block fetched from a 4.6B bank matches a regular model of more than 2x the parameters. That is thousands of bank parameters per useful dense-equivalent parameter [calc, crude], which fits the report's "FLOP-efficient, not parameter-efficient". Memory+ and UltraMem are already cited.

Checked and not overclaimed: **facts vs in-context skill interference** (no test of this exact sweep found, but see omissions for closely related work), and **soft-label distillation of facts** (no prior on bits/param or exposure savings found; search incomplete).

## Omissions

1. **In-context vs in-weights competition** bears directly on the Planck-critical experiment (ledger 8). Singh et al. 2023 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08360): in-context learning "first emerges, then disappears and gives way to" in-weights learning as training continues, with "initial evidence that ICL transience may be caused by competition between ICL and IWL circuits". L2 regularization helps ICL persist. Follow-ups: Chan et al. ICLR 2025 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23042), Singh et al. 2025 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05631), CoQE TMLR 2026 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13459). All are synthetic few-shot classification, not conversational recall. But they suggest that a heavily overtrained tiny model can lose in-context ability, which is exactly Planck's regime. The O'Neill 2026 result above (fact writes "barely degrade" in-context use, Qwen3) points the other way at larger scale. Experiment 7/8 should add a training-length axis, not only an N axis.
2. **Stochastic rounding for LLM pretraining**, Ozkara et al. AISTATS 2025 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20566): BF16 with stochastic rounding beats (BF16, FP32) mixed precision in validation perplexity up to 6.7B, with 1.54x throughput and 30% less memory. This belongs in the precision ladder (experiment 1) as the expected winner, not only as a gap-closer.
3. **Kumar et al. Finding 3** (fixed-N overtraining favors higher precision) and **App M** (BF16 vs FP32 reference: "no real difference" at 30M-220M on Dolma). The second is direct in-scale real-data evidence for "fp32 master weights are enough, fp64 is pointless", stronger than the 99.75% extrapolation.
4. **Gu et al. Sec 3.3**: the phase transition also appears for a clean reasoning subtask (slope calculation mixed into OpenWebMath, 70M). This partly pre-tests ledger idea 9 (skill threshold frequency) and should be cited there.
5. **Kim et al. 2026 Muon capacity theory** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26554), see novelty 2.
6. **Hierarchical parametric memories for small LMs** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02375, ICLR 2026). This is the most Planck-relevant memory result: a 160M anchor model holding "common knowledge and general reasoning" with long-tail facts in a fetched memory bank, at trillion-token scale. It is a real alternative to PKM for "facts outside the model".
7. **CoLA** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10940) and **ParetoQ** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02631), see novelty 3 and 4.
8. **Capacity Matters** (ACL 2025 L2M2 workshop, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14704): on SNOMED-derived synthetic data, "embedding size is the primary determinant of learning speed and capacity, while additional layers provide limited benefits". A small-transformer memorization result relevant to Planck's width/depth and tokenizer choices (workshop paper, single group).
9. **Hubble** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19811): 1B/8B models with inserted biographies. Memorization depends on frequency relative to corpus size, and data not seen again is forgotten. Large-scale, but it corroborates Gu and Chang.

## What changes for Planck

- The report's precision recommendation (bf16 compute with fp32 master weights, no fp64) survives. Add stochastic-rounding bf16 as an arm expected to match or beat mixed precision. Drop the argument that Kumar's law favors lower precision for Planck: for fixed-N overtraining it favors higher.
- Before running ledger 6 (scheduling), read Zucchet Fig 4 and Appendix E. The uniform/Zipf/warm-up comparison exists. Only the bits/param readout and the spacing arm are new.
- Ledger 8 (facts vs in-context skill) should include the ICL-transience literature and vary training length as well as N.
- The "grammar and word meaning are cheap" point stays a hypothesis. The geometric-memory citation does not support it.
