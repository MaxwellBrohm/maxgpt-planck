# Fact-check: archfp.md (architecture from first principles)

Checked 2026-09-23. Method: 14 primary PDFs pulled from arXiv and converted with pdftotext (2404.05405, 2607.18363, 2312.04927, 1909.11942, 2405.16039, 2604.21106, 2609.09691, 2507.06457, 2410.17980, 2608.16671, 2603.10145, 2406.06248, 2410.02117, 2407.13623), PMC6458406 read directly, and every parameter, KV and FLOP figure in section 3 recomputed with a short arithmetic script. Omission and novelty checks used arXiv abstract pages. Web search quota ran out partway (the session-wide 200-call cap), and the arXiv API returned HTTP 429, so the omission sweep was narrower than planned: absence claims below are "not found", not "does not exist". Nothing was trained or run.

## Summary

Every number in the 15 load-bearing claims matches its source. Six claims need a correction, and the corrections matter for the design:

1. **Looped GPT-BERT is misread (claim 8).** The looped 12.18M model's entity-tracking scores are compared with a non-looped 12-layer model of **29.9M**. The parameter-matched 4x1 control (same 12.18M, no looping) scores just as low: 17.38 and 17.52, against 15.78 and 17.15 for 4x12. So the drop comes from having fewer unique parameters, not from sharing. The evidence against a fully shared stack (L14, section 2.2 pattern, candidate C's prelude/coda rationale) is much weaker than stated.
2. **The head-rank evidence does not test rank = d (claim 12).** The 2.26M experiment factorizes the head *below* the model width (rank 48 in a width-96 model). It was trained for 1,200 steps on 1.23M tokens, ending at a loss of about 6 nats. Its own conclusion is that a harmful gradient bottleneck is *not* established. It cannot support "the strongest argument for d=512".
3. **The hybrid recall numbers are 1.3B-only (claim 9).** The paper says its 340M models "show insignificant recall capability ... We omit recall benchmarks for this reason."
4. **The MQRAR "distracted head" comes from a different model (claim 10).** It was a separate 2-layer, d=100, head-dim-32 model on 16 pairs. Separately, stick-breaking also beats softmax on RULER variable tracking at 1B, so it is not "only shown on a 2-layer toy".
5. **Structured matrices do have per-parameter LM evidence (claim 13).** CoLA reaches the same C4 perplexity with 43M parameters as a 58M dense LLaMA (34.04 vs 34.06, 1.1B tokens).
6. **Gated MLP (claim 2).** The 1.3x is LLaMA vs GPT-2 overall, with gated MLP named as the main cause. It is a capacity ratio, not "1.3x faster".

Both "untested" ledger ideas have close prior art: MASA, CLA and LiSA for L3; GDN hybrids and M2RNN for L7. They should be relabelled *tweak-of-known*.

## Scale misapplications

- **Head rank (bottom line 6, rules 6, L9, candidate C rationale).** A 2.26M model trained on 1.23M tokens with a rank-48 head inside width 96, plus a 2B run with a d=4096 backbone, are presented as showing that rank = d = 384 binds at 20M. Neither measures a rank-equals-width head against a wider one at small scale. The "strongest argument for d=512" is therefore unsupported. L9 is the right test; the prior should be neutral.
- **Hybrid recall curve (section 2.3).** The recall results are listed as "340M and 1.3B", but they exist only at 1.3B/100B tokens; at 340M the authors call recall "insignificant".
- **Looped GPT-BERT (sections 2.2, 3.2 C, L4, L14, open question 6).** A 12M vs 30M comparison (single seed, BabyLM, MNTP+CLM objective) is presented as evidence that sharing hurts entity tracking. The parameter-matched control shows no looping penalty.
- **ALBERT attention sharing for L3 and B.** An encoder at 12L d768, scored by GLUE/SQuAD fine-tuning, is used to argue that Q/K sharing is free in a 20M causal decoder. At E=768 (the tied-decoder analogue) attention sharing already costs 0.7.
- **SAN parity (sections 1.5, 2.1, candidate B).** Parity needs about 105B tokens on SYNTH, about 4,000 tokens per parameter. At 5B tokens the gap is 0.046 nats, and on fineweb-edu it is 0.040. Planck's chat data is neither SYNTH nor that long a run at 20M. The report flags the synthetic data but not the token dependence.
- **Q/K "freeze" (L3 why).** Spectral crystallization of Q/K (measured under Muon and AdamW at 6M-87M) is read as "low-information routing that can be reused across depth". Stable singular-value spectra show neither that the weights are frozen nor that different layers are similar.
- **phi = 0.65 with hyper-connections.** It comes from ~20 runs at the four lowest budgets, compute-optimal, with the anchor untreated. Extrapolating it to overtrained 20M (the report flags the overtraining) should also carry the authors' warning that phi would likely fall if the anchor were treated too.
- **Stick-breaking (bottom line 4, L6).** The attention-distraction mechanism rests on one qualitative example from a d=100 toy; the paper's 1B RULER-VT evidence is omitted.
- Already flagged by the report and fine as written: hyper-connections (>=1B), Gather-and-Aggregate (8B), Meta hybrid (350M), DenseFormer (>=350M), binary token codes (>=1B), Godey 2024 (Pythia up to 410M).

## Novelty overclaims (ledger ideas marked untested)

- **L3 (tie W_q/W_k across layer groups, reinvest in depth).** Cross-layer sharing of attention projections has been done:
  - **MASA** (arXiv 2508.04581, Aug 2025) decomposes Q, K, V and O across layers into shared dictionary atoms, trains from scratch at 100M-700M, cuts attention parameters by 66.7% "while achieving on-par performance", and beats GQA, low-rank and "Repeat-all-over/Sequential sharing at comparable parameter budgets".
  - **Cross-Layer Attention** (arXiv 2405.12981) shares K/V between adjacent layers, from scratch at 1B/3B.
  - **LiSA** (arXiv 2408.01890) shows highly similar attention patterns across layers and compresses Q and K 6x in pretrained 7-13B models.
  - ALBERT's shared-attention arm.
  - Within a layer, tying Q=K "breaks attention directionality", while tying K=V costs 3.1% perplexity at 300M/1.2B (arXiv 2606.04032, ICML 2026).

  The exact Q/K-only tie with depth reinvestment at 5-20M was not found, so relabel it *tweak-of-known*. Add MASA and a K=V arm as the controls.
- **L7 (one Gated DeltaNet layer among attention layers as a "current value" register).** Adding delta-rule layers to attention stacks is well tested:
  - The hybrid-ratio study above includes GDN hybrids from 24:1 to 3:1, and the 3:1 GDN hybrid beats the Transformer on RULER at 1.3B.
  - **M2RNN** (arXiv 2603.14360, Mar 2026) reports that "replacing even a single recurrent layer" in a hybrid gives accuracy gains, and targets entity tracking as a task transformers provably cannot do in general (7B MoE).
  - **Gated DeltaNet-2** (arXiv 2605.22791) exists specifically to make the erase and write steps of an overwrite separately controllable (1.3B/100B).

  The rationale "attention has no native overwrite" is also contested within attention itself: Selective Attention (2410.02703, in `arch.md`), stick-breaking, the Forgetting Transformer and PaTH (2505.16381, NeurIPS 2025, data-dependent Householder position encoding). A minority-linear hybrid (1 GDN : 3 attention) tested at 5-20M on correction probes was not found. Relabel it *tweak-of-known*, and use GDN-2's decoupled erase/write gate as the variant to test.

## Omissions

- **CoLA** (arXiv 2502.10940): the per-parameter structured/low-rank LM counterexample at 60M-1B (numbers in claim 13). It belongs in section 2.5 and arguably deserves a ledger entry: CoLA-style low-rank auto-encoder MLPs at 10-20M, measured on total parameters.
- **MASA** (arXiv 2508.04581): the closest in-scale test of cross-layer attention sharing, and the natural baseline for L3.
- **Cross-Layer Attention** (arXiv 2405.12981): relevant to candidate B's 57 MiB KV cache if memory ever matters, and to L3.
- **QKV-variants study** (arXiv 2606.04032, ICML 2026): K=V tying is a cheap parameter saver in the attention-heavy B; Q=K is not.
- **M2RNN** (arXiv 2603.14360) and **Gated DeltaNet-2** (arXiv 2605.22791): the current state of the art for the L7 mechanism.
- **Rethinking efficient attention in hybrids** (arXiv 2606.15378, Jun 2026): long-range retrieval "is mainly carried by full attention", while efficient layers shape the optimization trajectory. That predicts L7's GDN layer would not itself do the retrieval.
- **PaTH attention** (arXiv 2505.16381): a within-softmax, data-dependent alternative to L6/L7. The abstract says it improves on RoPE on synthetic benchmarks and in LM; which synthetic tasks were used was not checked.
- Not re-listed because the first run covers them: Selective Attention and the Forgetting Transformer (`arch.md`). Their absence from archfp's L6/L7 rationale is noted above.
