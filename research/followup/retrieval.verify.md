## Scale misapplications

1. **"Small models are more context-faithful" (bottom line 2, sec 3 synthesis).** Longpre's size trend comes from fine-tuned T5 QA readers trained on noisy single DPR passages, and it is concentrated on answer-overlap questions. The smallest point is 60M, pretrained. It says nothing direct about a from-scratch 10-60M decoder. With oracle-lookup training, override is near zero at every size (Longpre's own gold-passage result), so the size effect may not matter for Planck at all.
2. **"A 90M model uses retrieved knowledge as well as 3B" (bottom line 1, ledger 15 "why").** That is a pretrained 90M seq2seq BlenderBot (Reddit-pretrained, then fine-tuned), measured by overlap with human-chosen knowledge. Extrapolating to "a trained skill small models learn well" at 20M is untested. The report's own open question 1 says so. The bottom line should say so too.
3. **KLLM BM25 gains (NQ +9.2, TQA +10.6)** are SmolLM-1.7B at 10B tokens. The report listed "size not stated", and ledger 3's rationale leans on the grounded gains. At 135M-360M only per-benchmark fine-tuned task results exist.
4. **Answerability "in reach at its size"** was inferred from 149M-355M encoders at 86-90%. At 110-125M the paper shows 64-76%, and a fine-tuned 1B decoder scores 56.8%. Nothing measures an 18.5M decoder probe.
5. **"Verbatim beats extraction by 16-22 points"** (sec 7, ledger 12) is GPT-4o answering over GPT-4o-mini extractions. The claim is sound for design direction, but not evidence about what a 20M model can write or read.
6. **DST at 60M ≈ 220M** (ledger 11) is pretrained T5 with per-slot independent decoding. The ledger's notes block is sequential single-pass writing at 20M, which the same paper shows is more than 5 points worse, and the 60% target exceeds what 60M T5 reached (56%).
7. **FunctionGemma multi-turn 10-39% to 90-97%** (ledger 16) is per-turn accuracy for a 270M model pretrained on about 6T tokens and already tool-tuned. "Disappears with a few thousand traces at 60M" is extrapolation. The Sep 2026 FunctionGemma paper is single-turn and cannot back it.
8. **Retro-li / RETRO** (ledger 14) is 109M-and-up perplexity only. "Never pays at 20-60M" is an inference, correct in direction given how small the retrieval-only effect is (0.14 perplexity).

## Novelty overclaims (ledger ideas marked untested)

1. **Fictional-entity grounded dialogues (ledger 4).** The mechanism has been tried. Prereq-Tune (Liu et al., arXiv 2410.19290, ICLR 2025) trains on **fictitious synthetic data** explicitly to "disentangle the learning of skills and knowledge", at LLM scale (size not in abstract). Longpre 2021 (entity substitution training) and KLLM 2026 (entity placeholders in pretraining) are the counterfactual and anonymized versions, both already in the report. Yu, Merullo and Pavlick (arXiv 2310.15910) show on Pythia and GPT-2 that how frequently the entity appears in training decides whether a counterfactual context wins, which is the mechanism fictional entities exploit. Still untested: the specific real vs fictional vs 50/50 grounded-*dialogue* A/B on a tiny chat model.
2. **Answerability head / linear probe gate (ledger 10).** This has been tried at 7B-13B. Slobodkin et al. (arXiv 2310.11877) find query answerability linearly encoded in hidden states, "the representation of the first decoded token often being a strong indicator". Lookback Lens (arXiv 2407.07071) uses a **linear classifier** on attention-ratio features for **classifier-guided decoding**, cutting hallucination 9.6% on XSum (7B, transfers to 13B). Sufficient Context (arXiv 2411.06037, ICLR 2025) gates abstention on a context-sufficiency signal, +2-10% correct-when-answering for Gemini, GPT and Gemma. Probing-RAG (arXiv 2410.13339) uses a hidden-state prober for retrieval decisions. Untested only at 18.5M.
3. **Structured memory-write turn (ledger 11).** This has been tried at 117M in task-oriented dialogue. SimpleTOD (arXiv 2005.00796, GPT-2 117M, whose 55.72 JGA the report itself cites) writes the belief state as text in the same causal LM before the reply. SOLOIST does the same. Keep Me Updated! (Bae et al., arXiv 2210.08750) does memory management in multi-session open-domain chat, with operations that replace or delete outdated user facts. MemReader-0.6B is a trained small extractor with update handling. Untested: an open-domain chat model at 20M writing and overwriting notes inside the reply turn.
4. **Headline: size sweep with vs without lookup (ledger 15).** The general form has been tried. MassiveDS (arXiv 2407.12854) plots compute-optimal curves with and without a datastore and finds "a smaller model augmented with a large datastore outperforms a larger LM-only model on knowledge-intensive tasks". Shuster 2021 Table 11 is a with-retrieval size sweep on dialogue. Untested: a multi-turn chat pass threshold under lookup, and at 20M-150M. The novel part is the chat pass-size shift, not "retrieval substitutes for parameters".

## Omissions

- **Sufficient Context** (arXiv 2411.06037, ICLR 2025): larger models answer wrongly instead of abstaining when context is insufficient, while smaller models (Gemma 2, Mistral) "hallucinate or abstain often, even with sufficient context". This is directly the two-sided LK4 / false-abstain problem, with a ready autorater method **[>=2B]**.
- **Context-Parametric Inversion** (arXiv 2410.10796): during instruction tuning, context reliance under knowledge conflict first rises, then falls, while benchmarks keep improving. Seen across TULU, Alpaca and UltraChat, and across Llama, Mistral and Pythia. For Planck: generic chat SFT (UltraChat is in Max's mix) can erode reading, so LK5 should be tracked through SFT, not only at the end.
- **Characterizing Mechanisms for Factual Recall** (arXiv 2310.15910, Pythia and GPT-2) and **Context versus Prior Knowledge** (arXiv 2404.04633): entity frequency and familiarity predict whether context overrides memory. This is small-model evidence (Pythia includes 70M-410M) that the report's sec 3 lacks. It supports fictional or anonymized entities.
- **RAFT** (arXiv 2403.10131) and **Making RALMs Robust to Irrelevant Context** (arXiv 2310.01558): training recipes that mix oracle and distractor documents, including examples with no oracle document. These map onto LK3/LK4 training **[>=7B]**. RAFT uses chain-of-thought answers, which Planck excludes.
- **ChatQA / ChatRAG Bench** (arXiv 2401.10225): conversational RAG instruction tuning that includes unanswerable samples. ChatRAG Bench is a ready multi-turn RAG eval with unanswerable cases **[>=7B]**.
- **Context-DPO** (arXiv 2412.15280) and **CANOE** (arXiv 2505.16483): synthetic, easily verifiable QA plus preference or rule-based RL for context faithfulness. The verifiable-synthetic-data idea matches Planck's teacher pipeline **[LLM scale]**.
- **Lookback Lens** (arXiv 2407.07071) as an eval tool: the lookback ratio is computable on Planck for free and gives a mechanism-level grounding signal alongside LK scores.
- Lead not verified (search budget exhausted): Liquid AI's LFM2 "Nanos" reportedly include a RAG-specialized ~1.2B model for multi-turn grounded answering. Check the Liquid model cards before citing.

## Net effect on the report's recommendations

- Mostly stands: BM25 zero-parameter lookup, a small lookup budget, full-context queries, plain-text abstention, the two-sided metric, verbatim-first memory, and always-on retrieval before a tool route.
- Needs changing:
  - Add near-miss negatives (same entity, missing attribute) to LK4 and the training mix (Pleias failure).
  - Track LK5 and LK composite through SFT (context-parametric inversion).
  - Fix Longpre's metric (EM, not F1) and note the NQ-Train EM cost.
  - Relabel ledger 4, 10, 11 and 15 as "tried at larger scale, untested at 20M" with the citations above.
  - Base ledger 11 on the sequential-decoding number (48.9 at 60M on MultiWOZ 2.2), not the independent-decoding 56.
  - State the FLOP term as a lower bound (full-window decode is about twice the stated figure).
