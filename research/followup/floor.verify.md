# Fact-check: followup/floor.md (skill floor for multi-turn chat)

Checked 2026-09-23 against primary sources. arXiv PDFs were downloaded and converted with pdftotext (Zoology 2312.04927, Sanford et al. 2408.14332, MTP 2404.19737, TinyStories 2305.07759, SimpleTOD 2005.00796, Wu et al. 2505.20896, PI-LLM 2506.08184, Drozdz and Heilbron 2608.18083, LFM2 report 2511.23404, Boesch and Wee 2609.16183, SleepGate 2603.14517, Dual-Process Interference 2603.00270, TinyDialogues 2408.03617, bAbI 1502.05698, bAbI dialog 1605.07683). The Olsson et al. HTML was fetched and searched. Hugging Face cards and configs were fetched directly (LFM2.5-230M, LFM2.5-350M, TinyChat, TinyChat-1776K). Arithmetic was redone by hand.

Search limits, stated plainly: this session's WebSearch budget was already spent (200 of 200) when this check started, and the arXiv API and Semantic Scholar both returned HTTP 429 part way through. Omission searches therefore used direct arXiv abstract and PDF fetches, Hugging Face API calls, and the sibling follow-up files. Anything below marked "not found" means not found under those limits, not "does not exist".

No model was loaded or run. Nothing outside `research/followup/` was modified.

## Scale and task misapplications

1. **PI-LLM's size effect used as a 10-150M design fact (bottom line 4, sec 6, summary table).** The evidence is 0.6B-637B, with size as four coarse bins (XS = 0-15B), and 3-400 updates per key. A later, larger study with the same paradigm finds no size effect for PI (arXiv 2603.00270). Nothing here speaks to one or two corrections in a dialogue, or to models below 0.6B.
2. **Wu et al. (37.8M) used as evidence that "the stale-first shortcut is a learning stage".** The task has no reassignments. The line-1 heuristic is positional and is retained, not replaced. This is a task transfer, not a scale transfer, but it carries ledger idea 3's "why".
3. **MTP induction result generalized from a stories-only corpus to "plain next-token training".** The same paper's Appendix J shows next-token models forming induction (success >= 0.85) at 1M-1B non-embedding once books are mixed in. The "real floor near Planck's small end" is a data effect.
4. **SimpleTOD DST at 82M used as "accepting corrections learnable at 82M".** MultiWOZ DST is mostly slot accumulation with gold belief-state supervision. The share of turns that overwrite a slot is not reported, and JGA 54.5 means 45% of turns are wrong. Treat it as evidence for cumulative state tracking, not for corrections.
5. **LFM2.5-230M Multi-IF 37.7 used as "persistence across 3 turns shown at 230M".** It is an average accuracy over 3 turns, from a vendor harness, after ~19T training tokens (census.verify). A 37.7% average is not a demonstration of the skill, and 19T tokens is about 290x the compute Planck can spend (census.verify claim 10).
6. **Sanford et al.'s one-layer bound treated as a practical depth floor.** The bound is Omega(n) in heads x width x precision. At n = 2,048 and ordinary widths it does not bind. Olsson's smeared-key models give one-layer induction.
7. **Entity tracking "human level at 410M" in the summary table.** This is correctly labelled likelihood-only. But the 70M figure (53.5%) is at or near chance, and every Pythia point is 300B Pile tokens. So it measures Pile-era, likelihood-read tracking, not what a chat-trained 30-150M model can do.

## Novelty overclaims (ledger ideas marked untested)

1. **"Latest value wins" correction data with stale traps (idea 3).** The core idea, training small models from scratch on data where the latest value must beat an earlier one, has been tried repeatedly.
   - bAbI QA task 1 (Weston et al. 2015) is built on it: "Mary went to the bathroom ... Mary travelled to the office. Where is Mary? A: office" ([arXiv 1502.05698](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05698), Table 1).
   - bAbI dialog **Task 2, "Updating API calls"** (Bordes, Boureau, Weston 2016) is a correction dialogue set: "users then ask to update their requests between 1 and 4 times". Memory Networks trained from scratch reach 100% per-response and 100% per-dialog, while supervised embeddings reach 68.4% per-response and 0% per-dialog ([arXiv 1605.07683](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.07683), Table 2).
   - SleepGate (Mar 2026) trains a 4-layer, 793K-parameter transformer on PI-LLM-style latest-value streams ([arXiv 2603.14517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14517)). Its full-KV baseline scores 8.0% even at one update, which suggests an undertrained baseline, so it is weak evidence either way.

   What remains untested, as far as this check could find: a few percent of natural-language correction dialogues mixed into general LM pretraining at 30M, measured by stale-vs-corrected log-prob margins. The ledger should say "partially tested (bAbI, bAbI dialog T2, SleepGate); untested as a pretraining-mix intervention in a chat LM".
2. **"TinyChat" restricted-vocabulary, knowledge-free conversation corpus (idea 6).** Both the name and the core idea already exist, and the sibling `followup/chatdata.md` already lists them.
   - **TinyChat-1776K** (June 2024): 1,776,960 total parameters, Llama architecture, 3 layers, d=192, vocab 2,048, tied embeddings, context 256. Trained on "3 year old children level English conversations" with an A/B multi-turn template; card: "It has no world knowledge" ([card](https://huggingface.co/raincandy-u/TinyChat-1776K), [config](https://huggingface.co/raincandy-u/TinyChat-1776K/raw/main/config.json)).
   - **TinyChat** dataset (starhopp3r, Aug 2024): 1,000,000 GPT-4o-mini conversations in BASIC English, explicitly "inspired by the TinyStories dataset" ([card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/starhopp3r/TinyChat)). TinyChat15M is a 15M conversational model trained on it (Aug 2024, [post](https://nikhilr.io/posts/TinyChat15M/)).
   - **TinyDialogues** (EMNLP 2024): ~29M words of GPT-4 child-directed multi-turn dialogues, "Inspired by TinyStories", used to pretrain GPT-2 124M ([arXiv 2408.03617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03617)).

   None of these plants facts, adds corrections, or measures cross-turn skills. That part is new, and the win condition (any sub-skill above 80% at <=10M) is still unclaimed. But "TinyStories ... no one has done the same for dialogue" is false, and the idea needs a different name.
3. **Perspective-binding data (idea 4).** Not an overclaim as far as I could check: I found no data-only intervention against role capture at small scale. Related work that tackles speaker identity through architecture should be cited, though. TransferTransfo (117M) added dialog-state (speaker) embeddings ([arXiv 1901.08149](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.08149)). Speaker-Aware BERT (110M class) adds speaker embeddings for multi-turn response selection ([arXiv 2004.03588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03588)). Instructional Segment Embedding adds role-type embeddings at LLM scale ([arXiv 2410.09102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09102)). Unverifiable beyond this, given the search limits.

## Omissions

1. **MTP paper Appendix J (Figure S14).** With a 9:1 books plus stories mix, next-token models form induction (success >= 0.85) at every size from 1M to 1B non-embedding, and the MTP advantage survives only at the two smallest sizes. This directly answers open question 1 and weakens bottom line 6 and section 3 ([arXiv 2404.19737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)).
2. **"LLMs Remember First, Forget Last" (arXiv 2603.00270, Feb 2026).** 39 LLMs from 1B to 2.5T on the same 46-pair update paradigm: size predicts retroactive interference resistance (R^2 0.49) but not proactive interference (R^2 0.06, p = 0.14). Every model shows PI > RI (d = 1.73), and PI failures are "active primacy intrusion". This is a primacy bias, which fits the probe hint that small models prefer the stale value. It also removes the "size fixes it" reading.
3. **Smeared keys, token shift and short causal conv as the cheap route to one-layer induction** (Olsson et al., Argument 2). Boesch and Wee's finding that a short conv adds ~0.5 recall accuracy supports the same point. Both matter for Planck's depth budget at 10M and for the arch lane's conv-hybrid choice.
4. **bAbI and bAbI dialog Task 2** (see overclaim 1): the oldest direct evidence that "latest value wins" is learnable from scratch by small models on templated data.
5. **Existing tiny chat corpora and models** (TinyChat-1776K at 1.78M, TinyChat15M, TinyDialogues): floor.md's section 1 and the sub-100M table skip them. They are the closest existing points to Planck's 1-15M end. Their register is chat, with no measured cross-turn skill.
6. **Counter-evidence to "data gap, not capacity gap" (bottom line 3).** ufakzeka-1 (151M, Turkish, 13.5B tokens) reported that multi-turn identity tracking "did not move across any data change we tried" (data.verify omission 3, cited in `followup/chatdata.md`). One report, LLM-judged, but it belongs next to the thesis.
7. **Data-distribution drivers of in-context learning.** Chan et al. (2022): in-context learning emerges when training data is "bursty" and has many rare classes ([arXiv 2205.05055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05055)). Singh et al. (2023): in-context learning can be transient, giving way to in-weights learning as training continues ([arXiv 2311.08360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08360)). Both bear on idea 1 (recall-dense, distance-varied data) and on planted-fact design: planted values should be nonce or high-entropy so the model cannot answer from weights.
8. **MTP at small sizes is not untested territory for idea 2.** "Pre-Training Curriculum for Multi-Token Prediction" (May 2025) states "prior work has shown that smaller language models (SLMs) struggle with the MTP objective" and finds that a forward NTP-to-MTP curriculum helps them ([arXiv 2505.22757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22757); model sizes not re-checked here).
9. **Quantization amplifies proactive interference.** INT4 via bitsandbytes cuts high-interference accuracy (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct 81.0% to 68.3%), and INT8 carries a smaller penalty in 2 of 3 models ([arXiv 2608.18578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18578); 3.8B-7B only). This is relevant if Planck is deployed quantized: measure corrections on the deployed precision.
10. **Multi-IF scoring and the two-table mix-up** (claim 9): the harness score is a 3-turn average, and the comparison rows come from two different Liquid tables.
11. **Sub-230M multi-turn numbers that do exist:** 2-turn MT-Bench for Falcon-H1-Tiny-90M (4.33), SmolLM2-135M (2.68) and MobileLLM-125M (2.33); tau2-Bench for LFM2.5-230M (census.verify). The report's "nothing below 230M" needs "on Multi-IF".

## What floor.md should change

- Bottom line 4 and section 6: drop "resistance to proactive interference scales with parameters" or mark it contested (PI-LLM R^2 0.26 on 30 models vs 2603.00270 R^2 0.06 on 39 models, both >= 0.6B), and add that both studies use 3+ updates per key.
- Bottom line 6, section 3, the "What this means" point 2 and open question 1: cite Appendix J. The MTP induction floor is corpus-specific (children's stories with out-of-vocabulary name tokens). With books in the mix, next-token models form induction from 1M non-embedding. The 10M recall risk is real but is not established by this paper.
- Section 3 and the summary: "2 layers" should read "2 vanilla attention layers, or 1 with a previous-token path (smeared key, short conv)".
- Section 6 and idea 3's "why": replace the Wu et al. reading with bAbI dialog Task 2 (correction dialogues, memory networks at 100%), and keep Wu et al. only as evidence that multi-hop binding with distractors is learnable at 38M.
- Section 5 and the summary table: state that LFM2.5-230M's 37.7 is a 3-turn average in Liquid's harness. Use 32.92 for LFM2-350M if comparing within the LFM2.5 card, or keep 32.85 and 45.13 as the LFM2 report's table, not both.
- Idea 6: rename it (TinyChat is taken twice), cite TinyChat-1776K, TinyChat15M and TinyDialogues, and restate the novelty as "planted facts, corrections and a cross-turn probe battery at 1-10M".
- Idea 3: mark it partially tested (bAbI QA1, bAbI dialog T2, SleepGate).
